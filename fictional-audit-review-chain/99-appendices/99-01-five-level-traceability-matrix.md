# 99-01 — Five-Level Traceability Matrix

**How to trace any one matter from the workpaper that created it to the regulatory finding that resolved it.**

Each level of review uses its own numbering. This appendix maps them onto each other. The `ISS-nn` column is a
device of this repository; the other reference systems belong to the fictional firm and regulator.

> **Eight of the ten issues were correctly diagnosed at Level 1 or Level 2, before the audit report was signed,
> by the people who did the work.**
>
> `ISS-05` is the purest case. The preparer of `C-500`, in her first week on the client, wrote down that no
> specialist had looked at the £11.5m Level 3 fair value because it was outside the agreed scope. She recorded it
> in a procedure table on 5 March 20X5. The manager did not read the workpaper. The partner did not read the
> workpaper. The EQR did not read the workpaper. **The correct diagnosis sat on the file, unread, for four
> months, until an inspection team read it and made it an improvement point, and for a further eight months
> until a regulator read it and made it a finding.**

## 9. Three tests you can run on this matrix

**Test 1 — did any level add information the level below did not have?**

| Level | New information generated |
|---|---|
| 2 | Yes — 24 notes producing evidence; the inventory back-test; the specialist's range flagged as contradicted |
| **3** | **One item: verification that the Ethics Partner memorandum existed** |
| 4 | Yes — the projection; the hidden covenant column; the Technical Panel interview; the audit trail analysis |
| 5 | Yes — the version history; the aggregate; the incentive analysis; the Audit Committee Chair's evidence |

**Level 3 is the only level that added essentially nothing.** It is also the only level with a statutory
responsibility for the overall quality of the engagement.

**Test 2 — how many issues required more resources to find?**

Zero. Every issue was findable from documents on the file. `ISS-02` required a division. `ISS-10` required
opening a log. The most expensive procedure any level would have needed was a twenty-minute telephone call to a
specialist who had offered to make it.

**Test 3 — how many issues did the reviews find that the preparers had not?**

One: the covenant breach in the hidden column, found at Level 4. Everything else was identified by the person who
did the work, and either accepted, not read, or not escalated.
