# 99-02 — Escalation Case Study: Contract CN-4471

**One contract. One estimate. Five reviews. A delay claim nobody connected to it.**

This appendix follows a single matter — `ISS-01` — from the spreadsheet that created it to the regulatory
finding that resolved it, in narrative order. It is the shortest route to understanding what this repository is
about.

**Fictional throughout.** See the disclaimer in the top-level `README.md`.

---

## The contract

**CN-4471, Northline Rolling Stock Retrofit.** Auburn Ridge Group's largest open contract: £96.4m to strip and
rebuild 84 rail vehicles for the Meridian Rail Authority, running from 20X3 to 20X6 out of a depot ninety
minutes from the auditor's office.

Revenue is recognised over time on an input method: costs incurred to date over total estimated costs. Which
means that the single most important number on the contract is not a number at all. **It is an estimate of what
it will cost to finish**, made by the people building it, and changed whenever they change their minds.

---

## Act I — The estimate changes

### 30 September 20X4

At the Q3 review the position is unremarkable.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Contract price | £96.4m |
| Costs incurred to date | £38.9m |
| **Estimated total costs** | **£78.9m** |
| Percentage complete | 49.3% |
| Forecast margin | **18.2%** |

### 16 December 20X4

The Contract Review Board meets. Its minute records:

> *"CN-4471: forecast cost to complete revised to £74.8m (previously £78.9m) reflecting value engineering on
> bogie assembly and revised certification scope. Programme director to confirm savings are locked in with the
> supply chain before the year-end close. Noted."*

**A £4.1m reduction.** Three components: £2.6m from redesigning the bogie mounting assembly, £1.1m because the
MRA has accepted a revised test protocol reducing certification runs from six to four, and £0.4m from cutting
programme contingency from 4% to 3.5%.

### 31 December 20X4 — what the £4.1m does

Because progress is measured by costs incurred over total estimated costs, cutting the denominator moves the
percentage complete without anybody doing any more work.

| | At £78.9m | At £74.8m | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Percentage complete | 66.0% | **69.7%** | +3.7pp |
| Cumulative revenue | £63.7m | **£67.2m** | **+£3.5m** |
| Cumulative margin | £11.6m | **£15.1m** | **+£3.5m** |
| Forecast margin | 18.2% | **22.4%** | +4.2pp |

**£3.5m of revenue and £3.5m of profit, from a change of estimate.**

Group materiality is £3.10m. **The single change is 113% of materiality.**

The portfolio average forecast margin is 8.4%. CN-4471 is now 22.4% — the highest by 7.8 percentage points.
Excluding this contract, the portfolio margin is **7.6% against 7.9% last year**. The entire Equipment segment
margin improvement that the Group will report to the market is one contract.

### 31 December 20X4, 23:47

The Chief Financial Officer posts journal **JE-12047**: £0.90m, round sum, debiting the CN-4471 contract asset
and crediting Equipment & Systems revenue. No approval — she has an account that permits create-and-post. The
support is one line on a printed contract summary sheet, initialled.

It will meet **six of the fourteen** criteria the audit team later runs over 42,118 manual journals. No other
item in the population meets more than four.

## Act II — Level 1: the audit

### 8 January 20X5

The senior in charge, Dev Ramanathan, emails the Project Director. She replies in three sentences:

> *"Dev — confirming the position we discussed. We have taken £2.6m out of the bogie workstream through the
> redesign, £1.1m off certification now that MRA have agreed the shorter protocol, and we have trimmed
> contingency to 3.5%. Net £4.1m. Happy to talk it through. Fiona."*

### 21 January 20X5

`CN-4471 ETC v7.xlsx` arrives.

### 23 January 20X5

A 25-minute telephone call. He asks whether the savings are contractually secured with the supply chain. She
says the redesign has been *"agreed in principle with the two main subcontractors and we are working through
the commercial paperwork"*.

**That is the evidence. All of it.** By the time `C-100` is signed, the file contains management's own
spreadsheet, a three-sentence email, a note of a phone call, a board minute, and a written representation.

**What it does not contain:** anything from the Group's quantity surveyors; any supplier quotation or purchase
order; any MRA correspondence on the protocol change; any site visit; and the retrospective review of prior
estimates that ISA 540 requires.

### The look-back that was scoped and not performed

`A-700/2` procedure 7.4 requires a comparison of prior year estimates to outturns on the five largest
contracts. `C-100` §7 records:

> **Performed? No.** *Reason recorded: time was reallocated to the business combination following receipt of the
> Kestrel completion accounts on 6 February 20X5.*

Instead the team relies on a prior year portfolio-level comparison: aggregate forecast margin of 6.4% at
31 December 20X3 against a 6.4% outturn on contracts completed in FY20X4.

**Two aggregates matching tells you nothing about one contract.** Offsetting errors of any size are consistent
with it.

### The fact the file already held

`B-100` §6.1 — a workpaper the team prepared in November, four months earlier:

> *"Recommendation 2. The Group does not perform a formal retrospective comparison of forecast contract costs to
> actual outturn on completed contracts. Without such a comparison, management and the Contract Review Board
> have no objective measure of the historical accuracy of the forecasting process. **Management response:
> Agreed. Target date 31 December 20X4. Owner: Group Financial Controller.**"*

That is ARG's own internal audit function, in April 20X4. Status at the year end per the action tracker:
**overdue, not implemented.**

**The audit team obtained that report, filed it, summarised it, and then relied on management's estimation
process without the analysis management's own internal auditors had said was necessary to evaluate it.**

### 4 February 20X5 — the claim

Five weeks after the year end and five weeks before the audit report, the Meridian Rail Authority serves notice
claiming **£6.8m of liquidated damages** on CN-4471.

The basis: failure to achieve delivery gates 3 and 4 by the contractual dates. **41 weeks late on gate 3.
39 weeks late on gate 4.**

ARG's defence, per its notice of dispute of 18 February: the delays are attributable to **the Authority's own
variation of the certification protocol** and to late release of vehicles.

> ### Stop here and read those two facts together
>
> **In Q4 20X4 management reduced the cost of finishing this contract by £4.1m, and one of the three stated
> reasons was a narrowed certification scope arising from the MRA's protocol change.**
>
> **In Q4 20X4 the contract fell 40 weeks behind on two delivery gates, and ARG's defence to the resulting claim
> is the MRA's protocol change.**
>
> The same event is offered as the reason the contract got cheaper and the reason it got later. A retrofit
> programme running 40 weeks late — fixed depot, fixed workforce, standing overhead — does not normally get
> cheaper.
>
> Both propositions can be true. **The question is whether anybody asked.**

### What `C-950` did

The subsequent events workpaper handles the claim competently. Legal letters from four jurisdictions. The claim
correctly assessed as not probable (so no provision) and not remote (so disclosed). `C-950` §5.4 works through
four questions: does the claim affect the transaction price, does it make the contract loss-making, does it
impair the contract asset, does it affect going concern. All four answered.

There is a fifth row on that schedule:

| Question | Conclusion recorded |
|---|---|
| **"Does the claim indicate that the estimate of costs to complete may be understated?"** | **"Not addressed"** |

**The workpaper identified exactly the right question and left the answer blank.** Nobody who reviewed it
noticed the row was empty.

### 21 February 20X5 — `C-100` is signed

> *"Conclusion: the estimate of costs to complete on CN-4471 is within a reasonable range. The change in
> estimate is appropriately accounted for prospectively in the period in which it arose."*

## Act III — Level 2: the manager

### 28 February 20X5

Sasha Lindqvist raises `MRN-014`. It is the best review note in the file:

> *"The CN-4471 estimate reduction of £4.1m increases PBT by £3.5m, which is above materiality. The evidence is
> management's own schedule, a three-sentence email and a phone note. I want **(a)** a look-back on the accuracy
> of prior estimates on this contract and on the portfolio, and **(b)** something from outside the finance and
> programme function — supplier quotes, a QS view, the MRA correspondence on the test protocol."*

She asks for the two things the file lacks.

### 2 March 20X5 — she gets neither, and clears the note

The response: the look-back was scoped and not performed, time went to Kestrel, rely on the prior year
portfolio comparison. No supplier documentation available; the redesign is "agreed in principle".

**She clears it.**

The senior queries the portfolio-comparison point with her on 25 February. She tells him it covers it. He
records the exchange on the workpaper.

She also raises `MRN-015` — show me the portfolio margin excluding CN-4471 — and gets **7.6% against 7.9%**
within a day. That answer proves the entire segment improvement is one contract. It goes into the file as
reassurance.

And `MRN-043`, on the delay claim: *does it make the contract loss-making?* Answer: no, 22.4% margin, £6.8m
claim, still profitable. **Correct, and not the question.**

### 11 March 20X5, 21:15 — she writes it down

In `M-100` §6.1, in a document the firm's methodology does not require her to write, she lists six of her own
clearances as inadequate. On this one:

> *"I asked for a look-back analysis. I did not get one. I cleared the note anyway.*
>
> *My reasoning was that the prior year file contained a portfolio-level comparison... **It does not.** A
> portfolio aggregate of 6.4% against 6.4% can conceal offsetting errors of any size. The whole question with
> CN-4471 is whether *this* contract's estimate is reliable, and a portfolio average says nothing about that.*
>
> *I also knew, because I read `B-100`, that ARG's own internal audit function had recommended exactly this
> analysis in April 20X4, that management had agreed to do it by 31 December 20X4, and that it was overdue. That
> is a fact pointing directly at the reliability of the estimation process, **and I had it in front of me.**"*

And in `M-300` §5.1, in a memorandum addressed to the engagement partner:

> *"The Meridian Rail Authority says we are 41 weeks late on gate 3 and 39 weeks late on gate 4, and wants
> £6.8m. In the same quarter, management reduced its estimate of what it will cost to finish the same contract
> by £4.1m, and one of the three stated reasons was a reduction in certification scope arising from the very
> protocol change that we are relying on as our defence to the delay claim.*
>
> *I do not know what the answer is... **But we have never asked the question**, and a 40-week delay on a
> retrofit programme with a fixed depot, a fixed workforce and standing overhead does not usually make a
> contract cheaper.*
>
> *`MRN-043` asked whether the claim made the contract loss-making. It does not. **That is not the same
> question.**"*

**Level 2 gets it right. In writing. The night before the opinion is signed.**

## Act IV — Level 3: the partner

### 10 March 20X5

Ronan Belliveau opens `C-100` and spends **2.5 hours** on it — more than on any other workpaper in the file. He
raises three notes.

`PRN-06`:

> *"The £4.1m reduction on CN-4471 gives us £3.5m of profit, which is above materiality. What corroboration do
> we have beyond management's schedule and the project director?"*

**It is materially the same question as `MRN-014`, asked ten days later.** It receives materially the same
answer. `MRN-014` had already been cleared on that answer on 2 March.

**He clears `PRN-06` the same day.**

`PRN-07` asks whether the 22.4% margin against a portfolio average of 8.4% is explicable. The answer — that the
portfolio is 7.6% without this contract — is offered as reassurance and accepted as reassurance.

`PRN-08` asks what the alternative procedures gave us, the MRA confirmation never having been returned. The
answer, from the workpaper's own text: *the alternative procedures do not corroborate the percentage complete.*
**He clears it with no further comment.** The response states in terms that the audit did not corroborate the
one thing the significant risk is about.

### 11 March 20X5

He opens `C-950` for **0.4 hours** and raises `PRN-16`: confirm no provision is required and the disclosure is
in. It is. He clears it.

**He has now asked about the same contract in two workpapers on consecutive days.** In one he asked what
corroborated the cost estimate. In the other he asked whether a 40-week delay claim required a provision.
**He did not put them together, and neither did the note the manager raised, nor the EQR, nor — four months
later — the inspection team.**

### 11 March 20X5, 22:04

`M-300` arrives at 21:15. He opens it at 22:04 and closes it at 22:08.

**Four minutes.** He does not read §5.1.

He has signed a different listed audit report that afternoon.

### 11 March 20X5, 19:52 — he authorises

> *"I am satisfied that sufficient appropriate audit evidence has been obtained... I authorise the signing of the
> auditor's report."*

### 12 March 20X5, 11:20 — signed

The auditor's report reports contract revenue as a key audit matter and states:

> *"Across the contracts we tested, the aggregate estimate of total contract costs reduced by £2.3m during the
> fourth quarter. **The whole of that net reduction is attributable to a single contract**, on which management
> reduced the estimated cost to complete by £4.1m, increasing revenue and profit before tax by £3.5m... We
> consider the estimate of costs to complete to be within a reasonable range."*

**The firm told the market what it had done. It just did not have the evidence for the last sentence.**

### 14 March 20X5

The engagement quality reviewer signs off `C-100` — **two days after the report was dated.** Her comment:

> *"There is no third-party or engineering corroboration and no look-back on the accuracy of prior estimates... I
> would have wanted to see the look-back analysis. Having discussed it, I accept that this is a matter of degree
> rather than a conclusion that is unsupportable... I have recorded it as a matter for the FY20X5 audit."*

**Three reviewers — the manager, the partner and the EQR — independently identified that the look-back was
missing. All three concluded anyway.**

## Act V — The interlude nobody planned

### 28 April 20X5

ARG's Q1 contract review increases the forecast cost to complete on CN-4471 by **£3.2m.**

Seventy-eight per cent of the Q4 reduction, reversed in four months.

### 12 May 20X5 — the file is locked

Day 61 after the report date. The limit is 60. Nobody notices.

### 19 May 20X5 — a document appears

Dev Ramanathan performs the look-back analysis. It was matter 1 on the list of things to do next year at
`D-200` §14, and he has the data. He writes two pages, concludes *"no systematic bias is evident"*, and files it
as **`C-100/13`**.

He also amends `C-100` §7 — the paragraph recording that the retrospective review **was not performed** — to
cross-refer to it.

He does not know the file is locked. He does not know that `AV-AUD-19` requires dual approval from the
engagement partner and the Head of Quality. Nobody asks him for either. **The system permits both changes and
flags neither.**

**It is 68 days after the opinion was signed. It will matter enormously.**

## Act VI — Level 4: the inspection

### 14–25 July 20X5

Nathaniel Ferreira-Osei's team spends **141 hours** on a file the engagement partner reviewed for 11.5.

They do good work. They pull the e-file audit trail and establish the 11.5 hours, the 7.6% coverage, the
four-minute opening of `M-300`. They re-project the cut-off sample. They rebuild the going concern model and
find a covenant breach in a hidden column that four reviewers missed. They interview the National Technical
Panel.

### 12 August 20X5 — the draft

`INSP-F05`, a **finding**:

> *"Sufficient appropriate audit evidence was not obtained in respect of the estimate of costs to complete on
> contract CN-4471. The change in estimate increased revenue and profit before tax by £3.5m, being 113% of Group
> materiality... The retrospective review required by ISA 540 was scoped and not performed. **At least six items
> of evidence on the file inconsistent with management's estimate were not addressed, contrary to ISA 500.***
>
> ***This is a failure to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence over the audit's most significant risk and
> is a finding.***"*

The six items include the delay claim. **But it is listed as contrary evidence not addressed. The causal
question — why did a contract 40 weeks late get cheaper — still is not asked.**

Proposed grade: **3 — significant improvements required.**

### 29 August 20X5 — the response

Ronan Belliveau accepts almost everything. On this finding he contests one element:

> *"The single element I contest is the statement that the retrospective review required by ISA 540 was not
> performed. **`C-100/13` on the file is a two-page look-back analysis.** I attach it. It is high-level and it
> does not address CN-4471 specifically, so the criticism that the retrospective review was inadequate stands. But
> the finding as drafted says it was not performed, and there is a document on the file."*

He found it in August while looking for something to answer the finding with. **He does not check when it was
added.**

### 2 September 20X5

The lead inspector's internal assessment recommends **rejecting** this challenge and **retaining grade 3**.

### 9 September 20X5 — the meeting

Ninety minutes. Present: the lead inspector, the Head of Quality, and the engagement partner — who was the lead
inspector's own engagement partner on this client in FY20X1 and FY20X2, within the five-year period the firm's
own policy says bars him from inspecting it.

**No minute-taker. No minutes exist.**

### 11 September 20X5

Revised draft. Four findings reclassified. Grade 2.

`INSP-F05` becomes `INSP-IP10`, an improvement point. The rationale:

> *"**A look-back analysis is present at `C-100/13`**, though it is high-level and does not address the specific
> contract. The estimate remains within a range that a reasonable auditor could accept."*

### 12 September 20X5 — somebody asks

Rosalind Achebe-Kim, who performed 44 of the 141 hours, raises the timing of `C-100/13` with the lead inspector.

She is told **"the file is what it is and we review it as we find it."**

### 16 September 20X5 — she writes it down

> *"On `INSP-F05` I would want to know when `C-100/13` was put on the file before we rely on it. It is not
> referenced in the March completion memorandum and `C-100` §7 as I read it in July said the retrospective review
> had not been performed. **Either I misread it or something has been changed.**"*
>
> *"We have moved from six findings to two without a single fact changing... **What changed is the adjectives.**"*
>
> *"We are the firm's monitoring function. If we can find six things and report two, then what we report is not
> information about the firm's quality. **It is information about what the firm was prepared to write down.**"*

**She is right. Four months before the regulator establishes it.**

Her note is filed in the working papers. It is not referred to in the final report, not reported to the Risk and
Quality Committee, and not escalated — because the only escalation route runs over the head of the Head of
Quality to a committee on which he sits, and she is four years off partner.

### 30 September 20X5 — the final report

Two findings. **Grade 2 — compliant with limited improvements required.**

CN-4471 is an improvement point.

## Act VII — Level 5: the regulator

### 11 February 20X6

Olumide Ayakannu and Beatriz Salgado-Ferrer are working through Ashcroft Vane's inspection working papers.

**They find the dissent note.**

They request both versions of the report.

### 17 February 20X6

They obtain the e-file version history under s.44.

**`C-100/13` was added on 19 May 20X5.** Sixty-eight days after the opinion. Seven days after the file was
locked. No authorisation. And `C-100` §7 — which had recorded that the retrospective review was **not
performed** — amended the same day to point at it.

**The most significant substantive finding in the firm's inspection report was downgraded on the strength of a
document that did not exist when the opinion was signed.**

### 17 March 20X6 — the partner is shown the version history

He withdraws his contest that afternoon:

> *"The finding as originally drafted was correct. **There was no look-back analysis when the opinion was
> signed.**... A document supporting a critical judgement that is not referenced anywhere in the March completion
> memorandum should have made me look at the modification log, and it did not occur to me.*
>
> *The consequence is that the most significant substantive finding in this report was downgraded to an
> improvement point on the strength of a document that did not exist at the date of the opinion, **and I am the
> person who put it in front of the inspection team.** I did not intend that and I accept that intention is not
> the point."*

### The lead inspector

> *"What moved me on `INSP-F05` was the look-back analysis. Ronan pointed me to `C-100/13`. My fieldwork note
> said the retrospective review had not been performed, and there it was on the file. **I did not check when it
> had been put there.** That is the mistake and it is entirely mine. Had I opened the modification log I would
> have seen it was added on 19 May, without authorisation, **and the finding would have got stronger rather than
> weaker** — because a look-back analysis appearing on a file two months after the report date is a worse fact
> than no look-back analysis at all."*

### 24 July 20X6 — the finding

**`FRAOA-F04`, severity S1.**

And at `R-300` §4, for the first time in sixteen months and five levels of review, somebody puts the two facts
in one sentence:

> *"**A retrofit programme running 40 weeks late on two delivery gates, with a fixed depot, a fixed workforce and
> standing overhead, became £4.1m cheaper to complete in the same quarter — and the event ARG relies upon to
> explain the cost saving is the same event ARG relies upon to explain the delay.***
>
> *Both propositions may be true... But a 40-week delay on a contract of this type ordinarily increases cost, and
> **the audit file contains no consideration of the point at all.**"*

And:

> *"`C-950` §5.4 contains a row asking exactly the right question and the answer recorded against it is 'Not
> addressed'. The workpaper identified the question and left it open. **Nobody who reviewed the workpaper noticed
> that the row was blank.**"*

## Epilogue — how it turned out

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 12 Mar 20X5 | Unmodified opinion. £3.5m of profit accepted as within a reasonable range. |
| **28 Apr 20X5** | **Forecast costs +£3.2m** |
| **14 Nov 20X5** | **The MRA claim — "without merit", to be "resisted in full" — settles at £4.1m against ARG** |
| **3 Mar 20X6** | **FY20X5 results: cumulative adverse variance on CN-4471 of £9.1m. Contract margin restated from 22.4% to 11.4%.** |
| 3 Mar 20X6 | FY20X5 accounts include a material uncertainty related to going concern |
| 20X6 | The FY20X5 audit performs the look-back as remedial action E6. It finds the £9.1m. |

**The £74.8m estimate was understated by 12.2%.**

**The claim assessed as without merit cost £4.1m — almost exactly the £4.1m the estimate came down by.**

## What this case study is actually about

Read the sequence again and notice what is absent from it.

**No one lied.** The Project Director's email describes real workstreams. The Contract Review Board genuinely
minuted the change. The CFO's journal has a verifiable mechanism behind it. Management accepted every one of the
five adjustments the audit proposed, including a £1.24m inventory provision after a substantive challenge.

**No one lacked the information.** Every fact needed to reach the regulator's conclusion was in the audit file
on 12 March 20X5, in five workpapers the same team prepared. The delay claim was in `C-950`. The overdue
internal audit recommendation was in `B-100`. The £3.5m was computed in `C-100`. The uncorroborated percentage
complete was stated in `C-100` §8.2. The unasked question was printed on a schedule in `C-950` with the words
"Not addressed" beside it.

**No one failed to ask.** The manager asked for the look-back. The partner asked what corroborated the
estimate. The EQR said she would have wanted the look-back. The manager wrote down, the night before signing,
that a 40-week delay does not make a contract cheaper. **Four people identified the gap and four people
concluded anyway.**

**And nobody needed more resources.** The look-back took Dev Ramanathan two pages and part of a day in May. The
question about the delay claim takes one sentence. Opening the review notes register takes twenty minutes.
Checking the modification log takes one click.

What failed was not competence, honesty, information or budget. **What failed was that at each level, a
reviewer who had found the right problem accepted an answer that did not solve it, because the deadline was
fixed and the answer came from someone they trusted.** And when the level above was finally required to look
properly, it found the same thing, wrote it down correctly, and was then persuaded to unwrite it by a document
nobody thought to date.

That is the shape of most audit failures. Not a conspiracy. **A chain of reasonable-sounding concessions, each
one defensible on its own, ending at an opinion nobody had the evidence for.**
