# INDEPENDENT AUDITOR'S REPORT TO THE MEMBERS OF AUBURN RIDGE GROUP PLC

## Opinion

We have audited the financial statements of Auburn Ridge Group plc (the "Parent Company") and its
subsidiaries (the "Group") for the year ended 31 December 20X4, which comprise the consolidated income
statement, the consolidated statement of comprehensive income, the consolidated and Parent Company
balance sheets, the consolidated and Parent Company statements of changes in equity, the consolidated
cash flow statement, and the related notes 1 to 31, including the summary of significant accounting
policies.

In our opinion:

- the financial statements give a true and fair view of the state of the Group's and the Parent
  Company's affairs as at 31 December 20X4 and of the Group's profit for the year then ended;
- the Group financial statements have been properly prepared in accordance with IFRS as adopted in
  Meridia;
- the Parent Company financial statements have been properly prepared in accordance with IFRS as
  adopted in Meridia and as applied in accordance with the Meridian Companies Act 20W1; and
- the financial statements have been prepared in accordance with the requirements of the Meridian
  Companies Act 20W1.

## Basis for opinion

We conducted our audit in accordance with International Standards on Auditing as adopted in Meridia
(MAS) and applicable law. Our responsibilities under those standards are further described in the
Auditor's responsibilities section of our report. We believe that the audit evidence we have obtained
is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for our opinion.

### 1. Revenue recognition on long-term contracts

**Risk.** The Group recognised revenue of £831.7m over time on long-term contracts (20X3: £762.4m) and
held contract assets of £128.4m at the year end (20X3: £89.7m). Revenue is measured using an input
method, by reference to costs incurred as a proportion of total estimated contract costs. The estimate
of total contract costs requires significant judgement, and a change in that estimate has a direct and
immediate effect on the amount of revenue and margin recognised. There is a risk that estimated costs
to complete are understated, resulting in the overstatement of revenue and profit. We identified this as
an area at risk of fraud.

**Our response.** We selected twelve contracts with a combined contract value of £496.2m, on which £227.8m of revenue was
recognised in the period, representing 30.7% of segment revenue, on the basis of
size, movement in forecast margin, loss-making status and random selection. For each we:

- agreed the contract price to the executed contract and any variations;
- agreed costs incurred to the accounting records and tested a sample to supporting documentation;
- recalculated the percentage of completion and the revenue recognised;
- evaluated the estimate of costs to complete by obtaining and challenging management's forecast,
  discussing it with the relevant programme director, and considering it against the Contract Review
  Board's minuted deliberations;
- read contract correspondence for disputes, claims and variations, and confirmed the position directly
  with customers where practicable; and
- tested the cut-off of contract costs either side of the year end.

**Key observations.** Across the contracts we tested, the aggregate estimate of total contract costs
reduced by £2.3m during the fourth quarter. **The whole of that net reduction is attributable to a
single contract, on which management reduced the estimated cost to complete by £4.1m, increasing revenue
and profit before tax by £3.5m.** We obtained and challenged management's explanation, which attributes
the reduction to identified value engineering savings and to a reduction in the scope of third-party
certification. We consider the estimate of costs to complete to be within a reasonable range.

### 2. Impairment of goodwill relating to the Industrial Systems cash-generating unit

**Risk.** The Group carries goodwill of £412.3m, of which £112.7m is allocated to the Industrial Systems
cash-generating unit. The recoverable amount of that unit exceeds its carrying amount of £186.4m by
£3.9m, being 2.1%. The determination of recoverable amount requires judgement in forecasting cash flows
and in selecting a discount rate and a long-term growth rate. Given the limited headroom, there is a
risk that the unit is impaired and that the impairment is not recognised.

**Our response.** We rebuilt management's value in use model independently and tested its mathematical
integrity. We agreed the first three years of cash flows to the plan approved by the Board and assessed
the basis of the two-year extension. We compared the FY20X4 outturn for this unit to the forecast made
a year earlier. We benchmarked the long-term growth rate against economic and sector forecasts. **We
engaged our own valuation specialist to determine an independent range for the discount rate.** We
performed sensitivity analysis on each key assumption. We reviewed the adequacy of the related
disclosures.

**Key observations.** The unit is sensitive. An increase in the discount rate of 0.2 percentage points,
a reduction in the long-term growth rate of 0.5 percentage points, or a reduction in forecast cash flows
of 5% would each eliminate the headroom. The unit's FY20X4 operating cash flow was 18.5% below the
forecast made a year earlier. **The discount rate applied by management of 9.1% is below the range of
10.2% to 11.6% determined by our valuation specialist.** Having considered management's derivation of
its rate, which uses the Group's actual cost of borrowing and incorporates a unit-specific risk premium,
we concluded that the rate applied is at the low end of an acceptable range and that no impairment is
required at 31 December 20X4.

### 3. Accounting for the acquisition of Kestrel Dynamics Ltd

**Risk.** On 1 May 20X4 the Group acquired Kestrel Dynamics Ltd for total consideration of £86.0m,
including contingent consideration measured at a fair value of £11.5m against a maximum payable of
£18.0m. The identification and valuation of acquired intangible assets, and the measurement of
contingent consideration, require significant judgement. Contingent consideration is a Level 3 fair
value measurement.

**Our response.** We read the sale and purchase agreement and agreed the components of consideration to
the completion statement, share issue documentation and bank records. We assessed the competence and
objectivity of management's valuation expert. **We engaged our own valuation specialist to review the
identification and valuation of the acquired intangible assets.** We recalculated the valuation models,
tested the key inputs against Kestrel's historical data and external benchmarks, and assessed the useful
lives assigned. We agreed the mechanics of the earn-out to the sale and purchase agreement and
considered the Group's performance against the earn-out thresholds since acquisition. We reviewed the
IFRS 3 disclosures.

**Key observations.** We concur with the identification of developed technology, customer relationships
and brand as separately identifiable intangible assets, and we consider their aggregate value of £39.2m
to be within a reasonable range. Kestrel's annual recurring revenue has grown from £29.4m at acquisition
to £36.8m at the year end, which is ahead of the assumptions underlying the contingent consideration
measurement. No remeasurement of the contingent consideration has been recognised in the period.

teams and reviewed their reporting packs and completion memoranda.

Our audit procedures at the Group level, including our testing of revenue cut-off, were performed over
populations drawn from all locations including those subject only to analytical procedures.

### Extent to which the audit was considered capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud

We identified and assessed the risks of material misstatement due to fraud by, among other things:

- obtaining an understanding of the legal and regulatory framework applicable to the Group and the
  procedures in place for compliance;
- inquiring of management, internal audit and the Audit Committee about known or suspected instances of
  fraud and non-compliance;
- reading Board and Audit Committee minutes and the whistleblowing report register;
- discussing within the engagement team, including with component teams and with our IT and valuation
  specialists, where fraud might occur in the financial statements and how it might be perpetrated;
- considering the incentives and opportunities that exist within the Group, including **the linkage of
  senior management remuneration to adjusted earnings per share**;
- **identifying revenue recognition, in particular the estimation of costs to complete on long-term
  contracts and the cut-off of point-in-time revenue, as areas of heightened fraud risk;** and
- addressing the risk of management override of controls by testing journal entries selected using
  computer-assisted techniques against fourteen risk-based criteria, by reviewing accounting estimates
  for evidence of bias, and by evaluating the business rationale for significant transactions outside the
  normal course of business.

We incorporated unpredictability into our procedures, including by attending one inventory count
unannounced, by extending our testing to a location not previously visited, and by reducing our journal
entry testing threshold below the level applied in the prior year.
