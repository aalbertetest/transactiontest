## 4. Significant audit matters

### 4.1 Revenue recognition on long-term contracts

| | |
|---|---|
| **What we found** | We tested twelve contracts with a combined contract value of £496.2m, on which £227.8m of revenue was recognised in FY20X4 — 30.7% of Equipment & Systems revenue. Across the tested portfolio the aggregate estimate of total contract costs fell by £2.3m in Q4. **The entire net reduction is attributable to a single contract, CN-4471**, on which management reduced the estimated cost to complete by £4.1m. |
| **The effect** | The change in estimate increased FY20X4 revenue and profit before tax by **£3.5m**, which is above our materiality of £3.10m. The contract's forecast margin moved from 18.2% to 22.4%, against a portfolio average of 8.4%. |
| **Management's explanation** | Value engineering savings of £2.6m on the bogie assembly, £1.1m from a reduced certification scope following the Authority's acceptance of a revised test protocol, and a £0.4m reduction in programme contingency. |
| **Our work** | We obtained management's revised cost forecast, correspondence from the Project Director, and a record of our discussion with her. We read the December Contract Review Board minutes, which record the change. We tested costs incurred, recalculated the percentage complete, and reviewed the contract terms and variations. |
| **Our conclusion** | **We consider the estimate to be within a reasonable range.** |
| **What we would draw to your attention** | We did not obtain corroboration of the savings from the Group's engineering or quantity surveying functions, and we did not perform a look-back comparing prior contract cost estimates to actual outturns. **We note that your own internal audit function recommended in April 20X4 that management prepare such a look-back annually, that management agreed to do so by 31 December 20X4, and that the action remains outstanding.** We have included the retrospective review as a priority for the FY20X5 audit and we would encourage the Committee to press for implementation of the internal audit recommendation. |

### 4.2 Goodwill — Industrial Systems CGU

| | |
|---|---|
| **What we found** | Headroom of **£3.9m on a carrying amount of £186.4m — 2.1%**. Every other CGU has headroom above 50%. |
| **Sensitivity** | An increase in the discount rate of **0.2 percentage points**, a reduction in the terminal growth rate of 0.5 percentage points, or a **5% reduction in forecast cash flows** would each eliminate the headroom entirely. |
| **The discount rate** | Management has used a post-tax rate of **9.1%**. **Our own valuation specialist's independent range for this CGU is 10.2% to 11.6%.** Management's rate is below that range. At 10.2% an impairment of £22.1m would arise. |
| **Forecasting record** | The FY20X4 operating cash flow for this CGU was **18.5% below** the forecast made a year earlier. |
| **Our conclusion** | We have accepted management's rate. Management's derivation uses the Group's actual cost of borrowing and includes a CGU-specific risk premium. We regard the rate as at the low end of an acceptable range rather than outside it. **No impairment is required at 31 December 20X4.** |
| **A disclosure matter we ask the Committee to consider** | **See §5.** |

## 5. Disclosure matter for the Committee's consideration

**IAS 36 sensitivity disclosure — Industrial Systems CGU.**

IAS 36 requires that, where a reasonably possible change in a key assumption would cause the carrying
amount of a CGU to exceed its recoverable amount, the entity discloses the amount of the headroom, the
value assigned to the key assumption, and the amount by which that value would need to change to
eliminate the headroom.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Our view** | The condition is met. Headroom is 2.1% and a 0.2 percentage point movement in the discount rate eliminates it. **We asked management to make the disclosure on 26 February and again on 6 March 20X5.** |
| **Management's position** | That disclosing headroom of £3.9m would be misleading because the assumptions are prudent and the Board does not regard impairment as reasonably possible. Management has enhanced the narrative description instead, and the disclosure now includes the discount rate of 9.1% and the terminal growth rate of 2.0%. |
| **Our conclusion** | We have accepted management's position. The enhanced narrative discloses the underlying assumptions and enables a reader to form a view. **We do not regard this as a material misstatement, but we do not agree with management that the sensitivity is not reasonably possible, and we bring the matter to the Committee's attention so that the Committee can form its own view.** |

**We must report one matter.** Our internal policy caps non-audit fees for a listed audit client at

review performed by our consulting practice between July and November 20X4. The statutory cap of 70%

would place no reliance on any control affected by those recommendations**. We confirm that the
purchase-to-pay cycle has been audited on a fully substantive basis and that this safeguard has
operated.

We have reported the policy breach through our internal independence compliance process.

## 8. Significant deficiencies in internal control

We are required to report to you significant deficiencies in internal control identified during our
audit. **We identified four, all arising from the Aventine ERP migration.**

| # | Deficiency | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **D-01** | **14 users hold access exceeding their documented role definition. Two finance users can both create and approve journal entries and can amend vendor master data and release payments.** | These two capabilities together would allow an individual to create a supplier, approve a payment to it, and post the accounting entry, without any other person being involved. |
| **D-02** | **The privileged access log has not been reviewed since go-live.** 1,247 transactions were posted in H2 using a shared support account held by the implementation partner. | Activity by the most powerful accounts in the system is unmonitored. |
| **D-03** | **A shared account (`AVN_SUPPORT`) is in use.** | Activity cannot be attributed to an individual. |
| **D-04** | **47 of 312 changes to the production environment since go-live lack documented approval**, of which 11 affected financial reporting configuration and 2 altered posting logic. | Changes to how the system calculates and posts amounts are being made without a control. |

**In our view these four deficiencies should be read together.** Individually each is a control
weakness. Together they describe an environment in which a small number of individuals can post,
approve and conceal a transaction, and in which changes to the system's posting logic can be made
without approval or subsequent detection.

**Our audit response.** We have not relied on the purchase-to-pay authorisation controls. We reduced
our journal entry testing threshold from £500k to £250k and added criteria targeting new vendors,
manual payments and bank detail changes. Our conclusion at `B-200` is that reliance on IT general
controls remains appropriate for the full year, on the basis that the deficiencies concern the
administration of the environment rather than transaction processing, and that no misstatement has
been identified as arising from them.

**We understand management has commenced a remediation programme with a target date of 30 June 20X5.
We recommend the Committee track it directly.**

Twelve further deficiencies of lower severity have been reported to management in our management
letter dated 6 March 20X5.

### 9.2 Uncorrected

| # | Description | Effect on PBT £m |
|---|---|---:|
| U1 | Revenue recognised in December 20X4 on goods despatched 2–5 January 20X5, identified in our cut-off sample | 1.900 |
| U2 | ERP capitalisation — depreciation commencement date | 0.280 |
| U3 | Bonus accrual understated | 0.190 |
| | **Total overstatement of profit before tax** | **2.370** |
| | As a percentage of profit before tax | **3.80%** |
| | Group materiality | 3.100 |

**We have asked management to correct all three. Management has declined**, on the grounds that the
aggregate is below materiality, that each reverses in the following period, and that the same cut-off
practice applied in the comparative period.

**We are required to tell you two things about these items.**

First, all three affect **adjusted EPS**, the measure on which the Chief Financial Officer's long-term
incentive plan vests. Correcting them would reduce adjusted EPS from 149.2p to 145.1p. The vesting
range runs from a threshold of 138.0p to a maximum of 156.0p. **The vesting outcome would move from
62% of maximum to 39%.** We draw this to your attention as a qualitative factor bearing on the
Committee's consideration of the items, and not because we consider it to have influenced management's
position.

Second, the cut-off errors at U1 were identified in a sample. They arise from a **site-level invoicing
practice of raising invoices in December for goods ready for despatch but collected in January**. The
practice was found at three separate sites in two jurisdictions. We have reported it to management with
a recommendation that invoicing be aligned to the contractual transfer of control.

**We ask the Committee to consider whether it wishes management to correct these items.**
