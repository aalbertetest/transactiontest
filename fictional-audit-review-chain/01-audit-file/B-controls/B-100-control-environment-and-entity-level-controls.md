## 1. Purpose

To document the understanding of the components of the entity's system of internal control other
than control activities, and to evaluate whether the control environment provides an appropriate
foundation for the other components, in accordance with ISA 315 (Revised 2019).

### 2.1 Finance function capacity

The Group finance function absorbed three significant demands in FY20X4: the ERP migration, the
Kestrel integration, and the preparation of a first-time contingent consideration valuation.

| Indicator | FY20X3 | FY20X4 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Group finance headcount | 22 | 24 |
| Vacancies at 31 December | 1 | **4** |
| Turnover in Group finance | 14% | **27%** |
| Days to close the month (December) | 9 | **14** |
| Late journals posted after the reporting calendar deadline | 61 | **187** |

**Evaluation.** The finance function is under capacity strain. The month-end close lengthened by
five days and the number of late journals tripled. This is relevant to the assessed risk of
material misstatement in two ways: it increases the likelihood of error in the close process, and
it increases the volume of manual journals, which is the mechanism through which management
override would operate.

**Audit response.** No change to the planned approach, which is already fully substantive over the
financial close and reporting process, and which includes CAAT-based journal testing at `C-700`.
The journal testing threshold has been reduced from £500k to £250k (`A-500` §5).

### 5.1 The contract review board

The Group operates a Contract Review Board (CRB) chaired by the Chief Operating Officer, which
meets monthly to review contract performance and to approve changes to forecast costs on contracts
above £10m in value. This is the Group's principal control over the estimate that drives significant
risk 1.

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Membership | COO (chair), CFO, segment MDs, Group Financial Controller, programme directors |
| Frequency | Monthly |
| Threshold | Contracts with a value above £10m; 14 contracts in FY20X4 |
| Evidence of operation | Minutes and a standing pack of contract performance sheets |
| Approval of changes to estimated cost | Recorded in the minutes as approved, noted or deferred |

**Testing performed.** Minutes for all twelve FY20X4 meetings were read. The pack for the
December meeting (held 16 December 20X4) was inspected.

**Result.** The CRB met in all twelve months. Contract cost forecast changes were tabled and
recorded. The December meeting considered CN-4471 and records:

> *"CN-4471: forecast cost to complete revised to £74.8m (previously £78.9m) reflecting value
> engineering on bogie assembly and revised certification scope. Programme director to confirm
> savings are locked in with the supply chain before the year-end close. Noted."*

**Evaluation.** The control exists and operates. The engagement team has **not placed reliance on
the CRB as a control** because the audit approach to contract revenue is fully substantive
(`A-400` §6). The minutes are used as corroborative information at `C-100`.

*(The action recorded in the December minutes — "programme director to confirm savings are locked
in with the supply chain before the year-end close" — has not been followed up to determine whether
the confirmation was obtained. The January CRB pack was not requested.)*

## 7. Evaluation of the control environment

| Question | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Does the control environment provide an appropriate foundation for the other components of internal control? | **Yes, with reservations.** Governance and oversight are strong. Management's own risk identification is candid. The reservations are (a) finance function capacity strain, (b) the volume of overdue internal audit actions relating to the migration, and (c) the IT control position at `B-200`. |
| Do identified deficiencies in the control environment affect the audit approach? | The approach is already predominantly substantive. Controls reliance is planned in only two cycles (Aftermarket revenue and payroll), and both are contingent on the ITGC conclusion at `B-200`. |
| Are there indicators of an attitude permitting the override of controls? | No direct indicators. The incentive and opportunity conditions are present and are addressed at `A-500` and `C-700`. |

## 8. Conclusion

The control environment is adequate to provide a foundation for the audit approach set out at
`A-700`, subject to the conclusion reached at `B-200` on IT general controls.
