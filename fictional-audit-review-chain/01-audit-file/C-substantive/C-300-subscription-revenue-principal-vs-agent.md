## 4. Principal versus agent — the reseller channel

### 4.1 The arrangement

ARG appointed 15 additional reseller partners during FY20X4, taking the total to 19. Resellers sell
the Meridian Telemetry Suite to end customers in territories or verticals where ARG has no direct
sales presence.

| Term | Position under the standard reseller agreement |
|---|---|
| Who contracts with the end customer? | **The reseller** contracts with the end customer in its own name |
| Who sets the price to the end customer? | ARG publishes a recommended list price. **The reseller may discount at its own discretion**, up to 25% without approval. |
| Does the reseller hold inventory? | **No.** Licences are provisioned electronically by ARG on receipt of the reseller's order. |
| Who provides support to the end customer? | **ARG**, directly, under ARG's standard support terms. The end customer's support contract is with ARG. |
| Who bears credit risk on the end customer? | **The reseller.** ARG invoices the reseller and is paid regardless of whether the reseller collects. |
| Rights of return | **The reseller may return unactivated licences within 90 days** for full credit. |
| Reseller margin | 25% to 32% of list price, depending on tier |
| Is ARG obliged to fulfil? | Yes, on acceptance of a reseller order |
| Who is responsible if the software fails to perform? | **ARG**, under warranties given directly to the end customer |

### 4.3 Management's accounting memorandum

The Group's accounting memorandum on this question, obtained at `C-300/6`, is one paragraph and
reads in full:

> *"Reseller channel revenue is recognised gross. ARG is the primary obligor for the software: it
> provisions the licence, provides all support directly to the end customer, and gives the
> functional warranties on which the end customer relies. ARG controls the software before transfer
> and directs its use. Gross presentation is therefore appropriate under IFRS 15."*

### 4.4 Audit assessment

IFRS 15 requires an entity to determine whether it controls the specified good or service before it
is transferred to the customer. The standard sets out indicators that an entity controls the good or
service, including primary responsibility for fulfilment, inventory risk, and discretion in
establishing price.

| Indicator | Points toward | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Primary responsibility for fulfilling the promise | **Principal** | ARG provisions the licence and gives the functional warranty |
| Inventory risk before transfer | Neutral | Software licences carry no inventory risk in the conventional sense; the 90-day return right on unactivated licences means ARG bears some economic exposure |
| **Discretion in establishing the price to the end customer** | **Agent** | **The reseller sets the end price and may discount up to 25% without approval** |
| Who the end customer contracts with | **Agent** | The reseller contracts in its own name |
| Credit risk on the end customer | **Agent** | Borne by the reseller |
| Support relationship with the end customer | **Principal** | ARG contracts directly for support |

**The indicators are mixed.** Three point toward principal, three toward agent.

### 4.5 Conclusion recorded

> *"Management's conclusion that ARG is principal is supportable. The determinative factor is that
> ARG controls the software before it is transferred: it is ARG that provisions the licence, and the
> reseller cannot deliver anything to the end customer without ARG doing so. ARG's direct support
> obligation and functional warranty to the end customer reinforce that ARG is the party responsible
> for the specified service. The reseller's pricing discretion is real but operates within a
> framework ARG sets, and pricing discretion alone is not determinative.*
>
> *We have considered whether the growth in the channel from £18.2m to £38.2m changes the analysis.
> It does not: the contractual terms are unchanged from the four original partners.*
>
> *Accordingly, gross presentation is accepted. No adjustment is proposed."*

### 5.1 Activation rate analysis

| Period of sale to reseller | Licences sold £m | Activated by 28 Feb 20X5 | Activation rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Q1 20X4 | 6.1 | 5.9 | 96.7% |
| Q2 20X4 | 8.4 | 8.0 | 95.2% |
| Q3 20X4 | 9.6 | 8.9 | 92.7% |
| **Q4 20X4** | **14.1** | **10.2** | **72.3%** |
| — of which December 20X4 | 5.8 | 3.1 | **53.4%** |

**Assessment recorded:** the lower activation rate for Q4 and December sales is expected, since
activation typically occurs one to three months after the reseller's onward sale, and the December
cohort has had the least time to activate. The Q1 to Q3 rates demonstrate that licences do
ultimately activate.

*Note: the £0.31m of returns to 28 February 20X5 relates to licences sold throughout FY20X4. Of the
£2.7m of December-sold licences unactivated at 28 February 20X5, the 90-day return window expires
in March 20X5, after the date of this workpaper and after the audit report date.*
