### 3.4 A note on the presentation of the downside case in the model as filed

The downside case is present in the workbook at `C-900/3` on the tab `Scenario_DS`. The quarterly
covenant calculation for that scenario is in **columns AH to AK**. In the version of the workbook
provided to the audit team and filed, **columns AJ and AK are hidden**. Column AK contains the
30 September 20X5 test date, which is the 3.08× breach.

The summary tab `GC_Summary`, which is the tab reproduced in the Board paper, presents downside
leverage at only two dates: 31 March 20X5 (2.79×) and 31 December 20X5 (2.91×). Both are compliant.

*(The hidden columns and the two-date summary are recorded here as a description of the document as
received. The workpaper as archived on 12 March 20X5 contains the summary tab figures at §3.3 for
31 March and 31 December only. The 30 June and 30 September figures in the table at §3.3 above were
first extracted from the hidden columns by the internal inspection team in July 20X5 — see `I-300`
finding `INSP-F07` — and are included in this reconstructed workpaper so that the reader can see the
full position. **The 3.08× breach does not appear anywhere in the file as it stood at the audit
report date.**)*

| | FY20X4 forecast, made Mar 20X4 | FY20X4 actual | Variance |

**Recorded assessment:** *"Management forecast revenue and EBITDA accurately. Cash flow was
significantly below forecast, driven by the contract asset build described at `A-400` §3.1. Net debt
closed £35.5m worse than forecast. The FY20X5 base case assumes a £24m working capital inflow, being
the unwind of that build. The reliability of the FY20X5 base case therefore depends principally on
whether that unwind occurs."*

### 5.3 Evaluation of the downside case

> **Recorded conclusion:**
>
> *"Management has prepared a downside case applying reductions to revenue and margin and a delay to
> the working capital unwind. We have reviewed the summary of that case, which shows leverage of
> 2.79× at 31 March 20X5 and 2.91× at 31 December 20X5, in both cases within the 3.00× covenant.
> The downside sensitivities applied are reasonable in magnitude relative to the Group's historical
> volatility.*
>
> *We have considered whether a more severe downside would breach the covenant. A breach would
> require Adjusted EBITDA approximately 13% below the base case, or a working capital outflow of
> approximately £45m against the assumed £24m inflow. Neither is considered plausible on the basis of
> the order book and the billing schedules obtained."*

*(The calculation in the second paragraph is at `C-900/6`. It was performed against the base case
annual figures, not against the quarterly test dates. The quarterly profile in management's own
downside case breaches the covenant at 30 September 20X5 at a lower level of stress than the
workpaper's own threshold — because the 30 September test date combines the trough of the seasonal
cash cycle with the delayed working capital unwind. The team did not recalculate the downside case at
each quarterly test date, and the two test dates presented on the summary tab happen to be the two
least stressed.)*

## 8. Disclosure

### 8.1 The going concern statement as published

Note 2 to the financial statements states:

> *"The Directors have assessed the Group's ability to continue as a going concern for a period of at
> least twelve months from the date of approval of these financial statements. In making this
> assessment the Directors have considered the Group's cash flow forecasts, its committed facilities
> of £520.0m of which £442.0m was drawn at the year end, and its covenant headroom under both a base
> case and a severe but plausible downside scenario. In the downside scenario the Group continues to
> operate within its covenants throughout the assessment period.*
>
> *The Group's revolving credit facility of £220.0m matures in February 20X6. The Directors have
> commenced discussions with the Group's lending syndicate regarding a refinancing and, given the
> Group's investment grade credit rating and its track record of covenant compliance, have a
> reasonable expectation that a refinancing will be completed in good time.*
>
> *Accordingly, the Directors continue to adopt the going concern basis of accounting."*
