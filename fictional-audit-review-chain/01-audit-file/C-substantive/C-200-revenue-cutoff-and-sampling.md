## 1. Purpose

To obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence that revenue recognised at a point in time has been
recorded in the correct accounting period, in response to significant risk 2 (the presumed fraud
risk in revenue recognition under ISA 240).

## 2. Background

Point-in-time revenue of £452.9m arises principally in Aftermarket Services (£358.4m) and in the
hardware element of Software & Subscriptions. Revenue is recognised on despatch, which is when
control transfers under the Group's standard terms (Incoterms DAP, with control passing on
despatch under the specific contractual terms reviewed).

Control R2 — daily review of the unmatched despatch exception report — was tested at `B-300` §3.1
and **reliance could not be placed on it**. Cut-off is therefore addressed wholly substantively.

## 3. Population and sampling

### 3.1 Population definition

| | |
|---|---|
| Population | All despatches recorded in the Group despatch log between 15 December 20X4 and 15 January 20X5 |
| Rationale for the window | ±16 days around the year end. Prior year testing used ±14 days and identified no errors; the window was widened this year in response to the elevated fraud risk assessment. |
| Locations included | Meridia (all sites), Poland, Germany, **Delrado (ARG Aftermarket Inc)** |
| **Number of items** | **2,317** |
| **Value** | **£312.4m** |

The inclusion of the Delrado despatches is necessary because ARG Aftermarket Inc is subject to
analytical procedures only at component level (`A-600` §3.2), and the group team's cut-off testing
is the procedure relied upon to address cut-off at that location.

### 3.2 Completeness of the population

| Procedure | Result |
|---|---|
| Reconcile the despatch log to revenue recorded in the period | Agreed within £0.09m, being credit notes |
| Agree the record count to the system control total | Agreed |
| Test the completeness and accuracy of the despatch report used | **Not performed** — see `B-200` §7, which records that 10 of 14 system-generated reports were not tested for completeness and accuracy, including the despatch report |
| Sequence check on despatch note numbers | Performed. 4 gaps identified, all traced to cancelled despatch notes. |

### 3.3 Sample selection

| | |
|---|---|
| Method | Monetary unit sampling |
| Tolerable misstatement | Performance materiality, £2.17m |
| Expected misstatement | £0.30m |
| Confidence factor | 3.0 (95% confidence, expected error) |
| Calculated sample size | **40 items** |
| Sampling interval | £312.4m ÷ 40 = £7.81m |
| **Items selected** | **40** |
| **Value of items selected** | **£84.6m** |
| Coverage | 27.1% of the population by value |

| Sample composition | Items | Value £m |
|---|---:|---:|

| Poland | 8 | 16.4 |
| Germany | 5 | 11.8 |

## 4. Procedures performed

For each of the 40 items:

| # | Procedure |
|---|---|
| 4.1 | Agree the despatch date to the signed despatch note or carrier collection record |
| 4.2 | Agree the despatch note to the sales order and the customer purchase order |
| 4.3 | Agree the invoice to the despatch note as to quantity, description and price |
| 4.4 | Agree the accounting period in which revenue was recognised to the despatch date |
| 4.5 | For items despatched in the window either side of the year end, agree to third-party evidence of the date control transferred (carrier manifest or customer acknowledgement) |
| 4.6 | Consider whether any right of return, acceptance clause or bill-and-hold arrangement affects the timing |

## 5. Results

| Result | Items | Value £m |
|---|---:|---:|
| No exception | 37 | 82.7 |
| **Exception — revenue recognised in the wrong period** | **3** | **1.90** |
| **Total** | **40** | **84.6** |

### 5.1 The three exceptions

| # | Site | Despatch note | Invoice value £m | Recorded in | Actual despatch date | Third-party evidence |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| E1 | Verrastown Central | DN-114872 | **0.94** | December 20X4 | **2 January 20X5** | Carrier manifest dated 2 Jan |
| E2 | Kirkwall Road | DN-114966 | **0.61** | December 20X4 | **5 January 20X5** | Carrier collection record dated 5 Jan |
| E3 | Delrado (Fenwick, DL) | DN-D-08814 | **0.35** | December 20X4 | **3 January 20X5** | Customer acknowledgement dated 6 Jan |
| | | | **1.90** | | | |

In each case the goods were physically despatched in January 20X5 but the invoice was dated
31 December 20X4 and revenue was recognised in FY20X4.

### 5.2 Investigation of the exceptions

Discussed with the Sales Operations Manager and the Financial Controller on 17 February 20X5.

Explanation obtained:

> *"These are the year-end 'invoice-on-order-ready' cases. Where goods are picked, packed and
> ready for despatch at the year end but the carrier collection falls in the first working days of
> January, the sites have historically invoiced in December on the basis that the goods are
> committed to the customer. The Aventine implementation has not changed this practice. We accept
> that under the contractual terms control passes on despatch, so strictly these should be January
> revenue."*

Further procedures performed:

| Procedure | Result |
|---|---|
| Inquire whether the practice was directed by Group finance or arose locally | Local practice at site level; no evidence of Group direction |
| Inspect whether the practice existed in the prior year | Prior year cut-off testing (14 items, ±14 day window) identified no exceptions. The prior year sample did not include either Verrastown Central or the Delrado site in the relevant window. |
| Consider whether the exceptions indicate fraud | The pattern is consistent across three independent sites in two jurisdictions and is explained by a long-standing operational practice rather than a directed manipulation. No evidence of concealment: the despatch notes are correctly dated and the carrier records are on file. **Assessed as error, not fraud.** |
| Report to management | Yes, in the management letter at `B-200/9`, recommending that the invoicing practice be aligned to the contractual transfer of control |

### 5.3 Quantification

| | £m |
|---|---:|
| **Factual misstatement — the errors actually identified in the sample** | **1.90** |
| Effect | Revenue and profit before tax overstated in FY20X4; reversing in FY20X5 |

The £1.90m has been added to the schedule of uncorrected misstatements at `D-100` as item U1.

**Management's position:** management declined to adjust, on the basis that the amount is below
Group materiality of £3.10m, that the effect reverses in the following period, and that the same
practice would have applied at the prior year end so that the opening position is similarly stated.

*Note: the assertion that the same practice applied at the prior year end has not been tested. The
prior year sample identified no exceptions, but as recorded at §5.2 it did not cover the sites at
which the FY20X4 exceptions arose.*

## 6. Evaluation of the sample results

Firm methodology requires the evaluation of a sample to consider:

| Consideration | Recorded conclusion |
|---|---|
| Are the misstatements identified representative of the population, or anomalous? | The three errors arise at three different sites from a common operational practice. They are **not anomalous**. |
| Is the sample sufficient to conclude on the population? | Yes; the planned sample was completed. |
| Should the nature and cause of the misstatements be considered for their effect on other areas of the audit? | Considered. The practice affects point-in-time revenue only. Contract revenue is unaffected. |
| **Should the misstatement be projected to the population?** | **See §6.1** |

### 6.1 Projection

> **Recorded conclusion:**
>
> *"The misstatements identified total £1.90m against Group materiality of £3.10m. The errors are

## 7. Additional cut-off procedures

| Procedure | Result |
|---|---|
| Review of credit notes issued between 1 January and 28 February 20X5 above £0.155m, to identify December revenue subsequently reversed | 14 credit notes totalling £4.1m. 11 relate to pricing adjustments on FY20X4 invoices raised before December. **3 relate to December despatches, totalling £0.42m** — traced and confirmed as genuine returns, not cut-off errors. |
| Analytical review of daily revenue in the last 10 working days of December against the monthly average | December daily average £2.14m against the annual daily average £1.74m. The final three working days show £3.8m, £4.2m and £5.1m. Explained as normal year-end order fulfilment; the same pattern is present in FY20X2 and FY20X3. |
| Review of manual revenue journals posted in the last week of December and the first week of January | 22 journals. All traced to supporting documentation. One journal of £0.9m posted by the CFO — see `C-700` §6. |
| Goods received not invoiced / despatched not invoiced reconciliation at the year end | Agreed |

## 8. Conclusion

Cut-off testing on a sample of 40 items with a value of £84.6m from a population of 2,317 items
valued at £312.4m identified three exceptions totalling £1.90m. The exceptions arise from an
operational invoicing practice at site level, are assessed as error rather than fraud, and have been
reported to management and included in full on the schedule of uncorrected misstatements.

Sufficient appropriate audit evidence has been obtained that point-in-time revenue is recorded in
the correct period, subject to the uncorrected misstatement of £1.90m reported at `D-100`.

Significant risk 2 is addressed.
