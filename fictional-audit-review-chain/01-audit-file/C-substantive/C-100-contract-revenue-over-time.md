## 3. Accounting policy and its application

Revenue on design-and-build contracts is recognised over time because the assets created have no
alternative use to the Group and the Group has an enforceable right to payment for performance
completed to date. Progress is measured using an **input method**: costs incurred to date as a
proportion of total estimated contract costs.

The policy is unchanged from prior year and has been reviewed against IFRS 15. The enforceable
right to payment has been verified by reading the termination-for-convenience clauses in the five
contracts scoped for detailed testing. All five contain a right to payment for work performed plus
a reasonable margin.

### 6.3 Management's explanation

The reduction of £4.1m comprises:

| Component | £m | Explanation given |
|---|---:|---|
| Bogie assembly value engineering | 2.6 | Redesign of the mounting assembly reducing machining hours and material |
| Third-party certification | 1.1 | MRA accepted a revised test protocol reducing the number of certification runs from 6 to 4 |
| Contingency release | 0.4 | Programme contingency reduced from 4% to 3.5% of remaining cost |
| **Total** | **4.1** | |

> *"Dev — confirming the position we discussed. We have taken £2.6m out of the bogie workstream

> we have trimmed contingency to 3.5%. Net £4.1m. Happy to talk it through. Fiona."*

The file note of the telephone inquiry records that the Project Director was asked whether the
savings were contractually secured with the supply chain and responded that the redesign had been
"agreed in principle with the two main subcontractors and we are working through the commercial
paperwork".

### 6.5 Evaluation

The explanation given is specific, is attributable to identifiable workstreams, and is consistent
with the Group's stated value-engineering strategy at `A-400` §2.3. The Contract Review Board
considered and recorded the change in December 20X4. The Project Director is the individual with
the best knowledge of the programme.

The absence of contemporaneous supply chain documentation is noted. Management's position is that
the commercial paperwork was in progress at the year end and that the technical basis for the
saving was established.

**Conclusion:** the estimate of costs to complete on CN-4471 is within a reasonable range. The
change in estimate is appropriately accounted for prospectively in the period in which it arose.

### 8.1 CN-4471 — Meridian Rail Authority delay claim

On **4 February 20X5** the MRA served notice of a claim for liquidated damages of **£6.8m** in
respect of delays to the CN-4471 programme.

This is addressed at `C-950` (subsequent events). In summary: management assesses the claim as
without merit and intends to resist it; the Group's external legal advisers have provided a letter
stating that the claim is "at an early stage and the outcome cannot presently be predicted with
confidence"; no provision has been made; the matter is disclosed as a contingent liability.

For the purposes of this workpaper, the claim has been considered in relation to:

| Question | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Does the claim affect the contract price? | No. It is a claim against the Group, not a reduction in the transaction price agreed. Variable consideration constraints have been considered and no adjustment is required at this stage. |
| Does the claim indicate that the contract is loss-making? | No. Even if the claim succeeded in full, the contract would remain profitable at the current forecast margin. |
| Is a provision required? | No — see `C-950`. |
