To document the identification and assessment of the risks of material misstatement due to fraud,
the engagement team discussion, and the planned responses, in accordance with ISA 240.

### 2.1 Matters discussed

1. How and where the financial statements might be susceptible to material misstatement due to
   fraud, including how assets might be misappropriated
2. The circumstances that might indicate earnings management
3. Known external and internal factors that might create incentive, opportunity or an attitude
   permitting rationalisation
4. The importance of maintaining professional scepticism throughout
5. How the team would respond if fraud or suspected fraud were identified
6. The reporting lines if any member of the team had concerns

### 2.2 Points recorded from the discussion

> **RB:** The commercial context matters this year. The CFO's LTIP measurement period ends in
> FY20X5 and FY20X4 is the year that sets up the final number. Adjusted EPS is the metric. Anything

>
> **SL:** The area where a small number of people can move a large amount of profit is contract cost
> estimates. There are 61 open contracts and the top five are 58% of the segment. A change in

>
> **DR:** The rail contracts are also where the delivery pressure is. If the programmes are running
> late, that shows up first as cost, and cost is what we cannot independently observe.
>
> **YD:** Post-migration, my concern is that we cannot yet demonstrate who has access to post
> journals. If access provisioning is not controlled, a manual journal could come from anywhere.
>
> **EB:** Should we look at payments to suppliers who are new this year? With the migration, new

>
> **RB:** Yes. Add it to the journal work. Keep the scope proportionate.
>
> **SL:** I would also flag revenue cut-off at the Aftermarket sites. It is the volume area and

## 3. Fraud risk factors identified

|---|---|---|

## 4. Assessed fraud risks and responses

### 4.1 Presumed risk — revenue recognition (ISA 240)

The presumption that there are risks of fraud in revenue recognition has **not been rebutted**.

Where in revenue the risk lies:

| Revenue stream | Fraud risk | Rebutted? | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contract revenue over time | **Manipulation of costs to complete to accelerate margin** | No | `C-100` — including retrospective review of estimates |
| Aftermarket point in time | **Cut-off manipulation at the period end** | No | `C-200` — cut-off testing either side of year end |
| Software and subscription | Recognition of term licence revenue in the wrong period; side agreements | No | `C-300` — contract review, confirmations |
| Reseller channel | Channel stuffing at period end | No | `C-300` — return rates, post-year-end activation analysis |

### 4.2 Presumed risk — management override of controls (ISA 240)

Cannot be rebutted. Response at `C-700`:

1. Testing the appropriateness of journal entries, with CAAT-based selection
2. Reviewing accounting estimates for evidence of bias, including a retrospective review of prior
   period estimates
3. Evaluating the business rationale for significant transactions outside the normal course

### 4.3 Additional fraud risk — misappropriation through the procurement cycle

Not a presumed risk, but identified in the team discussion in light of the access control
deficiencies. Response: additional CAAT criteria in `C-700` covering new vendors created in the
period, payments made outside the standard payment run, and vendors whose bank details were amended
within 30 days of a payment.

## 5. Response to fraud risks — incorporation of unpredictability

ISA 240 requires an element of unpredictability in the selection of procedures. For FY20X4:

| Unpredictable element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Unannounced inventory count attendance | One of the four sites attended (Kirkwall Road spares depot, 29 Jan 20X5) was unannounced |
| Extension of testing to a location not scoped | Substantive testing of the Aftermarket Netherlands branch, not previously visited |
| Adjusted sampling threshold | Journal entry testing threshold set at £250k rather than the prior year £500k |
| Direct confirmation with a non-standard counterparty | Confirmation requested from the MRA's programme office as well as its accounts payable function |

## 6. Retrospective review of estimates

ISA 240 requires a review of prior period accounting estimates for indications of possible bias.

| Estimate | FY20X3 amount | Outturn in FY20X4 | Direction | Conclusion |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Warranty provision | £14.2m | Utilised £11.8m, released £1.1m | Overstated by £1.1m | Immaterial; not indicative of bias |
| Inventory provision | £17.4m | Utilised £9.2m, released £0.4m | Broadly accurate | No bias |
| Expected credit loss | £6.1m | Utilised £5.4m, released £0.3m | Broadly accurate | No bias |
| Contract loss provisions | £3.8m | Utilised £3.6m | Accurate | No bias |
| **Contract costs to complete (portfolio level)** | | | | **See below** |

**Contract costs to complete.** A retrospective review at the level of individual contracts has not
been performed for FY20X4. The prior year file (`FY20X3 C-100`) contains a portfolio-level
comparison showing that the aggregate of forecast contract margins at 31 December 20X3 was 6.4%
against an outturn of 6.4% on contracts completed in FY20X4, and this has been accepted as
evidence that the estimation process is not systematically biased.

*(Preparer's note: a contract-level look-back for the five largest contracts was scoped in the
audit programme at `A-700/2` procedure 7.4. It was not performed. Time was reallocated to the
Kestrel acquisition following the arrival of the completion accounts in February. DR 26/02/X5.)*

## 9. Conclusion

Two presumed fraud risks are identified and not rebutted: revenue recognition and management
override of controls. One additional fraud risk is identified in the procurement cycle. The
responses set out above are incorporated into the audit programme at `A-700`.

The most significant fraud risk factor identified is the combination of (a) the CFO's incentive
linked to adjusted EPS, (b) the subjectivity of contract cost estimates, and (c) the degraded access
control environment following the migration. The audit response focuses on `C-100`, `C-200` and
`C-700`.
