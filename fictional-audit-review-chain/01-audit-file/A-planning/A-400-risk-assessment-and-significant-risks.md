## 1. Purpose

To document the understanding of the entity and its environment, the applicable financial reporting
framework and the entity's system of internal control, and to identify and assess the risks of
material misstatement at the financial statement and assertion levels, in accordance with
ISA 315 (Revised 2019).

## 2. Understanding the entity

### 2.1 The business model

ARG earns revenue in three ways, each with a different risk profile:

| Segment | How revenue is earned | Judgement intensity | Principal risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equipment & Systems | Long-term design-and-build contracts, revenue over time on an input method | **Very high** — every contract requires an estimate of total cost | Estimation and management bias |
| Aftermarket Services | Spares and services, mostly point in time on despatch | Low | Volume and cut-off |
| Software & Subscriptions | Term licences, SaaS, bundled implementation, direct and reseller channels | **High** — allocation, timing, gross vs net | Recognition policy |

### 3.1 Investigation of variances outside expectation

**Contract assets, +43.1% against an expectation of +10–12%.**

Discussed with the Group Financial Controller (P. Aylesworth) on 4 December 20X4 and again on
26 February 20X5. Explanation obtained:

> *"The movement is driven by the phasing of milestone billings on CN-4471 and two other rail
> contracts. Under those contracts we bill on acceptance of defined delivery gates rather than on a
> monthly application basis, and three gates that were scheduled for Q4 have moved into Q1 and Q2
> 20X5 for programme reasons. The revenue is earned; the invoicing is behind it. It will unwind in
> H1 20X5."*

Assessment: the explanation is plausible and consistent with the contract terms, which the team has
read at `C-100/2`. Contract assets are tested for recoverability as part of `C-100`.

**Operating cash conversion, 47.4% against an expectation of 60–65%.**

This is the arithmetic consequence of the contract asset movement above (£38.7m of the £48.6m
working capital outflow) together with a £27.1m increase in trade payables in FY20X3 that did not
repeat. Explanation accepted as consistent with the contract asset position.

**Equipment segment margin, +0.5pp against an expectation of flat.**

Discussed with the Group Financial Controller. Explanation: value engineering savings realised
across the rail programmes in H2, in particular on CN-4471. Consistent with the strategic objective
at §2.3. Contract margins are tested at `C-100`.

### 3.2 Conclusion on preliminary analytical review

Three variances outside expectation were identified and explained. All three relate, directly or
indirectly, to the rail contract portfolio and specifically to CN-4471. The audit response is to
apply increased focus to contract accounting at `C-100`, which is already identified as a
significant risk.

## 5. Understanding of the system of internal control

Documented in section B. Summary of the components:

| Component | Assessment | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Control environment | Adequate, with the reservations at `B-100` §5 | `B-100` |
| Entity's risk assessment process | Adequate | `B-100` |
| Information system and communication | **Under strain following the migration** | `B-200` |
| Control activities | Mixed — see the deficiency log | `B-300` |
| Monitoring of controls | Adequate; internal audit is active and reports to the Audit Committee | `B-100` |

**General IT controls.** The migration to Aventine on 1 July 20X4 means that for FY20X4 the Group
operated two financial systems in the same reporting period. The audit approach must address:

1. The completeness and accuracy of data migrated
2. Whether ITGCs over the new environment were operating effectively from go-live
3. Whether controls in the legacy environment operated effectively to 30 June 20X4
4. Whether reliance on automated controls and system-generated reports is supportable in either
   environment

This is addressed at `B-200`.

## 6. Reliance on controls

| Cycle | Planned approach | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue — Aftermarket (point in time) | **Controls reliance plus substantive** | High volume; controls tested at interim |
| Revenue — Contracts (over time) | **Fully substantive** | Estimation-based; controls not capable of addressing the risk |
| Revenue — Software | Fully substantive | Judgement-based |
| Purchases and payables | **Fully substantive** | Independence safeguard `A-200` §4.3(4) precludes reliance on affected authorisation controls |
| Payroll | Controls reliance plus substantive | Stable, automated |
| Inventory | Fully substantive | |
| Treasury | Fully substantive | Low volume, high value |
| Financial close and reporting | Fully substantive | |

Controls reliance in the two cycles above is contingent on the conclusion at `B-200` that IT
general controls are operating effectively. If that conclusion cannot be reached, the approach in
those cycles must revert to fully substantive.
