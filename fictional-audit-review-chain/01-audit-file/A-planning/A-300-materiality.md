## 1. Purpose

To determine materiality for the financial statements as a whole, performance materiality, the
clearly trivial threshold, and any lower materiality for particular classes of transactions,
balances or disclosures, in accordance with ISA 320.

## 2. Selection of benchmark

### 2.1 Candidate benchmarks

| Benchmark | FY20X4 forecast | Common range | Implied materiality range |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| **Profit before tax** | £62.4m | 5% – 10% | £3.1m – £6.2m |
| Profit before tax and exceptional items | £70.6m | 5% – 10% | £3.5m – £7.1m |
| Revenue | £1,284.6m | 0.5% – 1% | £6.4m – £12.8m |
| Total assets | £1,742.5m | 1% – 2% | £17.4m – £34.9m |
| Equity | £662.5m | 1% – 5% | £6.6m – £33.1m |
| Adjusted EBITDA | £171.2m | 2% – 5% | £3.4m – £8.6m |

### 2.2 Benchmark selected: profit before tax

Reasons:

1. ARG is a listed, profit-oriented entity. Profit is the measure by which the principal users —
   equity investors and analysts — assess performance.
2. Profit before tax is the measure most consistently referenced in the Group's own investor
   communications, alongside the adjusted EPS APM.
3. Profit is stable and positive across the last five years and is not distorted by an unusual
   event in FY20X4.
4. The Group's debt covenants are set on Adjusted EBITDA, but the equity market is the more
   significant user group.

**Profit before exceptional items was considered and rejected.** The exceptional items in FY20X4
(£8.2m: transaction costs and restructuring) are cash costs of decisions taken by management in the
period. Using a benchmark that excludes them would set materiality by reference to a number that
overstates the profit available to shareholders. Prior year practice was also to use reported PBT,
and consistency assists comparability of the misstatement evaluation year on year.

## 3. Materiality for the financial statements as a whole

| | Planning (Sep 20X4) | Final (Feb 20X5) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Profit before tax | £61.0m (forecast) | **£62.4m (actual)** |
| Percentage applied | 5.0% | **5.0%** |
| **Group materiality** | **£3.05m** | **£3.10m** |

**Why 5% and not 10%?** The lower end of the range is appropriate because:

- The entity is listed with a significant retail shareholder base
- Accounting is complex and estimate-dependent, so the risk of undetected misstatement is higher
- The Group's client risk classification is Elevated (`A-100`)
- Debt covenants exist, giving a second class of user with a lower tolerance
- The CFO's incentive arrangements are linked to a profit-derived measure

The change from planning to final materiality is 1.6% and does not require any revision to the
audit plan or to sample sizes already determined.

## 4. Performance materiality

| | |
|---|---:|
| Group materiality | £3.10m |
| Percentage applied | **70%** |
| **Performance materiality** | **£2.17m** |

### 4.1 Rationale for 70%

Firm methodology offers a range of 50% to 75%, with the following guidance:

| Factor | Lower end (50–60%) | Upper end (65–75%) | ARG assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prior year misstatements | Numerous or large | Few and small | FY20X3: 4 corrected totalling £1.8m, 2 uncorrected totalling £0.6m → **upper** |
| Expected number of misstatements | High | Low | Consistent with prior year → **upper** |
| Reliability of the control environment | Weak | Strong | See below → **contested** |
| First-year audit | Yes | No | Fourth year → **upper** |
| Significant changes in the business or systems | Yes | No | **ERP migration and an acquisition → lower** |

**Conclusion recorded at planning (Sep 20X4):** 70% is appropriate. The prior year misstatement
history is good, this is the fourth year of the engagement, and the team's knowledge of the entity
is strong. The ERP migration is acknowledged as a factor pointing the other way, but the migration
was still in its first quarter at the date of planning and its effects were not yet known.

**Reconsideration at interim (Nov 20X4).** Following interim controls testing, the question of
whether performance materiality should be reduced was revisited. `B-300` identifies control
deficiencies in the Aventine environment. The conclusion reached was that performance materiality
should remain at 70% because the deficiencies identified are in access provisioning and change
management rather than in transaction-level processing, and no misstatement had been identified as
resulting from them.

*(Prepared by DR. Manager comment at review: "Agree conclusion but I would want us to revisit if
anything else emerges from the ERP work. SL 21/11/X4." No further revisit is recorded.)*

### 4.2 Effect of the percentage chosen

| 60% | 1.86 | Cut-off sample rises to approximately 47 items |

## 5. Clearly trivial threshold

| | |
|---|---:|
| Percentage of Group materiality | 5% |
| **Clearly trivial threshold** | **£0.155m** |

Misstatements below £155k need not be accumulated on `D-100` unless, in the judgement of the
engagement team, they are qualitatively material. Qualitative factors requiring accumulation
regardless of amount:

- Any misstatement affecting a covenant calculation
- Any misstatement affecting directors' remuneration or a related party disclosure
- Any misstatement arising from a suspected irregularity
- Any misstatement affecting the adjusted EPS APM or a KPI disclosed in the strategic report

## 6. Lower materiality for particular items

ISA 320 requires the auditor to consider whether misstatements of amounts below materiality could
reasonably be expected to influence users' decisions.

| Class | Specific materiality | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| **Related party transactions** | **£0.100m** | Statutory disclosure requirement; users' interest is in existence and nature rather than amount |
| **Directors' remuneration** | **£0.100m** | Statutory and Listing Rule disclosure; sensitivity to users |
| Audit fee disclosure | £0.025m | Statutory disclosure |
| Covenant-relevant balances | £3.10m, but qualitative override | A misstatement of any size that changes covenant compliance is material |
