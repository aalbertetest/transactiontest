## 1. Why this is the centre of the inspection

ISA 220 (Revised) places responsibility for the overall quality of an engagement on the engagement partner.
That responsibility is discharged through direction, supervision and review, and it is not delegable.

On this engagement the audit team performed 3,140 hours of work and produced 4,127 workpapers. The
engagement partner's documented review of that work occupied **11.5 hours** and touched **312 workpapers**.
Every issue subsequently identified in this inspection was present in the file at the date of signature.

This section therefore asks a narrower question than the rest of the report: **not whether the audit was
deficient, but whether the review that was supposed to detect the deficiencies could have done so.**

### 2.5 The observation that follows from §2.4

The partner raised a note every 36 minutes of review time. The manager raised one every 75 minutes.

**The partner's review was faster and less productive at the same time.** 68% of his notes posed no
question. Of the six that did, four were cleared the same day and one obtained new evidence.

A review that generates notes at twice the rate and evidence at one-ninth the rate is not reviewing. It is
annotating.

## 3. The review of the manager's review

This is the finding NQMG regards as the most important in the report, and it is the one that changed least
between draft and final.

### 3.1 What the partner did and did not do

| Step | Performed |
|---|:-:|
| Read the schedule of matters escalated to him (`M-500`) and responded to all five | ✔ |
| Discussed the file with the manager (three discussions, 1 h 35 m) | ✔ |
| Confirmed via the system dashboard that all review notes were closed | ✔ |
| **Opened the review notes register (`M-200`)** | **✖** |
| **Tested any sample of the manager's clearances** | **✖ — 0 of 49** |
| **Reviewed the manager's coverage of the file** | **✖** |
| **Considered the manager's hours (118 against 158 budgeted)** | **✖** |
| **Read the manager's review record (`M-100`)** | **4 minutes** |
| **Read the manager's conclusion memorandum (`M-300`)** | **4 minutes** |
| **Read the manager's coaching file (`M-400`)** | **Not opened** |

### 3.2 What the register would have shown

The review notes register is a standard report in the engagement management system, reachable in one click
from the dashboard the partner did consult. It would have shown:

| Visible on the register | What it indicates |
|---|---|
| 19 of 49 notes closed on explanation alone | Over a third of the review obtained no new evidence |
| **11 notes closed by the preparer, not the reviewer** | **Direct breach of `AV-AUD-14`** |
| 11 notes closed on the day raised | A proportion of the review was not a review |
| **Zero notes on `B-200`; zero notes on `C-500`** | **Two significant-risk workpapers reviewed by nobody** |
| **Zero of three going concern notes closed on new evidence** | The going concern conclusion rested entirely on explanation |
| **One note on `C-800`, raised and closed the same day** | Something on the related party workpaper warranted a note |

**Each of the last three rows leads directly to an issue in the spine.** The partner's own retrospective
assessment (`P-200` §6, prepared August 20X5) reaches the same conclusion and estimates the time required
at twenty minutes.

## 4. Direction and supervision, as distinct from review

ISA 220 (Revised) requires direction and supervision as well as review. The inspection examined whether the
engagement was adequately directed and supervised, and found three failures independent of the review
failures.

### 4.1 The resourcing decision

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 20X4-09-15 | `A-100` §4.3, approved by the engagement partner, records that the senior in charge is new to the role and that **40 hours are added to the manager's budget** to provide additional direction and supervision. Manager's budget: 158 hours. |
| 20X4-09-15 | `A-100` §4.2 records that the engagement partner considered moving the client risk classification from Elevated to High and declined, because High would require a second partner review at interim *"which the fee does not support"*. The manager's contemporaneous note records that she would have been comfortable with High. |
| 20X5-02-26 | Manager raises resourcing verbally with the engagement partner |
| 20X5-03-06 | Manager raises resourcing verbally with the engagement partner again |
| 20X5-03-10 | `A-700` §6 records manager hours of 118 against 158 |
| 20X5-03-11 | `D-200` §9.3 and `M-100` §3.1 record the same |
| **Outcome** | **No action taken. The 40 hours added specifically to supervise a first-year senior in charge were not worked, and the engagement partner was told so twice before signing.** |

The manager was carrying three December year-end engagements and recorded 214 hours across all three in the
fortnight to 11 March 20X5.

### 4.2 The two unreviewed workpapers

| | `B-200` | `C-500` |
|---|---|---|
| Significant risk | 9 | 5 |
| Prepared by | Y. Demirci, IT Audit Assistant Manager | A. Brennan-Sutton, Assistant |
| Preparer's position | Specialist function, separate reporting line | **Joined the engagement 2 February 20X5** |
| Budget | 90 h | **140 h** |
| Actual | 74 h | **86 h** |
| Manager review | **None** | **None** |
| Partner review | 20 min, no notes | **None** |
| Manager's stated reason | Outside her technical competence; assumed the partner would review it (`M-100` §4.2) | Assumed the valuation specialist's involvement covered the acquisition (`M-100` §4.3) |
| Partner's stated reason | Reviewed it, raised nothing | Understood the firm's specialist had reviewed the acquisition accounting (`P-100` §5) |
| **The gap** | **Each reviewer assumed the other, or a specialist, had covered it** | **Same** |

`C-500` is the clearest case. The valuation specialist's scope letter at `C-500/4` expressly excludes
contingent consideration. The preparer knew — she recorded it at `C-500` §5.3 procedure 5.10. The manager
did not know because she did not read the scope letter. The partner did not know because he did not read the
workpaper. **The £11.5m Level 3 fair value was, in consequence, audited by nobody, and each of the three
people who might have noticed believed one of the others had.**

### 4.3 Deployment of an inexperienced preparer to a significant risk

A. Brennan-Sutton joined the engagement on 2 February 20X5 and was assigned the business combination — a
significant risk, with a 140-hour budget — as her first task on a client she had not previously seen. She
completed it in 86 hours, finalised it on 5 March, and it entered the archive with her signature and no
other.

**NQMG does not criticise the preparer.** The workpaper is competent on its mechanics, correctly treats the
retention bonuses as post-combination remuneration, identifies a £0.52m deferred tax difference nobody asked
her to look for, and expressly records the specialist scope gap. The failure is in the assignment and in the
absence of any review of it.

## 5. Assessment against ISA 220 (Revised)

| Requirement | Assessment |
|---|---|
| The engagement partner shall take overall responsibility for managing and achieving quality | **Not met.** 0.37% of engagement hours in documented review; 4 of 9 significant-risk workpapers unopened. |
| The engagement partner shall be sufficiently and appropriately involved throughout the engagement | **Partially met.** Involvement in planning, client meetings and the Audit Committee was adequate. Involvement in review was not. |
| The engagement partner shall determine that the nature, timing and extent of direction and supervision of the engagement team is planned and performed in accordance with firm policy | **Not met.** 40 hours of planned supervision were not worked, the partner was informed twice, and no action was taken. |
| The engagement partner shall review engagement documentation at appropriate points | **Not met.** Review was concentrated into 11.5 hours on 10 and 11 March, after the manager's review was complete and the draft accounts were finalised. No interim review of any substantive workpaper took place. |
| The engagement partner shall take responsibility for the nature, timing and extent of consultation | **Not met.** Two mandatory consultations under `AV-TAP-04` were not performed; one consultation was performed and not documented. See `P-400`. |
| The engagement partner shall determine that an engagement quality review has been performed and completed on or before the date of the auditor's report | **Not met.** See `INSP-F01`. |
| The engagement partner shall not date the report until satisfied that sufficient appropriate audit evidence has been obtained | **Not met** in the eight areas set out at `I-300`. |

## 6. Conclusion

### 6.1 As drafted, 12 August 20X5

> *"The engagement partner's direction, supervision and review of this engagement did not comply with
> ISA 220 (Revised) or with firm methodology `AV-AUD-11`. Documented file review of 11.5 hours represents
> 0.37% of engagement hours against a benchmark of 2–3%. Four of nine workpapers addressing significant
> risks were not opened, and each contains a deficiency identified by this inspection. Sixty-eight per cent
> of the partner's review notes posed no question. No sample of the manager's 49 clearances was tested and
> the review notes register was never opened, although it displayed on its face the two matters that were
> subsequently missed.*
>
> *The engagement partner was provided, in writing, thirteen hours before signing, with an accurate account
> of the most serious deficiencies in the file, and did not read it.*
>
> ***This constitutes a significant failure of engagement-level quality management and is the principal
> basis for the overall grading of 3 (significant improvements required).***"*

### 6.2 As issued, 30 September 20X5

> *"The engagement partner's documented file review of 11.5 hours is materially below the firm's benchmark
> of 2–3% of engagement hours and represents a departure from `AV-AUD-11` in respect of four workpapers
> addressing significant risks. The partner's review notes were, in a majority of cases, confirmatory
> rather than challenging. The partner did not examine the basis on which the manager's review notes were
> cleared.*
>
> *We note in mitigation the compression of the review timetable arising from the late receipt of the draft
> financial statements, the engagement partner's concurrent responsibility for three other listed audit
> reports in the same week, and his full and constructive engagement with this inspection including the
> preparation of his own retrospective assessment at `P-200`.*
>
> ***This is recorded as a finding requiring remediation.***"*
