## `INSP-F01` — Engagement quality review completed after the date of the auditor's report

**Standard:** ISQM 2; ISA 220 (Revised); firm methodology `AV-EQR-01` §9
**Issue:** `ISS-09`
**Grade contribution:** Finding (draft and final)

### The facts

| Event | Timestamp |
|---|---|
| EQR sign-off, `D-100`, `D-200`, `D-400`, `D-500` | 20X5-03-11, 19:44 to 20:31 |
| **AUDITOR'S REPORT DATED AND SIGNED** | **20X5-03-12, 11:20** |
| **EQR sign-off, `C-100` (significant risk 1)** | **20X5-03-14, 10:07** |
| **EQR sign-off, `C-600` (significant risk 6)** | **20X5-03-14, 11:22** |
| **EQR sign-off, `C-900` (significant risk 7)** | **20X5-03-14, 12:48** |
| EQR hours recorded on 20X5-03-14 | **5.0** |

### Assessment

ISQM 2 requires that the auditor's report not be dated until the engagement quality reviewer has notified
the engagement partner that the review is complete. `AV-EQR-01` §9 is to the same effect and defines
completion as all procedures performed and all matters raised resolved.

Both individuals gave consistent accounts. The reviewer considered her review substantively complete on
11 March following her discussions with the engagement partner, and regarded the 14 March activity as
recording comments already formed. The engagement partner treated her sign-off of the completion documents
on the evening of 11 March as the notification the policy requires.

**Neither account meets the requirement.** Five hours of recorded time across three workpapers addressing
significant risks is not the time required to type up comments already formed. The reviewer's own timeline at
`D-300` §4 describes the activity as *"Review and sign-off of `C-100`, `C-600` and `C-900`"*.

There is a further consequence. The reviewer's recorded comments identify three matters — the absence of the
look-back analysis, the inaccuracy of the goodwill wording, and the absence of any forecast covering the
required going concern period. **All three are matters the manager had also identified. Had the comments
been on the file on 11 March, they would have corroborated the manager's escalations at a point when
something could have been done.**

### Contributing systemic factor

The engagement management system does not prevent an auditor's report being dated while EQR sign-offs remain
outstanding, and produces no report showing an engagement partner which sign-offs are missing. The
engagement partner's evidence was: *"In four years on this engagement I have never checked that, and I do not
believe I have ever been asked whether I check it."*

**NQMG tested this across the cycle 20X5 sample.** In **3 of the 7 engagements inspected**, at least one EQR
sign-off post-dated the auditor's report. This is not an ARG-specific matter and it is carried into the
firm-wide findings at `I-700` action 1.

## `INSP-F02` — Misstatement identified in a sample was not projected to the population

**Standard:** ISA 530; ISA 450; firm methodology `AV-AUD-22` §4.6
**Issue:** `ISS-02`
**Grade contribution:** Finding (draft and final)

### The facts

| | |
|---|---:|
| Population: despatches 15 Dec 20X4 – 15 Jan 20X5 | 2,317 items, **£312.4m** |
| Sample tested (monetary unit sampling) | 40 items, **£84.6m** (27.1% by value) |
| **Errors identified** | **3 items, £1.90m** |
| Nature | Goods despatched 2–5 January 20X5, revenue recorded December 20X4 |
| Amount included in the evaluation of misstatements at `D-100` | **£1.90m — the factual amount only** |
| **Projection performed** | **NONE** |

### The requirement and the trigger

`AV-AUD-22` §4.6: *"Where a misstatement is identified in a sample, the auditor shall project the
misstatement to the population from which the sample was drawn, unless the misstatement is demonstrably
anomalous. The projected misstatement, not the misstatement found, is the amount to be included in the
evaluation of misstatements."*

`C-200` §6 records the engagement team's own evaluation of the sample results. Against the consideration
*"Are the misstatements identified representative of the population, or anomalous?"* the workpaper records:

> *"The three errors arise at three different sites from a common operational practice. They are **not
> anomalous**."*

**The workpaper reached the conclusion that triggers the mandatory projection requirement, in terms, and
then did not apply it.** The reason recorded at `C-200` §6.1 is that *"the errors are factual, not estimated:
we have identified the specific invoices concerned"*, and the manager's contemporaneous advice, recorded in
the same section, was that projection *"would double-count"*.

That reasoning is incorrect. The identified errors are the errors within the tested 27.1% of the population.
Projection estimates misstatement in the untested 72.9%. The team's own workpaper `C-400` §3.2 correctly
extrapolates inventory test-count differences across a counted population, so the technique was understood
elsewhere in the same file by the same preparer.

### Quantification

**NQMG re-projection, `I-100/15`:**

| Method | Calculation | Projected misstatement |
|---|---|---:|
| **Ratio projection** | £1.90m ÷ £84.6m × £312.4m | **£7.02m** |
| Difference projection (mean-per-unit) | (£1.90m ÷ 40) × 2,317 | £11.01m |
| **Projection adopted for this finding** | Ratio (the more conservative) | **£7.0m** |

### Effect on the evaluation of misstatements

| `D-100` item | As recorded | With projection |
|---|---:|---:|
| U1 — revenue cut-off | 1.90 | **7.00** |
| U2 — ERP capitalisation | 0.28 | 0.28 |
| U3 — bonus accrual | 0.19 | 0.19 |
| **Total uncorrected** | **2.37** | **7.47** |
| Group materiality | 3.10 | 3.10 |
| **As % of materiality** | **76.5%** | **241.0%** |
| As % of profit before tax | 3.80% | **11.97%** |

### Assessment

Sufficient appropriate audit evidence was not obtained that point-in-time revenue was recorded in the correct
period. The evaluation of misstatements at `D-100` was performed on a figure that understates the estimated
misstatement in the population by £5.1m.

### Scope boundary

*NQMG's terms of reference (`I-100` §4.4) do not extend to a conclusion on whether the financial statements
were materially misstated or whether the audit opinion was appropriate. This finding records the projected
misstatement and its effect on the `D-100` evaluation. It does not reach a conclusion on the opinion, and no
referral to the Risk and Quality Committee has been made.*

*(The FRAOA takes the further step at `R-400` `FRAOA-F02` and treats the fact that NQMG did not take it as a
finding about the firm's monitoring process: `R-500` §4.)*

## `INSP-F07` → `INSP-IP12` — Going concern: the downside case covenant breach

**Draft classification: FINDING. Final classification: improvement point.**
**Issue:** `ISS-07`

### The facts

NQMG obtained management's going concern model `ARG GC Model FY20X4 v11.xlsx` from the audit file at
`C-900/3` and examined the workbook in full, including hidden columns.

| Tab | Content |
|---|---|
| `GC_Summary` | The tab reproduced in the Board paper and relied on at `C-900` §3.3. Presents downside leverage at **two** test dates only: 31 Mar 20X5 (2.79×) and 31 Dec 20X5 (2.91×). Both compliant. |
| `Scenario_DS` | The full downside case. Quarterly covenant calculation in **columns AH to AK**. |
| **Columns AJ and AK** | **Hidden in the version provided to the audit team and filed.** |

| Test date | Column | Downside leverage | Covenant | Visible in the version filed? |
|---|---|---:|---:|:-:|
| 31 Mar 20X5 | AH | 2.79× | ≤3.00× | ✔ |
| 30 Jun 20X5 | AI | 2.96× | ≤3.00× | ✔ |
| **30 Sep 20X5** | **AK** | **3.08× — BREACH** | ≤3.00× | **✖ HIDDEN** |
| 31 Dec 20X5 | AJ | 2.91× | ≤3.00× | **✖ HIDDEN** |

**The two test dates presented on the summary tab are the two least stressed of the four. The most stressed
test date shows a covenant breach and is in a hidden column.**

### Audit work performed

| Procedure | Performed |
|---|:-:|
| Base case recalculated at all four quarterly test dates | ✔ |
| **Downside case recalculated at all four quarterly test dates** | **✖** |
| Downside case reviewed | Via the summary tab only |
| **The workbook unhidden or examined beyond the summary tab** | **✖** |
| Break-even sensitivity performed | ✔ — **on annual figures**, at `C-900/6` |
| Manager's note asking for the break-even at each quarterly test date (`MRN-033`) | **Answered with the summary tab again. Self-cleared by the preparer.** |

### Assessment

NQMG makes no finding that the columns were hidden in order to conceal the breach. Hidden columns are common
in working models and the workbook contains other hidden columns of no significance. Management's Board
paper presented the same two dates.

**The finding is against the audit team, not against management.** The covenant is tested quarterly; the
audit procedure recalculated one scenario at four dates and the other at none. `MRN-033` asked precisely the
right question and was closed with the same summary tab. **Had the note been answered as asked, the 3.08×
breach would have surfaced on 8 March 20X5.**

The consequence is that note 2 of the financial statements states that *"in the downside scenario the Group
continues to operate within its covenants throughout the assessment period"*, and the auditor reported on
that statement without having tested it.

### As drafted, 12 August 20X5

> *"Management's own severe but plausible downside case shows a breach of the leverage covenant at the
> 30 September 20X5 test date. The engagement team did not recalculate the downside case at the quarterly
> test dates and relied on a summary presenting two of the four dates, being the two least stressed. A
> specific review note requesting the quarterly calculation was closed with the same summary. The financial
> statements assert covenant compliance throughout the assessment period in the downside scenario and that
> assertion was not tested.*
>
> ***This is a failure to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence over a significant risk and to
> evaluate the adequacy of a disclosure the auditor reported upon. This is a finding.***"*

### Final classification and rationale (`I-500` §4.5)

Downgraded to improvement point `INSP-IP12`.

Rationale recorded: *"The breach arises in a downside scenario at a single test date, by 0.08×, and the
scenario did not eventuate. The Group remained compliant at every actual test date during 20X5. The
engagement team's break-even analysis at `C-900/6`, though performed on annual figures, indicated headroom
against a 13% EBITDA shortfall. The procedural shortcoming in not recalculating the downside case at each
test date is an area for improvement."*

*(The Group's actual leverage at 30 September 20X5 was 2.88×, within the covenant. `R-400` `FRAOA-F07`
addresses the use of an outcome that did not eventuate as a basis for downgrading a finding about evidence
that was not obtained. FRAOA position: "The purpose of a downside case is to test what happens if the
downside occurs. That it did not occur is not a reason for not having audited it.")*

**Quantification (NQMG):** at an 85% probability the liability would be approximately **£15.9m**, an
understatement of **£4.4m**.

**`AV-TAP-04`(d)** requires mandatory consultation on any Level 3 fair value exceeding group materiality.

## `INSP-IP03` — IT general controls: conclusion unsupported by the workpaper's own findings · `ISS-04`

`B-200` identifies eight deficiencies in the Aventine environment, four assessed by the preparer as
significant:

| Deficiency | Detail |
|---|---|
| Access | **14 users with access exceeding their role definition; 2 finance users can create AND approve journals and amend vendor master data AND release payments** |
| Access | **Privileged access log never reviewed since go-live** |
| Access | Shared `AVN_SUPPORT` account used for 1,247 transactions, of which 34 general ledger journals |
| Change | **47 of 312 changes without documented approval; 2 altered posting logic** |

`B-200` §8.2 then concludes that **reliance on IT general controls remains appropriate for the full year**,
on the grounds that the deficiencies concern administration rather than transaction processing and that no
misstatement has been identified as resulting from them.

**The reasoning inverts the burden.** The question is not whether a misstatement caused by the deficiency was
found; it is whether the control environment provides a basis for reliance. Reliance was placed in two
cycles (`A-700` §4) on the strength of this conclusion.

**Review received:** manager — none, workpaper not opened. Partner — **20 minutes, no notes**. EQR — not
reviewed. **This is the least-reviewed workpaper in the file and it addresses a pervasive significant risk.**

**Related:** `C-700` §5.2 identifies four journals posted and approved by the same individual — the same two
users identified at `B-200` §4.1 — and concludes that the self-approval "arises from the access
configuration deficiency and not from an attempt to circumvent a control", without considering that an
individual able to create a vendor, release a payment to it and post the entry has no compensating control
above them.

**Firm-wide:** NQMG tested ITGC conclusions across the cycle 20X5 sample. **In 4 of the 7 engagements
inspected**, controls reliance was retained in the presence of unremediated significant ITGC deficiencies.
Carried to `I-700` action 3.

*Classification in both draft and final: improvement point. **FRAOA classification: finding `FRAOA-F09`,
and a firm-wide systemic conclusion at `R-500` §6.***

## `INSP-IP04` — Related parties: Larkspur Freight Ltd · `ISS-08`

| | |
|---|---:|
| Payments in FY20X4 | **£2.40m** |
| Payments in FY20X3 | nil |
| **Specific materiality for related party transactions** | **£0.100m** |
| **Multiple** | **24×** |
| Counterparty | Larkspur Freight Ltd |
| **Sole director and shareholder** | **G. Thwaite-Marchant, brother-in-law of the Chief Financial Officer** |
| Competitive tender on the file | **None** — a single-source justification form |
| Rates against two comparable hauliers on the approved list | **4–7% higher** |
| Payments outside the standard payment run | **£680k, three payments, no documented exception approval** |
| **Disclosed in the FY20X4 related party note** | **NO** |

**How it was found.** A first-year assistant performed a companies registry search on new vendors above
£250k, cross-referred the registered office to the CFO's own related party questionnaire, and identified the
relationship. Nobody asked him to.

**What the CFO's questionnaire says.** Question 6 asks whether the respondent is aware of any transaction
between the Group and any entity in which they or a close family member has a material interest. The CFO
answered **"No"**.

**What was done.** A telephone discussion with the CFO. Her characterisation accepted.

**What was not done.** No IAS 24 analysis — `C-800` §3.7 lists five questions and records that none was
addressed. No independent corroboration of arm's length terms. No inquiry of the Head of Logistics who
sourced the supplier and authorised two of the three manual payments, and who **is not a recipient of the
related party questionnaire**. No inquiry of any other director or of the Audit Committee. No reassessment of
the fraud risk assessment. No ISA 250 consideration of Companies Act s.412. No escalation to the engagement
partner.

**The written conclusion.** `C-800` §9 states the supplier was *"engaged on arm's length terms following
competitive tender"*. There is no tender. The assistant recorded that in writing on the workpaper. The
conclusion was not amended.

**The consequence.** In private session on 11 March 20X5 the Audit Committee Chair asked the engagement
partner whether the audit team had encountered any matter concerning the Chief Financial Officer that the
Committee should know about. He answered that there was not.

*Classification in both draft and final: improvement point.*

> **NQMG's recorded rationale for treating this as an improvement point rather than a finding:**
>
> *"The amount of £2.4m is below Group materiality of £3.10m. The relationship is by marriage rather than
> consanguinity and the Chief Financial Officer holds no interest in the counterparty. The engagement team
> identified the relationship, discussed it with the Chief Financial Officer and reached a conclusion. The
> deficiency is in the adequacy of the documentation of that conclusion and in the failure to escalate."*

> ### ⚠ Three problems with that rationale
>
> **First**, `A-300` §6 sets a specific materiality of £0.100m for related party transactions precisely
> because the significance of such transactions is qualitative rather than quantitative. Applying Group
> materiality of £3.10m to a related party matter defeats the purpose of having set a specific materiality,
> and the inspection's own report does not mention that £0.100m exists.
>
> **Second**, "the relationship is by marriage rather than consanguinity" is a legal proposition about the
> scope of IAS 24, stated without analysis, in a report criticising the engagement team for stating the same
> proposition without analysis.
>
> **Third**, the CFO answered "No" to a direct question on a signed questionnaire. Whether that answer was
> correct is the whole issue, and neither the audit file nor the inspection report addresses it.
>
> **FRAOA classification: finding `FRAOA-F10`, and a separate finding `FRAOA-F12` that the inspection did
> not consider whether the matter required reporting to the firm's General Counsel or externally.**

## `INSP-IP05` — Review note timing discrepancy

Four review notes (`MRN-046` to `MRN-049`, the inventory sequence) carry manuscript dates of 24–27 February
20X5 while the system metadata records all four as created and closed on 9 March 20X5.

The manager's account is that the inventory review was performed at the Kirkwall Road depot on 24–26
February, that she recorded the notes on a personal worksheet, and that she migrated them into the system on
9 March, at which point the system allocated the next available sequence numbers. The worksheet is on the
file at `M-200/4`.

**NQMG accepts the account.** The underlying substantive work carries contemporaneous preparer dates of 25
and 26 February, and the £1.24m adjustment was demonstrably agreed with management on 27 February.

**Improvement point:** review notes should be recorded in the system contemporaneously. The firm's system
does not support offline recording, which is the underlying cause. Carried to `I-700` action 6.

*(This is the only matter in the report where the inspection tested a documentation timing discrepancy
against the system metadata. Had the same test been applied to `C-100/13`, the post-assembly addition at

## `INSP-IP08` — Group scoping coverage

Full-scope coverage of 71% of revenue and 68% of profit before tax, against the 75% the firm's methodology
describes as typical for a listed group. ARG Aftermarket Inc (15% of revenue, 16% of PBT) received analytical
procedures only.

load-bearing assumption: that the group team's cut-off testing covers the Delrado population. **The manager

sample error was not projected. **The single procedure on which the scoping of a 15%-of-revenue component
depended is the procedure whose results were not properly evaluated.**
