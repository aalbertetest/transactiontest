## 1. Mandate

The National Quality & Monitoring Group ("NQMG") operates the firm's engagement inspection programme
under ISQM 1, which requires the firm to design and implement a monitoring process that provides
relevant, reliable and timely information about the design, implementation and operation of its system
of quality management, and to evaluate, communicate and remediate identified deficiencies.

NQMG reports to the Head of Quality and, quarterly, to the firm's Risk and Quality Committee.

### 3.3 How the declaration came to be given

*This subsection was not in the inspection file as issued on 30 September 20X5. It was added on
20X6-03-24 following the FRAOA's inquiry of 17 March 20X6.*

The independence declaration is completed in the NQMG workflow system. It asks:

> *"Have you served as engagement partner, engagement quality reviewer, or in any other capacity requiring
> you to be recorded as a member of the engagement team, on this engagement within the preceding five
> years?"*

Mr Ferreira-Osei answered **"No"**.

His account, given to the FRAOA on 17 March 20X6:

> *"I answered the question I was asked. I was not the engagement partner and I was not the EQR. On the third
> limb, I read 'recorded as a member of the engagement team' as referring to the current engagement team
> listing, and I was not on it.*
>
> *That is not a good answer and I am not going to pretend it is. I knew I had been the senior manager on
> Auburn Ridge for two years. I knew Ronan Belliveau, because I worked for him. **I should have flagged it and
> asked to be taken off, and the reason I did not is that NQMG had four people covering seven inspections and
> I was the only director available for a listed group of that size.***
>
> *Nobody checked. The system does not cross-reference the declaration against the firm's own engagement team
> records, which contain my name against Auburn Ridge for FY20X0 to FY20X2. It would be a single query."*

### 3.4 Whether the conflict affected the inspection

The FRAOA examined this question at `R-200` §3 and reached a conclusion. NQMG's own position, recorded on
20X6-03-24, is:

> *"The inspection identified fourteen findings in draft, including every substantive audit failure
> subsequently identified by the FRAOA's own file review at `R-300`, with two exceptions
> (the post-assembly modification of `C-100/13`, and the connection between the delay claim and the cost
> estimate). We do not accept that the lead inspector's prior involvement caused findings to be missed.*
>
> *We accept that it created a threat to objectivity which was neither declared nor safeguarded, and that
> the threat is most acute in relation to the grading decision at `I-500`, where the lead inspector met the
> engagement partner without a minute-taker on 9 September 20X5 and four findings were downgraded
> thereafter."*

### 4.3 The documentation scope limitation

The inspection reviewed the audit file **in the state in which it stood in July 20X5**.

`D-600` §4 records nine modifications made to the file after it was locked on 12 May 20X5, none of them
authorised as `AV-AUD-19` requires. The most significant is the addition on 19 May 20X5 of `C-100/13`, a
two-page look-back analysis of prior year contract cost estimates that was **not on the file when the
opinion was signed**.

**The inspection did not identify that `C-100/13` was a post-assembly addition.**

The consequence appears at `I-300` finding `INSP-F05`, which addresses the CN-4471 estimate and records
that *"a look-back analysis is present at `C-100/13`, though it is high-level and does not address the
specific contract"*. That finding is drafted on the assumption that the analysis was available to the
engagement team in March 20X5. It was not.

*(NQMG's practice is to review the current archived file and to inspect the modification log only where a
finding turns on documentation timing. The log at `D-600/2` was opened but not analysed. The FRAOA obtained
the full version history in February 20X6 and addresses the matter at `R-300` §6 and `R-400` finding
`FRAOA-F13`.)*

### 4.4 What the inspection did not ask

The inspection assesses **compliance with standards**. It does not reach a conclusion on whether the
financial statements were materially misstated or whether the audit opinion was appropriate.

| Question | In scope? |
|---|---|
| Was sufficient appropriate audit evidence obtained? | **Yes** |
| Were professional standards complied with? | **Yes** |
| **Were the financial statements materially misstated?** | **No** |
| **Was the audit opinion appropriate?** | **No** |
| **Should the firm consider whether the opinion needs to be withdrawn or the accounts revisited?** | **No** |

NQMG's terms of reference state: *"The programme assesses compliance with professional standards and firm
methodology. Conclusions on the appropriateness of an audit opinion are a matter for the engagement partner
and, where necessary, for the firm's Risk and Quality Committee on referral."*

**No referral to the Risk and Quality Committee was made in respect of this engagement.**

> This scope boundary is the most consequential feature of Level 4, and it is the reason Level 5 exists.
>
> `INSP-F02` finds that the cut-off sample error was not projected, and states that projection gives £7.0m.
> It does not go on to state that substituting £7.0m into `D-100` produces uncorrected misstatements of
> £7.47m against materiality of £3.10m, or ask what that means for an unmodified opinion. The arithmetic is
> two steps from a finding the inspection made, and the terms of reference stop at step one.
>
> The FRAOA takes the remaining steps at `R-400` finding `FRAOA-F02` and treats the omission as a finding
> about the firm's monitoring process rather than about the engagement: `R-500` §4.

## 5. Grading framework

| Grade | Definition |
|---|---|
| **1** | Compliant. No findings requiring action. |
| **2** | Compliant with limited improvements required. |
| **3** | **Significant improvements required. Sufficient appropriate audit evidence was not obtained in one or more material areas, or there was a significant failure to comply with firm methodology.** |
| **4** | Non-compliant. The audit opinion was not supported. |

Finding categories:

| Category | Definition | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Finding (`INSP-F`)** | A failure to comply with professional standards or firm methodology in a material respect | Contributes to the grade; requires a remediation action |
| **Improvement point (`INSP-IP`)** | A matter where practice could be improved but no material non-compliance is identified | Does not contribute to the grade |

`AV-QM-07`: *"Grading changes between draft and final inspection reports shall be documented with a written
rationale approved by the Head of Quality."*

## 8. Conclusion on scope

The inspection was performed in accordance with the NQMG methodology, subject to:

| Limitation | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Inspector independence** | The lead inspector served on the engagement 2 years 4 months before the inspection, against a 5-year requirement. Not declared, not safeguarded, not detected. |
| **Selection of the sample** | One of seven engagements from a tier representing 18.2% of the portfolio. |
| **Documentation timing not analysed** | The post-assembly addition of `C-100/13` was not identified, and one finding is drafted on the assumption that a document was available in March 20X5 when it was added in May. |
| **Terms of reference exclude the opinion** | The inspection quantified a projected misstatement of £7.0m and did not carry it into the misstatement evaluation or consider its consequence for the opinion. |
| **No referral to the Risk and Quality Committee** | Notwithstanding findings of insufficient audit evidence on matters exceeding materiality. |
| **One preparer not interviewed** | The preparer of the unreviewed significant-risk workpaper containing `ISS-05`. |
| **No second interview after the response** | Two findings were contested on points of fact and resolved at a meeting of which no minute exists. |
