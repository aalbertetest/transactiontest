## 1. Overall position

I accept the substance of this report.

Of the six findings, **I accept four without qualification**: `INSP-F01`, `INSP-F02`, `INSP-F03` and
`INSP-F06`. I accept the eight improvement points. I contest two findings, `INSP-F05` and `INSP-F07`, on
points of fact set out at §5 and §6. I ask that one finding, `INSP-F04`, be reallocated rather than
withdrawn, for the reasons at §3.

**I do not ask for the grade to be changed.** I have read `AV-QM-06` and I can see that `INSP-F02` alone
engages it. If the outcome of this inspection is grade 3, that is the correct outcome and I would not want
it recorded that I argued otherwise.

I have also prepared, unprompted, a note-by-note assessment of the manager's forty-nine review clearances,
which is the exercise I should have performed in March and did not. It is at `P-200`. It identifies six
clearances I regard as improperly made. **All six are the six the manager identified herself before I
signed.** I offer it because it is the most useful thing I can add and because it is more critical of my own
review than the draft report is.

## 2. What I am not going to say

Before the detail, three things I want on the record so that they are not mistaken for arguments later.

**I am not going to blame the timetable.** The draft financial statements arrived six days late and my review
was compressed from three days to two. That is true and it is in the file at `A-700` §3. It explains why I
had eleven and a half hours. It does not explain what I did with them. The three things I most needed to do
— open the review notes register, read a memorandum addressed to me, and write down a telephone consultation
— would have taken under an hour between them, and I had eleven and a half.

**I am not going to blame the manager.** See §3.

**I am not going to rely on the fact that the accounts were probably right.** As far as anyone knows the
FY20X4 accounts were not materially misstated in aggregate, other than possibly in respect of `INSP-F02` and
`INSP-IP02`. That is a fact and it is not a defence. On 11 March I signed a confirmation that sufficient
appropriate audit evidence had been obtained. Whether the answer turned out to be right is a different
question from whether I had the evidence, and I did not.

## 3. `INSP-F04` — the manager finding — I ask that it be reallocated

This is the only part of the report I feel strongly about.

Every fact in `INSP-F04` is correct. Eleven notes were closed by preparers in breach of `AV-AUD-14`. Nineteen
were closed on explanation alone. Two significant-risk workpapers were not opened. The `C-800` conclusion
refers to a competitive tender that does not exist. Larkspur was not escalated to me.

**Now consider where the inspection team got those facts.**

| Fact in `INSP-F04` | Where NQMG found it |
|---|---|
| Six clearances inadequate | **`M-100` §6 — the manager's own review record, dated 11 March 20X5, listing them** |
| The projection advice was wrong and the number is materially larger | **`M-300` §2 — her memorandum to me, dated 11 March 20X5** |
| She had not reviewed 55% of the file including two significant-risk workpapers | **`M-100` §4.2 and §4.3 — hers** |
| Larkspur was not escalated and should have been | **`M-300` §2 — hers. Her words: "It should have gone to RB and it should have gone in `D-400`."** |
| The `C-800` conclusion says "competitive tender" and there is no tender | **`M-100` §6.4 — hers, and the assistant's note on the workpaper** |
| 118 hours against a budget of 158 | **`A-700` §6, `D-200` §9.3, `M-100` §3.1 — all hers** |

**The inspection's finding against the manager is constructed almost entirely from documents the manager
wrote, voluntarily, criticising herself, before the report was signed.** `M-100`, `M-300` and `M-400` are not
required by the methodology. The standard manager review record is a one-page coverage sign-off.

Three further facts.

**First**, the forty missing hours. `A-100` §4.3, which I approved in September 20X4, added forty hours to her
budget specifically to supervise a first-year senior in charge. She worked 118 against 158. The gap is the
forty hours. She was carrying three December year-ends and recorded 214 hours across them in the fortnight to
11 March. **She told me on 26 February and again on 6 March. I did nothing.**

**Second**, `INSP-F04` criticises her for not escalating Larkspur. She escalated five other matters to me at
`M-500`, all of which reached me and all of which I responded to. She asked me three times for the going
concern consultation memorandum and I never provided it. **The escalation channel worked in every direction
except the one that depended on me.**

**Third**, and I think this is the point: **she told me about Larkspur at 21:15 on 11 March, and I opened the
document for four minutes and did not read it.** The inspection's finding is that she did not escalate. The
more accurate finding is that she escalated late and I did not receive it. Of those two failures, the second
is mine.

**What I ask.** That `INSP-F04` be reallocated into `INSP-F03`, where it belongs, and that the report record
that the manager's contemporaneous documentation was the source of most of the inspection's own findings. Not
because she performed well — she says herself that she did not — but because **a firm that puts a finding
against the person who wrote down her own failure, and softens the finding against the person who did not
read it, will teach every manager in the firm the wrong lesson.**

I would rather carry both findings myself than have that on the file.

## 4. `INSP-F02` — the projection — accepted without qualification

I accept this finding entirely and I want to add to it.

The projection was not performed. `AV-AUD-22` §4.6 makes it mandatory once the errors are found not to be
anomalous, and `C-200` §6 records in terms that they were **not anomalous**. The workpaper reached the
conclusion that triggers the requirement and then did not apply it.

**Two things the draft report does not say, which I think it should.**

**First**, the same preparer extrapolated a sample error correctly, unprompted, elsewhere in the same file.
`C-400` §3.2 extrapolates inventory count differences across the counted population to conclude £41k. She
applied the right technique in February and asked whether to apply it in March and was told not to. This is
not a competence failure at assistant grade.

**Second, and this is the part I would want any reader of this response to sit with**: the draft report
quantifies the projected misstatement at £7.0m and stops. Substituting it into `D-100` gives uncorrected
misstatements of **£7.47m against materiality of £3.10m — 2.4 times materiality.**

I understand that `I-100` §4.4 places the appropriateness of the opinion outside NQMG's terms of reference.
**But somebody in this firm needs to look at that number.** I have raised it in a separate note to the Head
of Quality dated 29 August 20X5 asking that the matter be referred to the Risk and Quality Committee for
consideration of whether anything further is required in respect of the FY20X4 opinion.

*(No referral was made. The Head of Quality's response of 3 September 20X5 records: "Noted. The engagement is
graded and the remedial actions will address the methodology point. I do not consider a referral necessary."
`R-400` `FRAOA-F02`.)*

## 6. `INSP-F07` — the downside covenant breach — I contest the characterisation

I accept that the downside case was not recalculated at each quarterly test date, that `MRN-033` asked for
exactly that and was closed with the same summary tab, and that this was a procedural failure.

**What I contest is the implication that the 3.08× breach was available to the engagement team and
overlooked.** The figure is in column AK of the `Scenario_DS` tab, which was **hidden in the version of the
workbook management provided**. The summary tab that management put in its own Board paper presents the same
two test dates the team reviewed. The team reviewed what it was given.

I accept that we should have unhidden the workbook, and that a team auditing a quarterly covenant should
recalculate every scenario at every test date. But "the team relied on a summary" and "the team overlooked a
covenant breach on the file" are different findings, and the second is not accurate.

*(NQMG's response, `I-500/3`: "Accepted as to the hidden column and NQMG's finding makes no allegation of
concealment by management or of the figure being overlooked by the team. The finding is that the audit
procedure did not test the assertion the financial statements make. That is unaffected by which columns were
visible." The finding was nonetheless downgraded to an improvement point on a different basis — that the
downside did not eventuate. `I-500` §4.5.)*

## 7. `INSP-F03` — my own review — accepted in full

I do not contest this finding and I do not want it softened.

The numbers are right: 11.5 hours of documented file review, 0.37% of engagement hours against a benchmark of
2–3%, 312 of 4,127 workpapers, five of nine significant-risk workpapers, thirteen of nineteen notes posing no
question, and no test of any of the manager's forty-nine clearances.

**What I would add is that I do not think the hours are the finding.**

I have thought about this for two weeks. More hours spent the way I spent them would have produced the same
conclusions, because what I read was a set of accurate summaries prepared by the person whose review I was
relying on. A summary can tell you what someone concluded. It cannot tell you what they did not look at.

**The finding is that I never asked what had not been looked at.** Three things would have answered it:

| | Time required |
|---|---|
| Opening the review notes register — which would have shown a same-day-cleared note on `C-800`, zero notes on two significant-risk workpapers, and three going concern notes none of which produced evidence | **~20 minutes** |
| Reading `M-300`, addressed to me, which told me the projection advice was wrong and told me about Larkspur | **~15 minutes** |
| Writing down the Technical Panel consultation, which the manager asked me for twice and the EQR once | **~20 minutes** |

**Under an hour, out of eleven and a half.** Each of the three was requested of me, directly, by somebody
below me in the review chain.

I mention this because the remedial actions in the draft at §9 are about hours and coverage percentages, and I
do not believe hours and coverage percentages are what failed. **A partner who reviews only what the level
below him chooses to show him will fail at any number of hours.** If the firm's response to this inspection is
to require partners to record more review time, this will happen again and the file will look better while it
does.

## 8. The improvement points

Accepted, with two observations.

**`INSP-IP01`, the contingent consideration.** I want to be clear how this happened, because I think it is the
most instructive item in the report. Three people each drew a reasonable inference. The preparer read the
specialist's scope letter, saw that contingent consideration was excluded, and wrote it down at `C-500` §5.3.
The manager did not read the scope letter and inferred from the specialist's engagement that the acquisition
had been covered. I did not read the workpaper and inferred the same. **An £11.5m Level 3 fair value went
unaudited because a specialist's deliverable was recorded in the file and her scope was not.**

The firm's system records that a specialist was engaged and that a memorandum was received. It does not
record what she was asked to do. That is a methodology point and it will recur.

**`INSP-IP04`, Larkspur.** I accept the improvement point and I think it understates the matter.

I would draw attention to something the draft does not mention. `A-300` §6 sets a **specific materiality of
£0.100m** for related party transactions, precisely because their significance is qualitative. £2.4m is
twenty-four times that. **The draft report assesses the matter against Group materiality of £3.10m and does
not mention that the specific materiality exists.** If the firm's inspection function applies Group
materiality to a related party finding, the specific materiality we set in September serves no purpose.

I would also record that the Audit Committee Chair asked me a direct question in private session on 11 March
and I gave her an answer that was accurate on what I knew and inaccurate on what the firm knew. I have not
been able to think of a way to describe that which makes it acceptable.

## 9. On the remedial actions proposed

Two comments.

The proposed actions are largely about measurement — minimum partner review hours, coverage thresholds,
enhanced sign-off. I have given my view at §7 on why I think that will not work.

**What I would propose instead**, for whatever it is worth from the person who caused the finding:

1. **Make the review notes register a mandatory partner review step with a sign-off of its own.** Not more
   hours. One specific document, twenty minutes, with a field the partner has to complete: how many notes were
   cleared on explanation alone, how many were self-cleared, and which significant-risk workpapers carry no
   notes.
2. **Record specialist scope, not just specialist deliverables**, and require the engagement team to reconcile
   the agreed scope to the assertions relied upon.
3. **Block the system from dating a report while any EQR sign-off is outstanding.** `I-400` §7 records that
   this was identified in cycle 20X3 and again in cycle 20X4 and has been deferred twice. It is the direct
   cause of one of the two findings this report retains.
4. **Reconcile the mandatory consultation checklist to the file automatically.** Both `AV-TAP-04` failures on
   this engagement come from two checklist answers that are wrong on the face of workpapers in the same file.
   That is a query, not a training issue.
