## 3. The five-why analysis on the three most significant deficiencies

### 3.1 `INSP-F02` — the cut-off sample error was not projected

| Level | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why was the misstatement not projected? | Because the manager advised the preparer that projection would double-count the errors already identified. |
| 2 | Why did the manager give that advice? | Because she believed that identifying the specific erroneous invoices made projection redundant. |
| 3 | Why did she believe that? | **A technical misunderstanding of what projection measures.** She has 9 years' experience and had extrapolated sample results correctly in other contexts. The same preparer extrapolated inventory count differences correctly at `C-400` §3.2 in the same file. |
| 4 | Why was the error not caught by any later reviewer? | The partner did not open `C-200` or the review notes register. The EQR did not review `C-200`. The manager identified her own error at `M-300` §2 on 11 March and the memorandum was opened for four minutes. |
| 5 | **Why did none of the firm's controls detect it?** | **The methodology at `AV-AUD-22` §4.6 makes projection mandatory but there is no workpaper field, checklist question or system prompt that requires the projection to be evidenced. A sampling workpaper can be completed and signed off with the projection step simply absent, and the file will not show that anything is missing.** |

**Root causes:** RC7 (competence) as the proximate cause; **RC2 and the methodology gap at why-5 as the
systemic causes.**

*Firm-wide test: NQMG examined the evaluation of sample errors across the cycle 20X5 sample. Three of the
seven engagements identified misstatements in a sample. **In 2 of those 3, no projection was documented.***

### 3.2 `INSP-IP01` and `INSP-IP03` — the two unreviewed significant-risk workpapers

| Level | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why were `B-200` and `C-500` not reviewed by anybody? | The manager did not open them; the partner did not open `C-500` and gave `B-200` twenty minutes. |
| 2 | Why did the manager not open them? | `B-200`: she judged ITGC to be outside her technical competence and understood that `AV-AUD-11` reserved significant-risk workpapers to the partner. `C-500`: she understood that the firm's valuation specialist had reviewed the acquisition accounting. |
| 3 | Why did she believe a specialist had covered the acquisition? | **Because she did not read the specialist's scope letter, which expressly excluded the contingent consideration.** The preparer had read it and recorded the exclusion at `C-500` §5.3. |
| 4 | Why did the partner not open `C-500`? | For the same reason. His evidence: *"I understood our own valuation specialist had reviewed the acquisition accounting and concluded it was within a reasonable range."* |
| 5 | **Why did the firm's system permit three people to each assume a fourth had performed the work?** | **Because specialist involvement is recorded in the file as a deliverable, not as a scope. The engagement management system records that a specialist was engaged and that a memorandum was received. It does not record what the specialist was and was not asked to do, and no checklist requires the engagement team to reconcile the specialist's agreed scope to the assertions the specialist's work is relied upon to address.** |

**Root cause:** RC3, with the systemic cause at why-5.

> **NQMG observation.** This is the cleanest failure in the file, in the sense that no individual behaved
> unreasonably. The preparer identified the gap and wrote it down. The manager and the partner each drew a
> reasonable inference from the fact that a specialist was engaged. **The £11.5m Level 3 fair value went
> unaudited because three reasonable inferences pointed at each other.**

### 3.3 `INSP-F03` — the extent and quality of the partner's review

| Level | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why was documented partner file review only 11.5 hours? | The review was compressed into 10 and 11 March, and the partner signed three other listed audit reports in the same week. |
| 2 | Why was it compressed into two days? | The draft financial statements arrived on 3 March against a plan of 27 February, and fieldwork over-ran by six days. The external reporting date did not move. |
| 3 | Why did the partner not review earlier, at interim or during fieldwork? | No interim partner review of any substantive workpaper is recorded. The engagement plan at `A-700` §5 does not specify review points; it specifies what must be reviewed, not when. |
| 4 | Why did the partner's review not detect the deficiencies the manager had documented? | **He reviewed documents that were summaries prepared by the manager, and did not open the review notes register, the manager's review record or her conclusion memorandum for more than four minutes each.** |
| 5 | **Why is a partner able to sign a listed audit opinion having reviewed 7.6% of the file and 5 of 9 significant-risk workpapers?** | **Because `AV-AUD-11` specifies *what* the partner must review and the system records *that* he signed, but nothing measures whether he did. The e-file audit trail from which the 11.5 hours and 7.6% coverage in this report were derived exists for every engagement and is examined only when an engagement is selected for inspection — 7 of 88 listed audits per cycle.** |

**Root causes:** RC1 and RC2, with the systemic cause at why-5.

> **NQMG observation.** The data on which finding `INSP-F03` rests was available to the firm in real time,
> on 11 March 20X5, from a standard system report. Nobody looked at it until July.

## 5. Matters considered and not carried forward as root causes

### 5.1 Fee pressure

| | |
|---|---:|
| Audit fee FY20X4 | £1.42m (+10.1%) |
| Budgeted hours | 2,860 |
| Actual hours | **3,140 (+9.8%)** |
| Recovery rate | **82%** (FY20X3: 94%) |
| Engagement partner's practice unit position against its FY20X5 profitability target at the date of file completion | **6% behind** |

**NQMG assessment.** The engagement over-ran its budget by 9.8% and recovered 82% of standard rates,
which indicates that the team was permitted to spend more time than was priced. The deficiencies are
concentrated in *review* rather than in *fieldwork*: assistant hours exceeded budget by 129 while manager
hours fell short by 40 and partner review hours by more than 50.

**A team constrained by fee pressure would show under-runs across all grades. This engagement shows an
over-run at junior grades and an under-run at senior grades**, which is the signature of a supervision and
availability problem rather than a cost problem.

**Assessed as low weight. Not carried forward as a root cause.**

*(The FRAOA disagrees, on the ground that the analysis examines the engagement's own economics and not the
partner's. `R-500` §5 addresses the partner remuneration framework, the 4% weighting of the quality metric,
and the absence of any consequence to Mr Belliveau's FY20X5 profit share. FRAOA position: "The firm's root
cause analysis asked whether the engagement had enough money. It did not ask what the engagement partner was
paid to optimise.")*

### 5.4 Whether the manager's candour is itself a factor

Considered and recorded, because it is unusual.

The manager produced three documents she was not required to produce (`M-100`, `M-300`, `M-400`), identified
six of her own clearances as inadequate before the report was signed, and told the engagement partner in
writing that her advice on the projection question had been wrong.

**NQMG assessment.** This is a positive feature of the engagement and NQMG records it as such. It is also a
diagnostic fact: **the firm's system of quality management generated accurate, timely, written information
about its own failure, and the information did not reach a decision.** The failure was not in detection. It
was in transmission.

**Carried forward as part of RC2.**

## 8. Conclusion

The deficiencies identified on this engagement arise from seven root causes, of which five are demonstrably
present across the firm's listed audit portfolio and five had been identified in a previous inspection cycle
without effective remediation.

The proximate causes are time compression, under-resourcing at manager grade, and a review culture in which
each level reviewed the conclusions of the level below rather than the review itself. The systemic causes are
methodology and tooling gaps that allow a mandatory step to be omitted without the file showing that
anything is missing, and a monitoring process that examines the necessary data only on the 8% of engagements
it selects.

**The most significant single observation is that the information required to identify the two most serious
deficiencies in this file existed, in writing, in the firm's own systems, on 11 March 20X5.** It was in the
review notes register and in a memorandum addressed to the engagement partner. The failure was not one of
detection but of transmission and of attention.

Remedial actions at `I-700`.
