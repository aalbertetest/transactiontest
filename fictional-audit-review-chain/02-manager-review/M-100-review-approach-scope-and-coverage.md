> **A note on this document.** The firm's standard manager review record (`AV-AUD-14` Appendix 2) is a
> one-page coverage sign-off: sections reviewed, notes raised, notes closed, signature. What follows is
> considerably longer and franker than that template requires, and §6 in particular — a list of the
> reviewer's own inadequate clearances, written before the audit report was signed — has no counterpart in
> the firm's methodology.
>
> That is a fact about this engagement, not an editorial device. It matters at every level above. The
> partner received this memorandum at 21:15 on 11 March 20X5 and opened it for four minutes (`P-100` §7).
> The internal inspection team read it in July 20X5 and cited it in support of a finding against the
> manager (`I-300`, `INSP-F04`). The regulator read it in January 20X6 and reached the opposite
> conclusion: that the firm's system of quality management produced an accurate, timely, written warning
> at Level 2 and that every level above Level 2 failed to act on it (`R-200` §5, `R-500` §3).

## 1. My responsibility

Under firm methodology `AV-AUD-14` and ISA 220 (Revised), as engagement manager I am responsible for:

1. Reviewing all workpapers other than those reserved to the engagement partner, and satisfying myself
   that the work performed supports the conclusions reached
2. Reviewing the work of the senior in charge and, through him, of the assistants
3. Raising review notes on matters requiring further work, further evidence or further explanation, and
   clearing those notes only when satisfied
4. Escalating to the engagement partner any matter I cannot resolve or that requires his judgement
5. Preparing the completion memorandum and the report to the Audit Committee for his approval

Firm policy `AV-AUD-14` also states that **review notes shall be cleared by the reviewer who raised
them, and that self-clearance by the preparer is not permitted.**

## 2. How I approached this review

This is my third year on the engagement and my second as manager. My approach was shaped by three
things.

**The change of senior.** M. Farrow, who ran the file for two years, transferred to Delrado. D.
Ramanathan is new to the role. `A-100` §4.3 records that 40 hours were added to my budget to provide
additional direction and supervision. I planned to spend those hours on the areas where his judgement
would be least tested by experience: contract accounting, going concern and the completion process.

**The migration.** Aventine went live halfway through the year. I expected the IT position to be the
weakest part of the file and I expected to need Y. Demirci's help to interpret it.

**The timetable.** Fieldwork was scheduled to finish on 21 February with the draft financial statements
on 27 February, giving me five working days for review before the partner. Fieldwork actually finished on
27 February and the draft financial statements arrived on 3 March. **I had four working days.**

### 5.1 On the eleven self-cleared notes

`AV-AUD-14` prohibits self-clearance. Eleven notes were closed in the system by D. Ramanathan or by the
assistant who prepared the workpaper, rather than by me.

This happened because of how the system works and how the last week ran. When a preparer responds to a
note, the system offers them a "respond and close" action. In a normal week I would see the response,
check it and close the note myself. In the week to 11 March I was reviewing responses in the system on my
phone between client meetings, and where the response looked adequate I let the preparer's closure stand
rather than reopening the note to close it again myself.

I am recording it plainly because the log shows it and there is no point in the log showing it and the
memorandum not. Six of the eleven concern matters I would characterise as administrative. Five do not.

### 6.1 `MRN-014` — the contract estimate

I asked for a look-back analysis. I did not get one. I cleared the note anyway.

My reasoning was that the prior year file contained a portfolio-level comparison of forecast margin to
outturn, and that this satisfied ISA 540. D. Ramanathan queried this with me on 25 February and I told him
the portfolio comparison covered the point.

It does not. A portfolio aggregate of 6.4% against 6.4% can conceal offsetting errors of any size. The
whole question with CN-4471 is whether *this* contract's estimate is reliable, and a portfolio average
says nothing about that.

I also knew, because I read `B-100`, that ARG's own internal audit function had recommended in April 20X4
that management prepare exactly this analysis, that management had agreed to do it by 31 December 20X4,
and that it was overdue. That is a fact pointing directly at the reliability of the estimation process,
and I had it in front of me.

### 6.2 `MRN-019` — the cut-off projection

C. Nakamura asked me whether the £1.9m of sample errors needed to be extrapolated. I said no, on the
grounds that we had identified the actual errors so projecting them would double-count.

That is a misunderstanding of what projection does. The three errors are the errors *in the sample*. The
sample was 27% of the population by value. Projection estimates the misstatement in the other 73%, which
we did not test. It is not double-counting; it is the whole reason for sampling.

`C-200` §6 records the team's own conclusion that the errors are **"not anomalous"**. Once that is
concluded, `AV-AUD-22` §4.6 requires projection. Ratio projection gives £7.0m. Substituting that into
`D-100` gives uncorrected misstatements of £7.47m against materiality of £3.10m.

I have set this out here because it is the single answer I gave during this review that I would most want
back.

### 6.3 `MRN-031` — the going concern period

I raised the right point. Management assessed 12 months from the balance sheet date; the requirement is 12
months from approval; the RCF matures inside the required period and outside the period used.

The response I accepted was that the CFO would extend the *wording* of the going concern statement, and
that the conclusion was unaffected. I cleared it the same day.

Extending the wording of a statement is not extending the assessment. There is no forecast on the file
covering to 31 March 20X6. There is no modelling of the 31 March 20X6 covenant test. There is no
documentation of the refinancing beyond an oral representation from the Group Treasurer.

I cleared it because RB told me on 10 March that he had spoken to the National Technical Panel and that
they were comfortable. **I never saw a memorandum of that consultation and I did not ask for one.**

### 6.4 `MRN-039` — Larkspur Freight

E. Balewa did good work here. He performed a registry search he was not specifically asked to perform,
identified the sole director, cross-referred the address to the CFO's own related party questionnaire, and
worked out that the counterparty is the CFO's brother-in-law. That is exactly what a first-year assistant
should do and he did it unprompted.

What happened next was mine. I spoke to the CFO, accepted her characterisation, and wrote a five-line
conclusion.

The conclusion I wrote says the supplier was "engaged on arm's length terms following competitive tender".
**There is no competitive tender on the file.** There is a single-source justification form. I wrote
"competitive tender" because that is the phrase the CFO used and I did not check it against the file. E.
Balewa flagged this to me at the time, in writing, on the workpaper, together with his rate comparison
showing Larkspur 4–7% above two comparators. I did not change the conclusion.

I also did not escalate it. £2.4m to an entity controlled by the CFO's brother-in-law is 24 times the
specific materiality we set for related parties precisely because these matters are sensitive
irrespective of amount. It should have gone to RB and it should have gone in `D-400`. RB was asked
directly by the Audit Committee Chair on 11 March, in private session, whether the audit team had
encountered any matter concerning the CFO that the Committee should know about. He said no. **He said no
because I had not told him.**
