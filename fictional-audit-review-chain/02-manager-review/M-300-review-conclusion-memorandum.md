## 2. What I want you to know before you review it

I would normally hand you a file and let you read it. This year I want to be direct about three things,
because if I do not say them here I am relying on you to find them in a file of 4,127 workpapers in two
days.

**First, I have not reviewed all of it.** I opened 1,842 of 4,127 workpapers, 44.6%. Two workpapers I
did not open at all are `B-200` (IT general controls) and `C-500` (business combination). Both address a
significant risk. `AV-AUD-11` reserves significant-risk workpapers to you, so on paper my omission is
covered — but I have always in practice reviewed them first, and this year I did not. Please do not
assume I have looked at them.

**Second, I got one thing wrong.** On `MRN-019` I told C. Nakamura that the £1.90m of cut-off sample
errors did not need extrapolating because we had identified the actual errors. That was wrong. The
sample was 27% of the population by value and the errors are not anomalous — `C-200` §6 says so
expressly. On any projection basis the number is materially larger. I have set this out at `M-100` §6.2.
**I have not corrected `D-100` because I do not think that is my call to make on the day before you
sign, and because doing so changes the misstatement conclusion and therefore possibly the opinion. It is
the first thing I would want you to look at.**

**Third, there is a matter I did not escalate to you and should have.** `MRN-039`, Larkspur Freight Ltd:
£2.4m paid in the year to a haulage company whose sole director and shareholder is the Chief Financial
Officer's brother-in-law, identified by E. Balewa from a registry search he performed on his own
initiative. No competitive tender on the file, rates 4–7% above two comparators, and £680k paid outside
the standard payment run. Our specific materiality for related party transactions is £100k. I concluded
on it myself after a telephone call with the CFO and I did not tell you.

You are seeing the Audit Committee Chair in private session this afternoon. She asked us in November
whether we had concerns about the CFO's approach to estimates. **You need to know about Larkspur before
that meeting.** I am sending this memorandum at 21:15 tonight because I have only just finished it, and I
recognise that is too late to be useful for a meeting that has already happened. I should have told you
on 7 March when I cleared the note.

### 5.1 On the second cross-reference gap

I am flagging this specifically because I noticed it late and did nothing with it.

The Meridian Rail Authority says we are 41 weeks late on gate 3 and 39 weeks late on gate 4 of CN-4471,
and wants £6.8m. In the same quarter, management reduced its estimate of what it will cost to finish the
same contract by £4.1m, and one of the three stated reasons was a reduction in certification scope
arising from the very protocol change that we are relying on as our defence to the delay claim.

I do not know what the answer is. It is possible both things are true: the protocol change did reduce
cost and did cause delay. But we have never asked the question, and a 40-week delay on a retrofit
programme with a fixed depot, a fixed workforce and standing overhead does not usually make a contract
cheaper.

`MRN-043` asked whether the claim made the contract loss-making. It does not. That is not the same
question.
