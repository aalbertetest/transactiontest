## E1 — Goodwill: the discount rate

**Escalated 20X5-03-06, by email at 08:12.**

> *"Ronan — I need your judgement on the Industrial Systems discount rate.*
>
> *Management has used 9.1% post-tax. **Helena Brasseur's independent range for this CGU is 10.2% to
> 11.6%.** Management's rate is not at the bottom of her range; it is below it.*
>
> *The workpaper as drafted said the rate was "at the low end but within a reasonable range". I have made
> them change that wording because it was not accurate.*
>
> *The numbers: headroom is £3.9m on a carrying amount of £186.4m, which is 2.1%. At 10.2% there is an
> impairment of £22.1m. At 9.3% — a 0.2 percentage point move — the headroom is gone.*
>
> *Two further facts. This CGU's FY20X4 operating cash flow came in 18.5% below the forecast made a year
> ago. And Helena's memorandum ends with an offer to discuss this CGU further, which nobody has taken up.*
>
> *My view is that 9.1% is not supportable on the evidence we hold. I recognise that is a judgement and I
> recognise the counter-argument — that her range is built from comparable companies and management's is
> built from ARG's actual borrowing cost. I also accept that the incentive argument does not apply here:
> goodwill impairment is excluded from adjusted EPS and from the covenant EBITDA, so nothing management
> gains on its LTIP or its banking depends on this number.*
>
> *What I would like is (a) your conclusion in your own words rather than a countersignature on ours, and
> (b) twenty minutes of Helena's time. She has offered."*

**Partner's response, 20X5-03-10, 16:40:**

> *"Agreed and noted. Management's derivation from actual cost of borrowing plus a CGU premium is a
> legitimate approach and I am satisfied 9.1% is at the low end of an acceptable range rather than outside
> one. Comparable-company WACC ranges are indicative, not determinative. Content to accept.*
>
> *No time to speak to Helena before tomorrow. Put it on the list for next year."*

**Outcome.** `C-600` §5.3 wording amended to state that the rate falls below the specialist's range.
Management's rate accepted. **Dr Brasseur was not contacted.** Recorded at `D-200` §14 as matter 3 for
FY20X5.

## E2 — Goodwill: the sensitivity disclosure

**Escalated 20X5-03-06, in the same email.**

> *"Related but separate. IAS 36 requires sensitivity disclosure where a reasonably possible change in a
> key assumption would cause an impairment. Headroom of 2.1% and a 0.2 percentage point trigger is
> exactly that case.*
>
> *We asked management for the disclosure on 26 February. It is not in the 3 March draft. I asked again
> today.*
>
> *If they refuse, my view is that this is a disclosure deficiency and we should say so, and that it needs
> to go to the Audit Committee in terms that let the Committee overrule management if it wants to."*

**Partner's response, 20X5-03-10, 16:44:**

> *"Push once more. If they hold, report it to the Committee as a matter on which we do not share
> management's view and leave the Committee to decide. I would not treat it as a misstatement — the
> narrative discloses the rate and the growth assumption, so a reader has what they need."*

**Outcome.** Management refused on 9 March. Reported at `D-400` §5 as a matter on which the auditor does
not share management's view. **The Audit Committee decided by majority that the narrative was sufficient;
the Chair recorded that she would have preferred the quantified disclosure to be made** (`D-400/5`).

*This is the escalation that worked best. The matter was pressed twice with management, escalated, framed
for the Committee in terms that permitted the Committee to disagree with management, and put in front of
the Committee in writing. It did not change the accounts, but the process did what it is supposed to do.*

## E3 — Going concern: the period of assessment

**Escalated 20X5-03-07, by email at 19:30.**

> *"This is the one I am least comfortable with.*
>
> *Management's going concern assessment covers 1 January to 31 December 20X5. They describe it as twelve
> months from the balance sheet date. The requirement is at least twelve months from the date the
> financial statements are approved, which will be 12 March, so the period should run to at least
> 31 March 20X6.*
>
> ***The £220m RCF matures on 27 February 20X6. That is inside the required period and outside the period
> they have used.***
>
> *I raised this at `MRN-031`. What came back was that the CFO will extend the wording of the going
> concern statement and that the conclusion is unaffected.*
>
> *Extending the wording of a statement is not extending the assessment. There is no forecast on the file
> that covers to 31 March 20X6, there is no modelled covenant test at that date, and the statement in note
> 2 will say we have assessed a period we have not assessed.*
>
> *I have cleared the note because I understood you were taking this up. Please confirm what you conclude
> and how you want it documented."*

**Partner's response, 20X5-03-10, 16:51:**

> *"I spoke to the Technical Panel this morning. Their view is that where the additional months contain no
> covenant test that the base case would fail and no maturity the entity cannot meet, the substance is
> addressed and the period point is presentational. I am content on that basis.*
>
> *Make sure note 2 says at least twelve months from approval."*

**Manager's follow-up, 20X5-03-10, 17:22:**

> *"Understood. Can you send me the consultation memorandum for the file? `AV-TAP-01` requires it within
> five business days and before the report date, and the EQR will want to see it."*

**No response to the follow-up is recorded in the engagement management system or in the partner's
correspondence file.**

**Outcome.** `C-900` §7 concludes that no further procedures are necessary. No extended forecast obtained.
**No consultation memorandum was prepared** — see `P-400`. The EQR recorded at `D-300` §5.4 that she had
not seen a written record of the consultation.

## E4 — Going concern: refinancing evidence

**Escalated 20X5-03-07, in the same email.**

> *"On the refinancing itself, what we hold is: no term sheet, no lender credit approval, no written
> indication of support, no correspondence. **The only evidence on the file is a file note of a
> conversation with the Group Treasurer saying the syndicate has indicated informal support and a term
> sheet is expected in Q2.***
>
> *We are relying on a £142m drawn facility being refinanced, and our evidence is an oral representation
> from the company's own treasurer.*
>
> *`MRN-032` asked for anything documentary and the answer was that there is nothing."*

**Partner's response, 20X5-03-10, 16:53:**

> *"The Group is BBB− affirmed in November, has ten years of covenant compliance, £78m undrawn on the
> existing facility and £96m of cash. This is a refinancing that will happen. I am not going to hold up an
> opinion for a term sheet on a facility that matures in fourteen months."*

**Outcome.** `C-900` §6 concludes that refinancing risk does not give rise to a material uncertainty.

*The indicative term sheet was received on 22 April 20X5, six weeks after the report date. The partner's
commercial judgement that the refinancing would happen was, in the event, correct. `R-400` finding
`FRAOA-F08` addresses whether being right about an outcome is the same as having had audit evidence for
it.*

asked the engagement partner directly whether the audit team had encountered any matter concerning the

*The internal inspection team treats this as a finding against the manager (`I-300`, `INSP-F04`). The
regulator treats it as a finding about the firm — specifically, that an escalation route which depends on
a single individual's discretion, exercised in the last week of a compressed timetable, is not a control
(`R-500` §3.4).*
