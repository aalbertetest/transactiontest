## 1. Approach

The FRAOA has adopted the firm's own root cause analysis at `I-400` as its starting point rather than replacing
it. `I-400` is a competent analytical document: it identifies seven causes, applies a five-why methodology
properly, tests each cause across the firm's inspection sample and finds five to be systemic, and establishes
that five had been identified in earlier cycles with four remedial actions still open.

**The FRAOA's disagreement with `I-400` is not about what it found. It is about two things it did not examine
and one thing the firm did not do with what it found.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Not examined 1** | The nature of the transmission failure, as distinct from the individuals who failed to transmit — §3 |
| **Not examined 2** | Partner incentives and the remuneration framework, expressly ruled out of scope — §5 |
| **Not done** | Two of the three causes weighted "high" have no remedial action — §7 |

## 3. Root cause 1: the transmission failure

### 3.1 What the firm concluded

`I-200` §3.3, on the engagement partner's four-minute opening of the manager's memorandum:

> *"This is not a failure of the firm's system of quality management to generate information. The information
> was generated, in the right form, by the right person, and delivered to the right recipient. **It is a failure
> of the recipient to act on it.**"*

### 3.2 Why the FRAOA does not accept that

The first two sentences are correct. The third is the error, and it is the error from which the firm's
remediation plan follows.

**Consider what the firm's system required in order to work on this engagement.**

| The system required | Condition |
|---|---|
| A manager to write three documents the methodology does not require | Did happen |
| Her to identify her own six inadequate clearances before the report was signed | Did happen |
| Her to send them to the partner | Did happen — at 21:15 on 11 March |
| **A partner to read two long memoranda at 22:04 on the night before signing** | **Did not happen** |
| **Having read them, to reopen a misstatement evaluation, telephone a client CFO, and delay a market announcement scheduled for 07:00 the following day** | **Untested** |

**A control that requires all five of those things is not a control.** It is a sequence of individual acts of
diligence, four of which happened and one of which did not, on an engagement where the one that did not was
being asked of a partner who had signed a different listed audit report that afternoon.

### 3.3 The counterfactual the firm did not consider

The firm's analysis asks why the partner did not read the memorandum. The FRAOA asks a different question:
**by what route, other than one individual choosing to write and another choosing to read, could this
information have reached a decision?**

| Possible route | Existed? |
|---|:-:|
| A mandatory partner review step on the review notes register | **✖** (introduced 22 Jan 20X6 as `I-700` F2) |
| A system alert where a significant-risk workpaper carries no review note | **✖** |
| A system alert where review notes are closed by the preparer | **✖** |
| A required reconciliation of specialist scope to the assertions relied upon | **✖** (agreed as `I-700` F6; open) |
| A block on dating a report while an EQR sign-off is outstanding | **✖** (agreed in three cycles; open) |
| An escalation route for a manager that does not depend on the engagement partner | **✖** |
| A second partner review, which the client risk classification of High would have triggered | **✖ — declined at `A-100` §4.2 because "the fee does not support it"** |

**Seven possible routes. None existed. The information reached the partner's screen and stopped.**

### 3.4 The same pattern one level up

The FRAOA notes that the transmission failure recurs, in identical form, at the level above.

| | Level 2 → 3 | Level 4 → firm governance |
|---|---|---|
| Who identified the problem accurately, in writing | **The audit manager** (`M-100` §6, `M-300` §2) | **The inspector who did a third of the fieldwork** (`I-500/5`) |
| Where it was filed | The engagement file | The inspection working papers |
| Who was supposed to read it | The engagement partner | The Risk and Quality Committee |
| **Did it reach them?** | **No — opened for 4 minutes** | **No — never referred to** |
| The escalation route that existed | None independent of the engagement partner | Over the head of the Head of Quality, to a committee on which he sits |
| **Was the escalation route used?** | n/a | **No.** *"He is on the Remuneration Committee and I am four years off partner."* |

**The firm's system produced two accurate written warnings about its own failures, at two different levels,
fourteen months apart, and neither reached a decision-maker.** The firm's analysis treats the first as an
individual failure and does not identify the second at all.

**This is the central systemic finding of this report.** The firm's problem is not detection. Its detection is
good. Its problem is that information about quality travels upward only when an individual chooses to carry it,
and the individuals asked to carry it are structurally the least able to insist.

## 4. Root cause 2: the terms of reference boundary

`I-100` §4.4 places outside NQMG's scope:

| Question | In scope? |
|---|:-:|
| Was sufficient appropriate audit evidence obtained? | ✔ |
| **Were the financial statements materially misstated?** | **✖** |
| **Was the audit opinion appropriate?** | **✖** |
| **Should the firm consider whether the opinion requires revisiting?** | **✖** |

### 4.1 What this boundary did on this engagement

| Step | Taken by |
|---|---|
| Identify that the cut-off sample error was not projected | **The firm's inspectors** |
| Perform the projection: £7.02m | **The firm's inspectors** |
| Observe that `D-100` understates the estimated misstatement by £5.1m | **The firm's inspectors** |
| Substitute £7.02m into `D-100`: **£7.47m against materiality of £3.10m — 2.4×** | **Nobody at the firm** |
| Ask what that means for an unmodified opinion | **Nobody at the firm** |

**The firm's inspectors performed four of the five steps and the fifth is a division.**

### 4.2 The referral that was requested and refused

| | |
|---|---|
| **The engagement partner requested a referral to the Risk and Quality Committee** | 20X5-08-29, `I-600` §4: *"I understand that `I-100` §4.4 places the appropriateness of the opinion outside NQMG's terms of reference. But somebody in this firm needs to look at that number."* |
| **Refused by the Head of Quality** | 20X5-09-03: *"Noted. The engagement is graded and the remedial actions will address the methodology point. I do not consider a referral necessary."* |

**The partner responsible for the deficient audit asked the firm to consider whether his own opinion required
revisiting, and the firm's Head of Quality declined to refer it.**

### 4.3 The FRAOA's conclusion on the boundary

A monitoring function may legitimately decline to *decide* whether an opinion was appropriate — that is a
judgement for the firm's governance, on advice. **It cannot legitimately be structured so that the question
cannot be asked.**

The boundary as drafted means that the more serious a deficiency, the less likely NQMG is to be able to
articulate its consequence. A minor documentation lapse is fully within scope. **A projected misstatement at 2.4×
materiality generates a finding whose consequence NQMG is not permitted to state.**

`AV-QM-06` compounds it. Grade 3 requires a conclusion that sufficient appropriate evidence was not obtained.
The Head of Quality's evidence to the FRAOA:

> *"I asked him whether he was telling me the opinion was wrong. He said the terms of reference did not permit
> him to say. I took the view that we should not attach the firm's most serious grade short of non-compliance to
> an engagement where we were expressly declining to say whether the outcome was wrong."*

**The terms of reference prevented the inspectors from stating a consequence, and the absence of the stated
consequence was then used to justify a lower grade.** The boundary did not merely limit the report. It
determined it.

Required action at `R-600` 4.

## 5. Root cause 3: incentives — the analysis the firm ruled out of scope

`I-400` §5.2: *"The firm's partner remuneration framework is a matter for the Remuneration Committee and is
outside the scope of engagement inspection."*

`I-400` §5.1 examines whether the *engagement* had enough money and concludes, correctly, that it did: the
engagement over-ran budget by 9.8% and recovered 82% of standard rates. **It does not ask what the engagement
partner was paid to optimise.** The FRAOA has performed that analysis under s.51.

### 5.1 The partner remuneration framework

| Component | Weighting |
|---|---:|
| Practice unit financial performance | **34%** |
| Portfolio growth and client retention | 22% |
| **Audit quality metric** | **4%** |
| Leadership, people and development | 18% |
| Firm-wide and market contribution | 12% |
| Risk and compliance | 10% |

### 5.2 How the 4% quality metric operates

| Trigger | Effect on the metric |
|---|---|
| Internal inspection **grade 1** | Full score |
| Internal inspection **grade 2** | **Full score** |
| Internal inspection **grade 3** | Partial reduction |
| Internal inspection grade 4 | Full forfeit of the 4% component |
| No engagement inspected in the cycle | **Full score by default** |

**Grade 2 attracts no reduction at all.**

### 5.3 The engagement partner's outcome

| | |
|---|---|
| FY20X5 practice unit performance against target at the date of file completion | **6% behind** |
| Internal inspection grade | **2** |
| **Effect of the ARG inspection on his FY20X5 quality metric** | **Nil** |
| **Effect on his FY20X5 profit share** | **Nil** |
| Effect had the grade been 3 | Partial reduction of a 4% component |
| **Maximum financial consequence of a grade 3 on this engagement** | **Less than 2% of total remuneration** |

### 5.4 What this means in combination

Put the three facts together.

| | |
|---|---|
| Probability of any given listed engagement being inspected in a cycle | **8.0%** (7 of 88) |
| Probability for an elevated-risk engagement | **6.3%** (1 of 16) |
| Maximum remuneration consequence if inspected and graded 3 | **<2% of total remuneration** |
| **Expected remuneration consequence of a quality failure on any given engagement** | **Approximately 0.13%** |
| Weighting of practice unit financial performance | **34%** |

**A partner deciding in March how to allocate a finite week between four listed engagements faces a 34%
weighting on financial performance and an expected quality consequence of roughly a tenth of one per cent.**

**The FRAOA does not find that Mr Belliveau consciously weighed those incentives.** The FRAOA's finding is
narrower and, it considers, more serious: **the firm has never asked what the framework rewards, and expressly
excluded the question from the root cause analysis of a quality failure.**

### 5.5 The governance conflict

**The Head of Quality who determined that the remuneration framework was outside the scope of the root cause
analysis is a member of the Partner Remuneration Committee.**

He also approves the inspection gradings that feed the quality metric that committee applies, and is the person
to whom, or over whose head, a dissenting inspector must escalate.

**One individual sits at the intersection of: approving inspection gradings; determining the scope of root cause
analysis; applying the quality metric those gradings feed; and being the escalation route for objections to any
of the above.** That is not a criticism of the individual. It is a structural defect. Required action at
`R-600` 7.

### 5.6 The incentive analysis nobody performed at any level

The FRAOA performed the following analysis. **It appears nowhere in the audit file, the manager's review, the
partner's review or the firm's inspection report.** `A-400` §2.4 and `A-500` §3.1 both identify the CFO's LTIP
as a fraud risk factor; `MRN-004` asked which procedure responded to it and was told it was a factor rather than
a risk.

| Scenario | Adjusted EPS | LTIP vesting (threshold 138.0p, maximum 156.0p) |
|---|---:|---:|
| **As reported** | **149.2p** | **62% of maximum** |
| Reversing the CN-4471 estimate change (`FRAOA-F04`, £3.5m) | 143.1p | **28%** |
| Also projecting the cut-off error (`FRAOA-F02`, £5.1m) | **134.2p** | **0% — below threshold** |

**The two issues on which the audit obtained least evidence are the two which, if resolved the other way, would
have taken the CFO's incentive from 62% of maximum to nil.**

`FRAOA-F05` (contingent consideration) and `FRAOA-F06` (goodwill) carry **no** adjusted-EPS effect, because
remeasurement and impairment are both adjusting items, and no covenant effect either.

**That asymmetry cuts both ways and the FRAOA states it plainly.** Two of the four principal issues had a large
incentive attached and two had none. **The FRAOA therefore does not find that the audit failures were a targeted
response to management incentives.** They are better explained by a general insufficiency of scepticism across
every judgemental area, which is consistent with `FRAOA-F03`.

**But the analysis is one page and it was never done.** The engagement identified the incentive at planning as a
fraud risk factor and never quantified it. `MRN-004` asked the question and received an answer that closed it.
Had the table above been prepared in March 20X5, it is difficult to see how `C-100` and `C-200` could have been
concluded as they were.

## 7. Why monitoring and remediation is assessed as not effective

ISQM 1 requires a monitoring process that provides **relevant, reliable and timely** information about the
firm's system of quality management, and a remediation process that designs, implements and **evaluates the
effectiveness of** remedial actions.

| Requirement | Position |
|---|---|
| **Monitoring provides relevant information** | **The information was generated. It was accurate. It was not transmitted.** Six findings became two; the dissent was filed and never referred to; grade 2 removed the engagement from individual reporting to the Risk and Quality Committee altogether. |
| **Monitoring is reliable** | **No.** The grading was unsupportable on the firm's own criterion and its own retained findings (`R-200` §10). Two of four reclassification rationales were factually wrong. The firm graded five of seven engagements higher than the FRAOA. |
| Monitoring is objective | **No.** The lead inspector had served on the engagement within the policy bar; the declaration was self-certified and unchecked; the report approver has four overlapping roles (§5.5). |
| Root causes are investigated | **Yes, and well** — subject to the two exclusions at §4 and §5. |
| **Remedial actions respond to the root cause** | **No.** Two of the three causes weighted "high" — review-phase compression and manager under-resourcing — have no action at all. Both had been identified in prior cycles. |
| **Remedial actions are implemented** | **Partially.** 23 actions open at 20X6-04-24, up from 19; 13 carried from prior cycles; 8 outstanding more than two cycles. The EQR system control has been agreed in three cycles and deferred twice. |
| **The effectiveness of remedial actions is evaluated** | **No.** All seven actions closed in the period were closed on the owner's confirmation. Three methodology changes were issued on 22 January 20X6 and marked complete on issue. **Nothing has been tested for effectiveness.** |

> ### The conclusion
>
> **Ashcroft Vane LLP can see its own audit failures. This inspection has established that with unusual
> clarity, because the firm's own draft report identified twelve of the FRAOA's fifteen findings.**
>
> What the firm cannot do is act on what it sees. Between fieldwork and publication, six findings became two.
> The inspector who objected filed a note that nobody read. The partner who asked the firm to consider whether
> his own opinion required revisiting was refused. The remediation plan omits both of the causes the firm's own
> analysis weighted highest, and closes its actions on the confirmation of the person responsible for them. The
> single system control that would have prevented one of the two findings the firm did report has been agreed
> three times and built once — not at all.
>
> A firm that cannot detect its deficiencies has a remediable problem. **A firm that detects them precisely,
> reclassifies them, files the objection and reports to its own governance that the portfolio is broadly
> satisfactory has disabled the mechanism by which any of this could improve.** The information on which
> improvement depends is created every cycle and reaches no one with authority to act on it.
>
> **The firm's monitoring and remediation process is therefore assessed as NOT EFFECTIVE.**

Required actions at `R-600`.
