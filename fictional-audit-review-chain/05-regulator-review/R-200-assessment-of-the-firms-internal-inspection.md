## 1. The subject of this document

This document does not assess the audit of Auburn Ridge Group plc. That is at `R-300`.

**This document assesses the firm's own inspection of that audit** — the work performed by the National
Quality & Monitoring Group between 14 July and 30 September 20X5, the findings it reached, and what happened
to those findings before the report was issued.

It is the most important document in this report, and it is the reason the FRAOA has assessed the firm's
monitoring and remediation process as **not effective**.

## 4. Inspector independence — `FRAOA-F03`

### 4.1 The facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Lead inspector | N. Ferreira-Osei, Director, NQMG |
| Role on the ARG engagement, FY20X0 | Audit Manager |
| **Role on the ARG engagement, FY20X1 and FY20X2** | **Audit Senior Manager** |
| Engagement partner during FY20X1–FY20X2 | **R. Belliveau** |
| Last audit report on which he served | 9 March 20X3 |
| Inspection fieldwork | July 20X5 |
| **Elapsed period** | **2 years 4 months** |
| **`AV-QM-09` requirement** | **5 years** |
| Independence declaration given | **"No conflict"**, 20X5-07-07 |

### 4.2 How the declaration came to be given

The NQMG workflow question asks whether the inspector has served *"as engagement partner, engagement quality
reviewer, or in any other capacity requiring you to be recorded as a member of the engagement team"* within
five years.

Mr Ferreira-Osei's account, given under s.47 on 20X6-03-17:

> *"I answered the question I was asked. I was not the engagement partner and I was not the EQR. On the third
> limb I read 'recorded as a member of the engagement team' as referring to the current engagement team
> listing, and I was not on it.*
>
> *That is not a good answer and I am not going to pretend it is. I knew I had been the senior manager on
> Auburn Ridge for two years. I knew Ronan Belliveau, because I worked for him. **I should have flagged it and
> asked to be taken off, and the reason I did not is that NQMG had four people covering seven inspections and I
> was the only director available for a listed group of that size.***
>
> *Nobody checked. The system does not cross-reference the declaration against the firm's own engagement team
> records, which contain my name against Auburn Ridge for FY20X0 to FY20X2. It would be a single query."*

### 4.3 FRAOA assessment

The FRAOA accepts that the declaration was not deliberately false, and that the question as drafted is
capable of the reading he gave it. It is nonetheless wrong: he had served on the engagement in a capacity
requiring him to be recorded as a member of the engagement team, within the five-year window.

**The FRAOA does not find that the conflict caused findings to be missed.** §3.2 shows that the firm's draft
identified twelve of the FRAOA's fifteen findings. The detection was good.

**The FRAOA finds that the conflict created an unmanaged threat to objectivity at the point where objectivity
mattered most: the grading decision.** On 9 September 20X5 the lead inspector attended a 90-minute unminuted
meeting with the engagement partner who had been his own engagement partner for two years, together with the
Head of Quality, and within two days had reversed his own recommendation of 2 September to retain grade 3.

The FRAOA also notes his own evidence on the point: *"I should not have been in that room and I certainly
should not have been in it without a minute-taker."*

**The failure is threefold, and only the first concerns the individual:** a self-certified declaration; a
system that does not cross-reference the declaration against the firm's own records of who served on which
engagement; and the resourcing constraint that made him the only available director.

## 5. Selection of the inspection sample

| | |
|---|---:|
| Listed audits in the firm's portfolio | 88 |
| Engagements inspected in cycle 20X5 | **7 (8.0%)** |
| **Engagements in the firm's own "elevated risk" tier** | **16 (18.2% of the portfolio)** |
| **Elevated-risk engagements selected** | **1** |
| Proportionate representation on a sample of 7 | 1.3 |

`AV-QM-03` requires elevated-risk engagements to be represented at least in proportion to their share of the
portfolio. On a strict reading, 1 selection against an expectation of 1.3 is arguably compliant.

**The FRAOA does not accept that reading, for three reasons.**

**First**, the firm's own risk tiering exists to direct monitoring effort. A process that assigns 16
engagements to an elevated tier and then samples that tier at the same rate as the rest of the portfolio has
not used the tiering for anything.

**Second**, sample size. A 7-engagement sample cannot meaningfully sample a 16-engagement tier at all. One
observation supports no inference about 16 engagements.

**Third**, and decisively:

| Cycle | Elevated-tier engagements | Selected | Proportionate expectation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 20X2 | 14 | **1** | 1.1 |
| 20X3 | 15 | **1** | 1.2 |
| 20X4 | 15 | **1** | 1.2 |
| 20X5 | 16 | **1** | 1.3 |
| **Four cycles** | | **4 of 28** | **4.8** |

**Across four cycles the firm has inspected 4 elevated-risk engagements out of a possible 28.** Each cycle is
individually defensible by rounding. The pattern is not.

## 6. The recorded dissent — `FRAOA-F04`

### 6.1 The document

On 20X5-09-16, five days after the revised draft reclassifying four findings was circulated within NQMG and
ten days before the Head of Quality approved it, Senior Manager R. Achebe-Kim — who had performed 44 of the
141 inspection hours — recorded a written dissent at `I-500/5`.

The FRAOA regards two passages as material:

> *"We have moved from six findings to two without a single fact changing. I have re-read the draft and the
> final side by side. Every number is the same. The £3.5m is still 113% of materiality. The projection is still
> £7.0m. The covenant still breaches at 3.08×. The assessment period is still three months short of the
> requirement and the facility still matures inside the gap.*
>
> *What changed is the adjectives."*

> *"We are the firm's monitoring function. If we can find six things and report two, then what we report is not
> information about the firm's quality. It is information about what the firm was prepared to write down."*

The note also records that she raised the timing of `C-100/13` with the lead inspector on 12 September 20X5
and was told *"the file is what it is and we review it as we find it"*. **She was right, four months before
the FRAOA established it. Her concern was the only contemporaneous challenge to the document that the most
significant downgrade relied upon.**

### 6.2 What happened to it

| | |
|---|:-:|
| Filed in the inspection working papers | ✔ |
| **Referred to in the final inspection report** | **✖** |
| **Reported to the Risk and Quality Committee** | **✖** |
| Reflected in the cycle 20X5 summary of 20X5-10-14 | **✖** |
| Escalation route available under `AV-QM-05` (direct referral to the Chair of the Risk and Quality Committee) | Available |
| **Used** | **✖** |
| Any response to the note recorded | **✖** |

### 6.3 Why the note was not escalated

Ms Achebe-Kim's evidence, given under s.47 on 20X6-03-19:

> *"I filed the note. I did not go over the Head of Quality's head to the Committee chair. He is on the
> Remuneration Committee and I am four years off partner. I am not going to dress that up as anything other
> than what it was."*

### 6.4 FRAOA assessment

**This is the most serious finding in this report about the firm's system of quality management, and it is
more serious than the reclassifications themselves.**

ISQM 1 requires a monitoring process that provides **relevant and reliable** information about the operation
of the firm's system of quality management. The information generated by this inspection was relevant and
reliable. **It did not reach the body responsible for the system.**

Three features compound it:

1. **The dissent was accurate.** Every proposition in it has been verified by the FRAOA, including the one the
   lead inspector dismissed on 12 September.
2. **It came from the person best placed to hold the view**, having performed a third of the fieldwork and
   having not been present at the meeting that changed the outcome.
3. **The only escalation route available required the dissenting inspector to go over the head of the Head of
   Quality, who is a member of the Partner Remuneration Committee, to the Chair of a committee on which the
   Head of Quality sits.** A route with that architecture is not a route. See §11.

**The FRAOA records that this note is the reason it obtained both versions of the report.** Had it not been
in the working papers, the FRAOA would have found a deficient audit and an internal inspection reporting two
findings. It would not have found that the firm's own inspectors identified six. **The firm's system
depended, for the discovery of its most significant weakness, on a senior manager filing a note nobody read
and a regulator happening to look in the right folder.**

## 7. Completeness of the fieldwork — the `C-100/13` gap

Three matters the FRAOA found were not identified by the firm's inspection. One of them matters
disproportionately.

| Not identified | FRAOA finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Nine unauthorised post-assembly modifications to the archived file, including the addition of `C-100/13` on 19 May 20X5** | **`FRAOA-F13`** | **Severe — see below** |
| The Larkspur matter was not referred to the General Counsel or considered for external reporting | `FRAOA-F12` | Moderate |
| The file was assembled on day 61 against a 60-day limit | Within `FRAOA-F13` | Minor |

### 7.1 Why the `C-100/13` gap is severe

| | |
|---|---|
| What `C-100/13` is | A two-page look-back analysis of prior year contract cost estimates |
| **When it was added to the archived file** | **20X5-05-19 — 68 days after the audit report, 7 days after the file was locked** |
| Authorisation under `AV-AUD-19` | **None** |
| What `C-100` §7 said as archived on 20X5-05-12 | **That the retrospective review was not performed** |
| Second unauthorised modification, same day | **Amended `C-100` §7 to cross-refer to the new schedule** |
| **What the firm's inspection did with it** | **Relied on it to downgrade `INSP-F05` — the most significant substantive finding in the report — from a finding to an improvement point** |

The downgrade rationale at `I-500` §4.3 reads: *"A look-back analysis is present at `C-100/13`, though it is
high-level and does not address the specific contract."*

**The rationale for downgrading the report's most significant substantive finding relies on a document that
did not exist when the opinion was signed.**

### 7.2 How it happened

| Party | Position |
|---|---|
| **The inspection** | Reviewed the file in its current state. `I-100` §4.3 records that the modification log at `D-600/2` was **opened but not analysed**. |
| **The lead inspector** | *"Ronan pointed me to `C-100/13`. My fieldwork note said the retrospective review had not been performed, and there it was on the file. I concluded I had been wrong on a point of fact. **I did not check when it had been put there.** Had I opened the modification log I would have seen it was added on 19 May, without authorisation, and the finding would have got stronger rather than weaker — because a look-back analysis appearing on a file two months after the report date is a worse fact than no look-back analysis at all."* |
| **The engagement partner** | *"I pointed at `C-100/13` because it was on the file and the draft finding said it was not. I did not know when it had been added and I did not check. Dev Ramanathan put it there in May. Nobody asked me to approve it and I did not know it existed until I went looking for something to answer the finding with. When the FRAOA showed me the version history I understood what I had done, which is to defend myself with a document that should not exist."* (Withdrawn at `I-600` §5 addendum, 20X6-03-24.) |
| **The senior in charge** | Evidence of 20X6-03-12: *"I did the analysis in May because it was on the list of matters for next year at `D-200` §14 and I had the data. I did not think of it as adding to a closed file. I did not know the file was locked or that anything needed approval."* |
| **The dissenting inspector** | Raised the timing on 20X5-09-12 and was told the file is reviewed as found. |

**The FRAOA does not find that any individual added the document in order to mislead the inspection.** The
senior in charge's account is credible: he performed an analysis flagged as a matter for the following year
and filed it where it belonged, not knowing the file was locked.

**The FRAOA finds that the firm's controls over document integrity do not exist in any operative form.** Nine
modifications were made to a locked file, none authorised, six without a recorded reason. The system permitted
each of them and flagged none. And the one control that would have caught it — analysing the modification log
— was the control the inspection had already demonstrated it knew how to apply: `INSP-IP05` tests a review
note timing discrepancy against system metadata and resolves it correctly. **The same test was available and
was not applied to a document being used to downgrade a finding.**

## 9. The root cause analysis

### 9.1 As an analytical document

**Good.** `I-400` identifies seven root causes, applies a five-why methodology properly, tests each cause
across the cycle sample, finds five to be systemic, and establishes that five had been identified in prior
cycles with four remedial actions still open. **The FRAOA has adopted `I-400`'s causal analysis and built on
it at `R-500` rather than replacing it.**

### 9.2 The two things it does not do

**It does not examine incentives.** `I-400` §5.2 records: *"The firm's partner remuneration framework is a
matter for the Remuneration Committee and is outside the scope of engagement inspection."*

The FRAOA's position: a root cause analysis of a quality failure that excludes the framework determining what
partners are paid to optimise has excluded a candidate cause by definition rather than by evidence. `I-400`
§5.1 examines whether the *engagement* had enough money — and concludes, correctly, that it did, on the
evidence of a 9.8% budget over-run. **It does not ask what the engagement partner was paid to optimise.**
`R-500` §5 performs that analysis.

**The FRAOA notes that the Head of Quality who approved `I-400`, and who determined that the remuneration
framework was out of scope, is himself a member of the Partner Remuneration Committee.** See §11.

**Its conclusions were not carried into the remediation.** `I-400` §4 weights three causes as "high": time
compression (RC1), manager under-resourcing (RC4), and methodology and tooling gaps. **The remediation plan at
`I-700` contains no action addressing RC1 or RC4.** `R-500` §7.

## 10. The grading determination

### 10.1 The firm's own criterion

`AV-QM-06`: *"An engagement shall be graded 3 where the inspection concludes that sufficient appropriate
audit evidence was not obtained in respect of a material balance, class of transactions or disclosure, or
where a significant failure to comply with firm methodology is identified."*

### 10.2 Applying it to the two findings the firm retained

| Finding retained in the final report | Does it engage `AV-QM-06`? |
|---|---|
| **`INSP-F02` — cut-off sample error not projected** | **Yes.** The firm's own finding quantifies a projected misstatement of £7.0m in a class of transactions and records that the evaluation of misstatements was performed on a figure understating it by £5.1m. That is a conclusion that sufficient appropriate audit evidence was not obtained in respect of a material class of transactions. |
| **`INSP-F01` — EQR completed after the report date** | **Yes.** A breach of ISQM 2 in respect of three workpapers addressing significant risks is a significant failure to comply with firm methodology. |

**On the firm's own criterion, and on the two findings the firm itself retained, grade 3 was required.** The
grade of 2 is not supportable even on the final report's own contents, without regard to the four
reclassifications.

### 10.3 The FRAOA's grade

**D — Significant improvements required.**

Basis: sufficient appropriate audit evidence was not obtained in respect of contract revenue and contract
assets (`FRAOA-F04`), point-in-time revenue (`FRAOA-F02`), the contingent consideration liability
(`FRAOA-F05`), goodwill (`FRAOA-F06`), and the going concern assessment and disclosure (`FRAOA-F07`,
`FRAOA-F08`). Full reasoning at `R-400`.

| `INSP-IP02` Goodwill discount rate | *(not downgraded — improvement point in both)* | **£21.0m of impairment recognised in FY20X5 against the £22.1m the FY20X4 sensitivity analysis showed would arise at the specialist's lower bound.** |

**Each of the three downgraded matters was resolved against the position the downgrade relied upon within
twelve months.** The FRAOA does not use these outcomes to find against the engagement team — see `R-100` §7 —

> ### The point of this document
>
> The FRAOA has inspected many firms whose monitoring functions do not find enough. This is not one of them.
>
> Ashcroft Vane's inspectors found almost everything. They found the projection error, the covenant breach in
> the hidden column, the mischaracterised consultation, the unreviewed workpapers, the 11.5 hours. They tested
> their causes across the portfolio and established that five were systemic. They established that five had
> been raised before and not fixed. On 12 August 20X5 they wrote it down accurately and proposed the right
> grade.
>
> **Then the firm reduced six findings to two, softened the finding the responsible partner had asked it not
> to soften, filed the dissent of the inspector who had done a third of the work, and reported to its own Risk
> and Quality Committee that the engagement required limited improvements.**
>
> ISQM 1 requires a monitoring process that provides relevant and reliable information about the firm's system
> of quality management. This process generated that information and then declined to transmit it. A firm that
> cannot see its failures may improve by looking harder. **A firm that sees them clearly and records something
> else has removed the possibility of improvement, because the information on which improvement depends never
> reaches anyone with the authority to act.**
>
> That is why the assessment is *not effective*, and why the required actions at `R-600` are directed at the
> monitoring function and its governance rather than at the audit of Auburn Ridge Group plc.
