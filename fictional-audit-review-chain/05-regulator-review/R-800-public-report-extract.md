# ASHCROFT VANE LLP

## Audit Quality Inspection Report 20X5/20X6

---

## 1. Overview

We inspected seven statutory audits performed by Ashcroft Vane LLP and reviewed the firm's system of quality
management under s.51 of the Audit Oversight Act 20W8. Fieldwork was performed between January and April 20X6.

### 1.1 Engagement inspection results

| Grade | Definition | Number |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Good, with limited improvements required | **0** |
| B | Limited improvements required | 2 |
| C | Improvements required | 3 |
| **D** | **Significant improvements required** | **2** |

Two of the seven audits inspected required significant improvements. In both, we concluded that sufficient
appropriate audit evidence had not been obtained in respect of one or more material balances or disclosures.

**We do not conclude that the financial statements concerned were materially misstated.** We do not audit and we
do not opine on financial statements. Our finding is that the auditor did not obtain the evidence required to
support the opinion given.

## 2. Our principal finding

**The most significant matter arising from this inspection does not concern the firm's ability to identify
deficiencies in its audits. It concerns what happens to the deficiencies the firm identifies.**

### 2.1 The firm's monitoring function detected these matters

We record the following, because a reader could otherwise draw the wrong conclusion from the assessment at §1.2.

On the engagement we have graded D and the firm graded 2, the firm's own monitoring function:

- spent 141 hours reviewing a file the engagement partner had reviewed for 11.5 hours;
- extracted and analysed the electronic file audit trail, establishing the partner's review time, his coverage of
  the file, and the fact that two workpapers addressing significant risks had been reviewed by nobody;
- independently re-projected a sampling error the engagement team had failed to project, and quantified it
  correctly;
- rebuilt the entity's going concern model and found, in a hidden column of a spreadsheet on the audit file, a
  covenant breach in the entity's own downside scenario that four reviewers had not identified;
- interviewed the firm's own technical panel and established that a consultation the audit file relied upon had
  been materially mischaracterised in that file;
- tested each of the causes it identified across all seven engagements in its inspection cycle, and established
  that five were present across the firm's listed audit portfolio; and
- established that five of those causes had been identified in a previous inspection cycle and that four
  remedial actions remained open.

**On 12 August 20X5 the firm's inspectors issued a draft report identifying six findings and proposing the
firm's grade 3 — significant improvements required. Twelve of the fifteen findings we have made were in that
draft.**

### 2.2 What then happened

**On 30 September 20X5 the firm issued its final report identifying two findings and grading the engagement 2 —
compliant with limited improvements required.**

| | Draft, 12 Aug 20X5 | Final, 30 Sep 20X5 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Findings | **6** | **2** |
| Improvement points | 8 | 8 |
| **Grade** | **3** | **2** |

Four findings were reclassified as improvement points. **No underlying fact changed. Every figure in the final
report appears in the draft.**

In the twenty-one days between the firm's internal recommendation to retain the higher grade and the revised
draft reclassifying the findings, the only recorded event is a meeting attended by the lead inspector, the Head
of Quality and the engagement partner. **No minute of that meeting exists**, contrary to the firm's own policy.

**The lead inspector had served on the engagement in a senior capacity within the five-year period during which
the firm's own policy barred him from inspecting it.** His independence declaration, which recorded no conflict,
was self-certified and was not reconciled against the firm's own records of who had served on the engagement.

### 2.3 The dissent

**A senior manager who had performed approximately a third of the inspection fieldwork recorded a written
dissent from the reclassifications.** Her note stated that the report had moved from six findings to two
"without a single fact changing", and that "if we can find six things and report two, then what we report is not
information about the firm's quality; it is information about what the firm was prepared to write down".

Her note was accurate. We have verified every proposition in it, including one that the lead inspector dismissed
at the time and which we subsequently established to be correct.

**Her note was filed in the inspection working papers. It was not referred to in the final report. It was not
reported to the firm's Risk and Quality Committee. It was not escalated.** The only escalation route available to
her required her to go over the head of the Head of Quality to the chair of a committee on which the Head of
Quality sits.

**We located her note in the firm's working papers. It is the reason we requested both versions of the report.**
Had we reviewed only the final report and the audit file, as is our standard practice, we would not have known
that the firm's own inspectors had identified six findings.

### 2.4 Two of the reclassification rationales were factually wrong

- One relied on a workpaper that was **added to the archived audit file sixty-eight days after the auditor's
  report was signed**, without the dual authorisation the firm's own policy requires. The narrative in the file
  recording that the relevant procedure had *not* been performed was amended on the same day to cross-refer to
  the new document. The firm's inspection reviewed the file in its modified state and did not examine the
  modification log.
- One relied on an event occurring six weeks **after** the auditor's report was signed, and cited a technical
  consultation as supporting the audit conclusion when the firm's own technical director had told the inspection
  team, eight weeks before the reclassification, that he **would not have countersigned that conclusion** had he
  known what the audit file actually contained. That interview was in the inspection's own working papers and
  was quoted in its own draft finding.

### 2.5 Our conclusion

**A firm that cannot detect its own audit failures has a problem capable of remedy by better and more numerous
inspectors. A firm that detects them accurately and then records something else has removed the mechanism by
which improvement could occur, because the information on which improvement depends never reaches anyone with
authority to act on it.**

We have therefore assessed the firm's monitoring and remediation process as **not effective**. The assessment
does not reflect on the technical capability of the firm's monitoring function, which we have assessed as good.
It reflects on what the firm does with what that function finds.

## 3. The engagement graded D

We do not identify the entity or any individual.

### 3.1 What the audit was

A listed group with revenue of approximately £1.3bn. The reporting period included a mid-year enterprise
resource planning migration and a significant acquisition. The group had narrow headroom on the goodwill of one
cash-generating unit and a committed borrowing facility maturing fourteen months after the balance sheet date.

### 3.2 What we found

Sufficient appropriate audit evidence was not obtained in respect of:

| Area | Summary |
|---|---|
| **Long-term contract revenue** | A reduction in the estimated cost of completing the group's largest contract increased revenue and profit by an amount exceeding materiality. The supporting evidence was management's own schedule, a three-sentence email from a project director and a note of a twenty-five-minute telephone call. **The retrospective review of prior estimates required by the auditing standards was planned and not performed.** At least six items of evidence on the audit file inconsistent with management's estimate were not addressed — including a substantial customer claim, served during fieldwork, asserting that the same contract was some forty weeks late. |
| **Point-in-time revenue** | Errors identified in a sample were not projected to the population, contrary to the firm's own methodology and notwithstanding that the audit file expressly recorded the errors as not anomalous. **Projection would have increased uncorrected misstatements from below materiality to approximately 2.4 times materiality.** |
| **A contingent consideration liability** | A Level 3 fair value exceeding materiality by more than three times. **No audit procedure was performed on its measurement.** The firm's own valuation specialist's agreed scope expressly excluded it; the preparer of the workpaper recorded that exclusion; and the workpaper was reviewed by nobody. Three people each inferred that another had covered it. |
| **Goodwill** | The discount rate applied by management fell **below the range determined by the firm's own valuation specialist**. Headroom was 2.1% and an increase of 0.2 percentage points would have eliminated it. The specialist offered in writing to discuss the unit further; the offer was escalated to the engagement partner and declined for want of time. |
| **Going concern** | The assessment period was three months shorter than required and the omitted period contained the maturity of the facility. No documentary evidence of refinancing progress was obtained. **The entity's own downside scenario showed a covenant breach; the audit did not recalculate that scenario at any quarterly test date.** The consultation relied upon to resolve the period question was not documented, was requested three times by two other reviewers, and was never provided. |

We also found that £2.4m of payments to an entity connected to a director were not disclosed as a related party
transaction; that the audit file contained no analysis of the relevant accounting requirement; and that the
matter was not communicated to those charged with governance.

### 3.3 Engagement-level quality management

**The engagement partner's documented review of the audit file occupied 11.5 hours on an engagement of 3,140
hours — 0.37%, against the firm's own benchmark of 2 to 3%.** He opened 7.6% of the file and five of the nine
workpapers addressing significant risks. Four of the five he did not open contain a finding in this report.
Sixty-eight per cent of his review notes posed no question. **He did not examine the basis on which any of the
forty-nine review notes raised by the level below him had been cleared.**

The auditor's report was dated **two days before the engagement quality reviewer completed her review of three
workpapers addressing significant risks.**

### 3.4 A matter we record in the engagement team's favour

**The most significant deficiencies in this engagement were identified and documented contemporaneously by a
member of the engagement team below the engagement partner, in writing, before the auditor's report was signed,
in documents the firm's methodology did not require her to prepare.** Those documents identified her own
inadequate clearances, corrected her own earlier advice on the sampling question, and disclosed the related party
matter.

**They were opened by the intended recipient for a combined eight minutes on the evening before the report was
signed, and were not read.**

We make no criticism of the individual concerned and we have made no referral in respect of her. We record the
matter because a firm's system of quality management depends on people documenting problems accurately and
passing them upward, and because that behaviour is discretionary, unrewarded and personally costly. On this
engagement it happened, and the failure lay in the response to it.

**We note that the same pattern recurred fourteen months later within the firm's own monitoring function, when
an inspector's written dissent was filed and never read.** A firm in which accurate written warnings are produced
at two different levels and reach a decision-maker at neither does not have an information problem. It has a
structural one.

### 3.5 A further matter we record

The audit was not uniformly deficient, and we record two respects in which it was not.

**The audit of inventories was well performed.** Provision percentages were back-tested against prior period
outcomes, which identified an under-provision; demand evidence was obtained on the largest slow-moving items;
net realisable value was established from actual disposals; management's contrary evidence was obtained and
demonstrated to be inapplicable; and an adjustment of £1.24m was proposed and accepted.

**The report to those charged with governance was materially better than the audit file it reported on.** It
disclosed that the discount rate applied was below the range determined by the firm's own specialist; that no
refinancing term sheet had been received; that a recommendation of the entity's own internal audit function was
overdue; and it quantified the effect of the uncorrected misstatements on the measure by which senior management
remuneration vested. The published auditor's report likewise disclosed that management's discount rate fell
below the firm's own specialist's range.

## 4. Firm-wide findings

### 4.1 Systemic deficiencies established on the firm's own testing

The firm tested each cause it identified across all seven engagements in its own inspection cycle. Its results,
which we have verified:

| Deficiency | Incidence |
|---|---|
| Engagement quality review sign-off post-dating the auditor's report | **3 of 7** |
| Mandatory consultation checklist answer inconsistent with the audit file | **5 of 7** |
| Reliance on internal controls retained despite unremediated significant IT control deficiencies | **4 of 7** |
| Sampling error identified and not projected | **2 of the 3 engagements that found errors** |
| Review phase shorter than planned | **5 of 7** |

**The firm established these results and then reported the cycle to its own Risk and Quality Committee as
"broadly satisfactory with targeted improvements identified".**

### 4.2 Remediation

| | |
|---|---:|
| Remedial actions open at April 20X6 | **23** |
| Carried forward from a previous cycle | **13** |
| Outstanding for more than two full cycles | **8** |
| Actions closed in the period on the action owner's own confirmation | **7 of 7** |
| **Actions tested for effectiveness** | **0** |

**One control — a system prohibition on dating an auditor's report while an engagement quality review sign-off
remains outstanding — has been agreed by the firm in three successive inspection cycles and has never been
implemented.** It is the direct cause of one of the two findings the firm itself reported on the engagement we
have graded D, and the deficiency it would prevent was present in three of the seven engagements the firm
inspected.

### 4.3 Governance

**One individual approves the firm's inspection gradings, determines the scope of its root cause analysis, sits
on the committee that applies the partner quality metric those gradings feed, and constitutes the escalation
route for objections to any of the above.**

The firm's root cause analysis of this quality failure **expressly excluded the partner remuneration framework
from scope**, on the basis that it was a matter for the Remuneration Committee. That exclusion was determined by
the individual described above.

The partner remuneration framework weights audit quality at **4%** and practice unit financial performance at
**34%**. A grade 2 outcome attracts no reduction to the quality component. **A partner none of whose engagements
is inspected in a cycle scores the full quality component by default.**

We have not concluded that this framework caused the failures we have found. **We have concluded that the firm
has never asked, and excluded the question from the scope of the analysis in which it would have arisen.**

## 5. Actions required

We have required **fourteen** actions under s.61 of the Audit Oversight Act 20W8. They are directed principally
at:

1. **Restructuring the reporting line and governance of the monitoring function**, so that gradings are approved
   by a person who does not apply the metric they feed.
2. **A dissent process** under which any inspector's recorded dissent is reproduced in the final report, reported
   to the Risk and Quality Committee, and responded to in writing — without requiring escalation through the
   person whose decision is dissented from.
3. **Minuted meetings and dated evidence** for any change to a finding classification or grading between draft
   and final report, approved by a person who did not perform the fieldwork.
4. **Mandatory referral to the firm's governance** where an inspection identifies an absence of evidence or a
   potential misstatement exceeding materiality, incapable of being declined by the Head of Quality.
5. **Re-performance** of the internal inspection of the engagement we have graded D, and of two others, by
   independent inspectors.
6. **Automated reconciliation of inspector independence declarations** against the firm's own records.
7. **An independent review of the partner remuneration framework.**
8. **An operative control over the integrity of archived audit files.**
9. **Implementation of the engagement quality review system control, by 31 December 20X6.** We have refused the
   firm's request for an extension.

The firm is required to appoint an **independent monitor**, approved by us, reporting quarterly to the firm's
Risk and Quality Committee and simultaneously to us, until March 20X8.

**We will re-inspect the firm in cycle 20X7/20X8.** If the monitoring and remediation process remains not
effective at that point, we will consider imposing conditions on the firm's registration under s.66.

We have made **one referral to our Enforcement Division** in respect of an individual's conduct. Three
firm-level matters are under consideration. A referral is not a finding of misconduct, and the individual
concerned is entitled to make representations before any determination.

## 6. The firm's response

The firm's full response and our reply are recorded in the inspection file. The firm did not contest any finding
of fact.

**Of nine representations made, we accepted two.** Both concerned the recognition of matters in the firm's and
its staff's favour: that its monitoring function had in fact detected almost all of the matters we found, and
that the member of the engagement team who documented the deficiencies contemporaneously should be recognised as
having done so. **We have amended this report to reflect both.**

We record the firm's own concluding statement:

> *"We accept that a firm which sees its failures clearly and records something else has removed the mechanism by
> which it might improve. We do not accept that this is what we intended, and we accept that it is what we
> did."*

## 7. What audit committees should take from this report

We publish these reports so that those charged with governance can ask better questions. On this inspection we
suggest four.

**1. Ask how many hours your engagement partner spent reviewing the audit file, and what proportion of the file
he or she opened.** This information exists in every audit firm's electronic file system for every engagement.
On the engagement we graded D it was 11.5 hours and 7.6%, and nobody looked at it until an inspection team did,
sixteen months after the opinion was signed.

**2. Ask whether the engagement partner examined how the review notes raised below him were cleared, and not
merely that they were closed.** On the engagement we graded D, nineteen of forty-nine notes were closed on an
explanation with no new evidence, eleven were closed by the person who prepared the workpaper in breach of the
firm's own policy, and two workpapers addressing significant risks carried no notes at all because nobody had
opened them. All of that was visible in a single report the partner did not open.

**3. If you ask your auditor a question in private session, ask whether the answer represents what the audit
team knows or what the person answering knows.** On the engagement we graded D, an audit committee chair asked a
direct question about a director and received an accurate answer from a partner who had not been told the
relevant facts by his own team. As she put it to us: *"We did not ask a question of a man. We asked it of a firm,
and the firm had the answer and could not produce it."*

**4. Ask your auditor what its own internal inspection found, and ask whether any finding was reclassified
between draft and final report.** You are entitled to ask. On the engagement we graded D, the answer would have
been that the firm's own inspectors found six findings and reported two.
