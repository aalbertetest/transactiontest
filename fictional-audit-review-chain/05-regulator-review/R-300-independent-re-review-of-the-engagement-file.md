## 1. Purpose and approach

To form an independent view of the audit, without reliance on the firm's inspection conclusions, so that the
FRAOA can (a) reach its own grading and (b) assess the firm's inspection against an independent benchmark.

**The FRAOA reviewed the file before reading the firm's inspection report.** The engagement review was
performed by O. Ayakannu between 19 January and 14 February 20X6. The firm's inspection working papers were
received on 3 February and read by B. Salgado-Ferrer, working separately. The two work streams were brought
together on 17 February.

This sequencing matters: the FRAOA's findings at `R-400` were not derived from the firm's findings, which is
why the comparison at `R-200` §3.2 is meaningful.

## 2. The distinction the FRAOA maintains throughout

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Were ARG's FY20X4 financial statements materially misstated?** | **The FRAOA does not know and does not opine.** It has not audited them. |
| **Did the auditor obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence?** | **No, in five areas.** |
| Are these the same question? | **No.** An auditor who reaches a correct conclusion without evidence has still failed. An auditor with adequate evidence whose conclusion is later shown to be wrong has not. |

Every finding at `R-400` is a finding of the second kind.

## 4. The connection nobody made

The FRAOA sets this out separately because it is the clearest single illustration of what went wrong, and
because **no document at any of the four levels below states it.**

### 4.1 Two facts, both in the audit file, both known before the report was signed

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fact 1** — `C-100` §6.2 | In Q4 20X4 management reduced the estimated cost of completing contract CN-4471 by **£4.1m**, increasing revenue and profit by £3.5m. One of the three stated reasons was **a reduction in third-party certification scope following the Meridian Rail Authority's acceptance of a revised test protocol.** |
| **Fact 2** — `C-950` §5.1 | On 4 February 20X5 the Meridian Rail Authority served notice claiming **£6.8m of liquidated damages** on contract CN-4471, asserting delays of **41 weeks on delivery gate 3 and 39 weeks on delivery gate 4**. ARG's defence, per `C-950` §5.1, is that the delays are attributable to **the Authority's own variation of the certification protocol** and to late release of vehicles. |

### 4.2 The question

**A retrofit programme running 40 weeks late on two delivery gates, with a fixed depot, a fixed workforce and
standing overhead, became £4.1m cheaper to complete in the same quarter — and the event ARG relies upon to
explain the cost saving is the same event ARG relies upon to explain the delay.**

Both propositions may be true. A narrowed test protocol can reduce certification cost and cause programme
delay simultaneously. **But a 40-week delay on a contract of this type ordinarily increases cost, and the
audit file contains no consideration of the point at all.**

### 4.3 Where it was asked, and where it was not

| Document | What it asked | Level |
|---|---|---|
| `C-100` §8.1 | Whether the claim affects the contract price, makes the contract loss-making, or requires a provision | 1 |
| `C-950` §5.4 | Whether the claim makes the contract loss-making or impairs the contract asset | 1 |
| **`C-950` §5.4, fourth row** | **"Does the claim indicate that the estimate of costs to complete may be understated?" — recorded as "Not addressed"** | 1 |
| `MRN-043` | Whether the claim makes the contract loss-making | 2 |
| `PRN-16` | Whether a provision is required and the disclosure is in | 3 |
| `INSP-F05` | Lists the delay claim among contrary evidence not addressed, without putting the causal question | 4 |
| **This document** | **The question** | **5** |

`C-950` §5.4 contains a row asking exactly the right question and the answer recorded against it is **"Not
addressed"**. The workpaper identified the question and left it open. Nobody who reviewed the workpaper
noticed that the row was blank.

### 4.4 The related unreconciled item

`C-700` §5.3 records journal **JE-12047**: £0.90m, posted by the Chief Financial Officer at 23:47 on
31 December 20X4, in a round sum, without approval, debiting the CN-4471 contract asset and crediting
Equipment & Systems revenue, with a one-line manuscript support. It met **six of the fourteen** CAAT criteria
— more than any other item in a population of 42,118 manual journals.

`MRN-037` asked whether the £0.90m is part of the £3.5m estimate change or additional to it. The answer
recorded was *"do not double-count"*. `PRN-13` asked what the answer was and received the mechanism, not the
reconciliation.

**No workpaper reconciles the £0.90m to the £3.5m. Whether it is inside or outside remains undetermined.**

### 4.5 What subsequently happened

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 28 Apr 20X5 | CN-4471 forecast costs increased by **£3.2m** — reversing 78% of the Q4 reduction within four months |
| 14 Nov 20X5 | **MRA delay claim settled at £4.1m against ARG** — a claim management had assessed as "without merit" and expected to resist in full |
| 3 Mar 20X6 | FY20X5 results: cumulative adverse variance on CN-4471 of **£9.1m**; contract margin restated from 22.4% to **11.4%** |

**The FRAOA does not rely on any of this to find that the audit was deficient.** The finding at `FRAOA-F04`
rests on the evidence in the file on 12 March 20X5. These events are recorded because they bear on the
grading decision at `I-500`, which was taken with knowledge of the first of them.

### 6.3 The FRAOA's assessment of intent

**The FRAOA does not find that any individual added `C-100/13` in order to mislead the inspection.**

> *"I did the analysis in May because it was on the list of matters for next year at `D-200` §14 and I had the

That account is credible and consistent with the timing: the modification was made seven days after the lock,

matter expressly carried forward at `D-200` §14. **A person concealing a gap does not first write down that
the gap exists.**

The engagement partner's evidence is that he found the document in August while preparing his response and did
not check its date. He withdrew his contest of the finding on 20X6-03-24 (`I-600` §5 addendum) and has
reported the matter under the firm's document integrity protocol.

## 7. Evidence of the ARG Audit Committee Chair

Interviewed under s.47 on 20X6-04-02. The firm's own inspection could not interview her (`R-100` §4.4).

Dame Rosemary Ndlovu-Frost, a former audit partner, was shown the relevant extracts of `C-700`, `C-800` and
`D-400/5`.

> *"I asked the question in private session for a reason. We had a Chief Financial Officer in her second year
> who was extremely capable and whose incentive arrangements were tied to adjusted earnings per share in a
> measurement period ending the following year. That is a situation an audit committee watches. I asked in
> private session precisely so that the auditor could tell me something he could not say in front of
> management, and the answer I was given was that there was nothing.*
>
> *I have now read the workpaper. There were payments of £2.4m to a haulage company owned by her brother-in-law
> which are not in the related party note, no tender, rates above the alternatives, and £680,000 paid outside
> the payment run. **The Committee set no materiality for that. The Committee would have wanted to know about
> £50,000 of it.***
>
> *I want to be fair to Mr Belliveau, because I have read his account and I accept it. He did not know. His
> manager had not told him. He answered honestly on what he had.*
>
> ***But that is a worse answer than if he had known and said nothing, not a better one.*** *If he had known
> and told me nothing, that would be one partner's misjudgement. What actually happened is that his firm knew,
> in writing, in two workpapers, and the information could not get from a workpaper to the partner across four
> weeks. **We did not ask a question of a man. We asked it of a firm, and the firm had the answer and could not
> produce it.***
>
> *On the disclosure point I would add this. The Committee concluded that the enhanced narrative on the
> goodwill sensitivity was sufficient and I recorded that I would have preferred the quantified disclosure. I
> was in a minority of one. **Had I known about the haulage payments I would not have been in a minority of
> one about anything that year**, because I would have been asking a different set of questions about what else
> we had not been told."*

### 7.1 FRAOA assessment

The evidence is material in three respects.

**First**, it establishes that the information was material to those charged with governance in the sense that
matters. The Committee's own view is that it would have wanted to know about £50,000 of it. The firm assessed
£2.4m against a Group materiality of £3.10m (`INSP-IP04`) and did not mention that a specific materiality of
£0.100m had been set for exactly this purpose.

**Second**, it identifies the failure correctly. ISA 260 obligations run from the auditor to those charged
with governance. **A firm that holds information in two workpapers and cannot get it to the engagement partner
in four weeks has not discharged that obligation, whatever the individual partner knew.**

**Third**, it establishes a consequence the firm's inspection did not consider. `I-700` §4 records that the
question whether the Committee should be informed that the answer was incomplete was **"not considered"**.
The FRAOA has required it at `R-600` action 12.
