## 3. The nine representations

### 3.1 Representation 1 — the characterisation of the grade change · **REJECTED**

**The firm submitted:**

> *"The reclassification of four findings followed a legitimate technical process. A written rationale exists for
> each, as `AV-QM-07` requires, and each was approved by the Head of Quality. The FRAOA's language at `R-200`
> §2 — that the firm 'declined to record what it saw' — implies a deliberate suppression which the evidence does
> not support and which the FRAOA itself does not find. We ask that the language be moderated."*

**FRAOA reply:**

The FRAOA has considered this carefully and does not accept it, but records two clarifications.

**First**, the FRAOA makes **no finding of deliberate suppression**, and says so expressly at `R-200` §8.3: the
evidence points away from it. The engagement partner asked for the grade to remain at 3 and asked that the
finding against himself not be softened; both requests went the other way. The two changes he did request that
would have been adverse to the firm's reported quality were refused.

**Second**, the finding does not depend on intent. It depends on this: six findings became two, no fact changed,
two of the four rationales are factually wrong, the meeting was unminuted, and the dissent of the inspector who
performed a third of the fieldwork was filed and never referred to. **The language at `R-200` §2 describes an
outcome, and the outcome is accurately described.**

The FRAOA has added one sentence to `R-200` §8.3 making the absence of a finding of pressure more prominent.
Otherwise unchanged.

---

### 3.2 Representation 2 — reliance on `C-100/13` · **REJECTED**

**The firm submitted:**

> *"Our lead inspector relied on `C-100/13` in good faith. The engagement partner drew it to his attention in
> good faith. Neither knew it had been added post-assembly. The FRAOA accepts that the senior in charge did not
> add it to mislead. The finding at `FRAOA-F13` should therefore be recorded as a control failure rather than as
> a failure of the inspection process."*

**FRAOA reply:**

**It is recorded as both, and both are correct.**

`FRAOA-F13` states in terms that the FRAOA makes no finding of intent against any individual, and gives the
reason: *"A person concealing a gap does not first write down that the gap exists."*

The finding against the inspection process is separate and stands. **The firm's inspection had already
demonstrated, in the same report, that it knew how to make this test.** `INSP-IP05` tests a review note timing
discrepancy against system metadata and resolves it correctly. The same test was available and was not applied to
the one document being used to downgrade the report's most significant substantive finding.

The FRAOA also notes that the firm's own inspector **raised the timing of `C-100/13` on 12 September 20X5** and
was told *"the file is what it is and we review it as we find it"*. The question was asked inside the firm, four
months before the FRAOA asked it, and was closed.

Rejected.

### 3.3 Representation 3 — the incentive analysis · **REJECTED**

**The firm submitted:**

> *"The FRAOA's analysis at `R-500` §5.4, deriving an 'expected remuneration consequence of approximately 0.13%',
> is speculative. No evidence suggests that any partner performs such a calculation. The firm objects to the
> implication that its remuneration framework causes audit failure."*

**FRAOA reply:**

The FRAOA makes **no such implication** and says so at `R-500` §5.4: *"The FRAOA does not find that Mr Belliveau
consciously weighed those incentives."*

**The finding is that the firm has never asked the question.** `I-400` §5.2 excluded the remuneration framework
from the root cause analysis of a quality failure on the basis that it *"is a matter for the Remuneration
Committee"*. That exclusion was determined by the Head of Quality, who sits on that Committee.

The FRAOA's finding is not that the framework caused this failure. It is that a firm which weights quality at 4%
against practice unit financial performance at 34%, in which a grade 2 outcome attracts no reduction at all and
an uninspected partner scores full marks by default, and which excludes that framework from the scope of its own
root cause analysis, has not tested a candidate cause. **Required action 8 requires the firm to test it
independently. The FRAOA has not prejudged the answer.**

Rejected. The FRAOA has added the words *"and the FRAOA has not prejudged the outcome of that review"* to action
8.

### 3.4 Representation 4 — the assessment of the fieldwork · **ACCEPTED IN PART**

**The firm submitted:**

> *"The draft report is fair to our inspectors' technical work at `R-200` §3 but the balance of the document, and
> in particular the summary at `R-800`, does not convey that our own inspectors identified twelve of the FRAOA's
> fifteen findings, re-projected the sampling error, found the covenant breach in a hidden column that four
> reviewers had missed, and established that the National Technical Panel's advice had been mischaracterised. A
> reader of the published extract would conclude that our monitoring function failed to detect these matters. It
> detected almost all of them."*

**FRAOA reply — ACCEPTED IN PART.**

The representation is well made. **The distinction between a firm that cannot detect its failures and a firm
that detects them and does not act is central to the FRAOA's own reasoning, and the draft extract did not carry
it.**

The FRAOA has amended `R-800` to state expressly that the firm's inspectors identified twelve of the FRAOA's
fifteen findings in draft, and to state that the deficiency lies in what happened to those findings rather than
in the capability of the monitoring function.

The FRAOA has not amended the assessment of *not effective*, which does not depend on detection.

### 3.6 Representation 6 — action 14, the EQR system control · **REJECTED**

**The firm submitted:**

> *"The firm accepts that this control should have been implemented and does not defend the deferrals. However,
> the deadline of 31 December 20X6 is not achievable. The engagement management system is a network platform and
> a change of this nature requires the agreement of the network's technology governance board, which meets
> twice a year. We request a deadline of 30 September 20X7."*

**FRAOA reply:**

Rejected.

| Cycle | Identified | Action agreed | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20X3 | ✔ | System block | Deferred |
| 20X4 | ✔ | Same action | Deferred |
| 20X5 | ✔ | Same action, target 31 Mar 20X6 | Open at 20X6-04-24 |
| **20X6** | | **Required under s.61, deadline 31 Dec 20X6** | |

The action has been agreed by the firm three times over three cycles and implemented once — not at all. **The
platform constraint the firm now advances existed in 20X3, 20X4 and 20X5 and was not advanced then.**

The FRAOA notes that the control need not be a platform change to be effective. **A daily exception report of
audit reports dated while an EQR sign-off is outstanding requires no network governance approval and would
identify every instance.** The firm's inspectors produced exactly such an analysis for seven engagements in July
20X5.

**Deadline unchanged. No extension will be granted.** The FRAOA has added the sentence above regarding an
interim exception report to action 14.

### 3.7 Representation 7 — the Larkspur matter and legal privilege · **REJECTED**

**The firm submitted:**

> *"Required action 11 directs a referral to the firm's General Counsel and requires the firm to report the
> conclusion and the basis of it to the FRAOA. Any such conclusion will be legally privileged. The firm will
> comply with the referral but cannot waive privilege in the advice."*

**FRAOA reply:**

The FRAOA does not require the firm to produce privileged legal advice.

Action 11 requires the firm to report **the conclusion reached and the basis of it** — that is, whether the firm
has determined that any reporting obligation arises, and the factual matters taken into account. That is not
privileged advice; it is the firm's own decision about its own obligations.

Action 11 stands as drafted. The FRAOA has added: *"The firm is not required to produce privileged legal advice.
It is required to state its conclusion and the factual basis on which it was reached."*

### 3.8 Representation 8 — the ARG Audit Committee · **REJECTED**

**The firm submitted:**

> *"Required action 12 directs the firm to determine whether to inform the Audit Committee that the answer given
> in private session was incomplete. The firm is no longer the auditor of the entity for the purposes of that
> engagement year, the engagement partner has rotated off, and the matter is more than three years old. The firm
> submits that the action risks disturbing a client relationship to no evident benefit."*

**FRAOA reply:**

Rejected, and the FRAOA records its concern at the terms of the representation.

**The FRAOA has not directed the outcome.** Action 12 requires the firm to *determine and report with reasons*
whether it will inform the Committee. The firm may properly conclude that it will not. What the FRAOA requires is
that **the question be answered by someone with the authority to answer it**, which is precisely what did not
happen when the firm's own inspection recorded the matter as *"not considered"* at `I-700` §4.

The FRAOA draws the firm's attention to the evidence of the Audit Committee Chair at `R-300` §7:

> *"We did not ask a question of a man. We asked it of a firm, and the firm had the answer and could not produce
> it."*

**A representation that answering her question risks "disturbing a client relationship to no evident benefit"
is, in the FRAOA's view, an illustration of the cultural matter at `R-500` §3 rather than an answer to it.**

Action 12 stands. The FRAOA has recorded this exchange in the clearance meeting minute at the firm's request.

### 3.9 Representation 9 — publication · **REJECTED**

**The firm submitted:**

> *"The firm requests that the assessment of its monitoring and remediation process as 'not effective' be
> published as 'improvements required'. The former is the FRAOA's lowest assessment and has been applied to only
> two firms in the last six years. The firm's monitoring function is materially more capable than that
> assessment conveys, as Representation 4 sets out, and the assessment will be read by audit committees as a
> statement about the reliability of our audits generally."*

**FRAOA reply:**

Rejected.

The assessment does not turn on the capability of the monitoring function, which the FRAOA has assessed as good
and has amended `R-800` to say so. It turns on the seven requirements tested at `R-500` §7, of which the firm
meets **one** — root cause identification.

The determinative matters are that:

- the monitoring process generated accurate information that did not reach the firm's governance;
- the grading was unsupportable on the firm's own criterion and its own two retained findings;
- two of the three root causes the firm itself weighted "high" have no remedial action at all;
- 23 actions are open, 13 carried from prior cycles, 8 outstanding more than two cycles;
- **no remedial action has ever been tested for effectiveness**, which is an express ISQM 1 requirement; and
- the single control that would have prevented one of the two findings the firm did report has been agreed three
  times and built never.

**A monitoring and remediation process that satisfies one of seven requirements is not "improvements required".**

Publication proceeds as drafted, as amended by Representations 4 and 5.
