## 2. Why the actions are directed where they are

The FRAOA has issued no required action in respect of the audit of Auburn Ridge Group plc.

That is deliberate. The FY20X4 audit is complete, the opinion has been issued, the engagement partner has
rotated off, and the FY20X5 audit — performed by a different partner — identified and corrected the substantive
consequences of the FY20X4 deficiencies. Actions E6 and E7 of the firm's own remediation plan produced the
look-back that found a £9.1m adverse variance and the discount rate reassessment that produced £21.0m of
impairment.

**The FRAOA's actions are directed at the firm's monitoring and remediation process, its governance, and its
document integrity controls, because that is where the failure lies that is capable of recurring.** `R-500` §7.

## 6. The audit manager — a statement the FRAOA considers necessary

The FRAOA does not ordinarily comment on individuals in whose respect it makes no finding. It does so here
because the firm's draft report proposed a finding against the audit manager, because that finding was
constructed from documents she wrote criticising herself, and because the FRAOA is concerned about what the
episode would teach if left unaddressed.

### 6.1 The facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Her review was inadequate | **Yes.** 44.6% coverage; two significant-risk workpapers unopened; 19 of 49 notes cleared on explanation alone; 11 self-cleared in breach of `AV-AUD-14`; a conclusion at `C-800` §9 asserting a tender that does not exist; wrong advice on the projection question; and a £2.4m related party matter not escalated. |
| She identified those failures herself | **Yes — six of them, individually, at `M-100` §6, before the report was signed.** |
| **She wrote three documents the methodology does not require** | **`M-100`, `M-300`, `M-400`.** The firm's template is a one-page coverage sign-off. |
| **She told the engagement partner, in writing, that her own advice had been wrong and that the misstatement figure was materially larger** | **`M-300` §2, 21:15 on 11 March 20X5** |
| **She told him about Larkspur in the same document** | **Yes** |
| He read neither | **Opened for four minutes each** |
| She asked him twice for the going concern consultation memorandum | **Never provided** |
| She was resourced at 118 hours against a budget of 158 | **Yes — the missing 40 being those added to supervise a first-year senior in charge** |
| She reported the resourcing position to him twice before he signed | **Yes** |
| She was carrying three concurrent December year-ends | **214 hours across three files in the fortnight to 11 March** |

### 6.2 The FRAOA's position

**Most of what the FRAOA and the firm's inspectors know about the failure of this engagement, they know because
the audit manager wrote it down.** `M-100` §6 is the list of inadequate clearances that `P-200` later grades and
that `INSP-F04` was built from. `M-300` §2 is the document that identifies the projection error, four months
before the firm's inspectors re-derived it.

The FRAOA **agrees with the firm's decision to reclassify `INSP-F04`** — the only one of the four
reclassifications it endorses — and with the engagement partner's reasoning at `I-600` §3:

> *"A finding against the manager for the quality of a review she documented as inadequate, and which she
> escalated to a partner who did not read the escalation, allocates this to the only person in the chain who did
> their job in the respect that matters most: she wrote it down and she told someone."*

**The FRAOA makes no finding against her and has made no referral in respect of her conduct.**

### 6.3 Why this is a systemic point and not a courtesy

A firm's system of quality management depends on people recording problems accurately and passing them upward.
That behaviour is discretionary, unrewarded and personally costly.

**On this engagement it happened twice and was punished or ignored on both occasions:**

| | The audit manager | The dissenting inspector |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded an accurate written account of a failure | ✔ `M-100`, `M-300` | ✔ `I-500/5` |
| Sent it to the person responsible | ✔ | ✔ (filed for the report) |
| **Read and acted upon** | **✖ — four minutes** | **✖ — never referred to** |
| Consequence to the author | **A proposed adverse inspection finding** | None recorded; not promoted; not referred to |
| Consequence to the recipient | Finding softened at his own request declined | Grading changed as he approved |

**If the lesson a firm's people draw is that documenting your own inadequacy attracts a finding while declining
to read the documentation attracts a mitigation paragraph, the firm will not receive that information again.**

The FRAOA regards this as directly relevant to the assessment at `R-500` §7 and has framed required action 2
accordingly.

### 6.4 The assistant

The FRAOA records one further matter.

`FRAOA-F10` — £2.4m paid to an entity controlled by the Chief Financial Officer's brother-in-law, undisclosed in
the related party note — is identifiable at all because a **first-year audit assistant** performed a companies
registry search nobody asked him to perform, cross-referred the registered office to the CFO's own related party
questionnaire, worked out the relationship, and then recorded in writing on the workpaper, twice, that his
manager's conclusion did not match the evidence on the file.

He also proposed, at the fraud discussion in September 20X4, the three CAAT criteria that found the payments.

**Of the eight individuals whose work the FRAOA has examined on this engagement, the one with the least
experience and the least authority is the one whose conduct requires no qualification whatsoever.**
