## 2. Why this inspection is different from Level 4

The firm's own inspection at Level 4 asked: *was this audit performed in accordance with professional
standards?*

The FRAOA asks two questions, and the second is the one that dominates this report:

| | Question | Where answered |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Was this audit performed in accordance with professional standards?** | `R-300`, `R-400` |
| **2** | **Does the firm have a functioning system of quality management — including a monitoring process capable of identifying its own failures and a remediation process capable of correcting them?** | `R-200`, `R-500` |

The distinction matters because of what the FRAOA found. On question 1 the FRAOA largely agrees with the
firm's own inspectors: the substantive audit failures the firm identified in **draft** were correctly
identified. **The FRAOA's most serious findings concern what happened to those findings between 12 August and
30 September 20X5.**

> A firm whose monitoring function cannot detect its failures has a detection problem, which is remediable by
> better inspectors and more of them.
>
> **A firm whose monitoring function detects its failures accurately and then reclassifies them has a
> different problem, and it is not remediable by better inspectors.**

> manager who performed 44 of the firm's 141 inspection hours, recording that the report had moved from six

## 7. The FRAOA's treatment of hindsight

This deserves a section of its own, because the firm's inspection relied on post-report-date events to
downgrade two findings (`I-500` §4.4, §4.5) and the FRAOA does not accept that reliance.

**The FRAOA's position:**

| Proposition | FRAOA view |
|---|---|
| An auditor is judged on the evidence available at the date of the report | **Agreed, without qualification** |
| A subsequent favourable outcome cannot supply evidence the auditor did not have | **Agreed.** The RCF term sheet arrived on 22 April 20X5. It cannot bear on the sufficiency of evidence held on 12 March. `FRAOA-F08`. |
| A subsequent adverse outcome does not, of itself, prove the audit was deficient | **Agreed.** The FRAOA does not find against the engagement team because the CGU was later impaired or because the contract later overran. |
| **A subsequent adverse outcome is relevant to assessing whether a *risk* was properly evaluated, and is relevant to the reliability of a *grading decision* taken with knowledge of it** | **Agreed, and this is the distinction that matters here** |

**Applied to this engagement:**

| Post-report-date event | Used against the engagement team? | Used to assess the grading decision? |
|---|:-:|:-:|
| Q1 20X5: CN-4471 costs +£3.2m (28 Apr 20X5) | **No** | **Yes** — known to the firm's inspectors before the draft report |
| £16.2m impairment of the Industrial Systems CGU (6 Aug 20X5) | **No** | **Yes** — six days before the draft report |
| RCF term sheet (22 Apr 20X5) | **No** | **Yes** — the firm used it to downgrade a finding, which the FRAOA rejects |
| MRA claim settled at £4.1m against ARG (14 Nov 20X5) | **No** | **Yes** — after the final report; relevant to remediation |
| FY20X5: further £5.9m overrun; material uncertainty disclosed (3 Mar 20X6) | **No** | **Yes** — relevant to whether grade 2 was defensible |

**Every finding in `R-400` against the engagement team is made on evidence that was in the audit file, or
capable of being obtained, on or before 12 March 20X5.** The FRAOA has tested each finding against that
standard and says so, finding by finding, at `R-400` §3.
