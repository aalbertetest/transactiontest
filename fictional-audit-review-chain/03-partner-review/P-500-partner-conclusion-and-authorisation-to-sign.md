### B3. What actually went wrong at this level

I want to separate four things, because the inspection report at `I-300` runs them together and I think the
distinction matters for whether anything changes.

**First, the hours.** 11.5 hours of documented file review against a benchmark of 63 to 94. That number is
indefensible and I do not defend it. But I have thought about it a good deal and I do not think more hours
spent the way I spent them would have found much. I read the summaries. Reading them more slowly would have
produced the same conclusions.

**Second, what I reviewed.** I reviewed a set of documents each of which was a summary prepared by the
person whose work I was reviewing. `D-200`, `D-100`, `D-400` — all accurate, all prepared by the manager.
A summary can tell you what someone concluded. It cannot tell you what they did not look at. **I never once
asked what had not been looked at.**

**Third, the twenty minutes I did not spend.** This is the one I would put at the top. The review notes
register was one click from the dashboard I did look at. It would have shown me: a note on `C-800` raised
and cleared the same day, zero notes on two significant-risk workpapers, three going concern notes none of
which produced evidence, and eleven notes closed by preparers. **Any one of those four observations,
followed for ten minutes, leads to something I missed. All four were visible in a table I never opened.**

**Fourth, the eight minutes.** The manager sent me two documents at 21:15 on 11 March. One of them told me
in terms that her advice on the cut-off projection had been wrong and that the misstatement number was
materially larger. The other listed six of her own clearances as inadequate. I opened them for four minutes
each and read neither. She had also asked me three times for a consultation memorandum I never wrote.

### B4. The loose end on independence

`A-200` §6 records that the consulting engagement's recommendations included changes to Aventine role
definitions, and that whether the independence safeguard extends to the access provisioning controls relied
upon at `B-200` **"has not been separately evaluated"**.

The manager records at `M-300` §7 that she could not evaluate it because she had not read `B-200`. I did
read `B-200`, for twenty minutes, and I did not evaluate it either. The Ethics Partner's memorandum
expressly made it my responsibility: *"The engagement partner should satisfy himself at completion that the
IT audit workpaper identifies the affected controls and that no reliance has been placed on them."*

I confirmed the safeguard had operated in respect of purchase-to-pay, which was substantive anyway. I did
not consider whether it extended to the controls we did rely on.

### B5. What I would say to the inspection team, and to whoever reads this afterwards

Two of the draft findings I will contest at `I-600`, on points of fact rather than substance.

On everything else my position is this. **The three failures that matter at this level were each preventable
by an act taking less than half an hour**: opening the review notes register, reading a memorandum
addressed to me, and writing down a telephone consultation. None required more budget, more staff or more
time in the timetable. Two of the three were requested of me directly by the person below me.

I am aware how that sounds as a defence and it is not offered as one. It is offered because the remedial
actions being drafted at `I-700` are about hours and coverage percentages, and I do not think hours and
coverage percentages are what failed here. **A partner who reviews only what the level below him chooses to
show him will fail at any number of hours.**

## Reader's note

Part A is what the audit file contains. Part B is what the same person wrote five months later, once an
inspection team had spent 141 hours on a file he had reviewed for 11.5.

The distance between them is not new information. Almost everything in Part B was derivable on
11 March 20X5 from documents that existed on 11 March 20X5. What changed between March and August was not
the evidence. It was that somebody was required to look.

That is the argument for a fourth level of review, and it is also — once you notice that the inspection
team's own findings were downgraded three weeks after they were drafted — the argument for a fifth.
