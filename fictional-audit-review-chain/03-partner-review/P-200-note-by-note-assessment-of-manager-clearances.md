### D1 — `MRN-019` · Revenue cut-off projection · `ISS-02`

| | |
|---|---|
| **The note asked** | Whether the £1.90m of errors found in a sample of 40 needed extrapolating to the population. |
| **The response** | *"Discussed with SL, who advised that as we have identified the actual errors, extrapolation would double-count."* |
| **Cleared** | Same day. By the preparer. |
| **Why the clearance fails** | The advice is wrong as a matter of sampling. The sample covered 27.1% of the population by value; the errors found are the errors in that 27.1%. Projection estimates the misstatement in the untested 72.9%. It is not double-counting. |
| **Compounding factor** | `C-200` §6 expressly concludes the errors are **"not anomalous"**. Firm methodology `AV-AUD-22` §4.6 requires projection once that conclusion is reached. The workpaper reached the conclusion that triggers the requirement and then did not apply it. |
| **Consequence** | Ratio projection: £1.90m ÷ £84.6m × £312.4m = **£7.02m**. Substituted into `D-100`, uncorrected misstatements become **£7.47m** against materiality of £3.10m — **2.4× materiality**. |
| **What this means for the opinion** | An unmodified opinion is not available on those figures without either an adjustment or a modification. **This is the finding that goes to whether the opinion was appropriate.** |
| **Could I have found it?** | Yes. `M-300` §2 told me in terms, at 21:15 on 11 March. I opened the document for four minutes and did not read it. Independently, the register showed the note cleared same-day by the preparer on a bare explanation. |

### D2 — `MRN-039` · Larkspur Freight Ltd · `ISS-08`

| | |
|---|---|
| **The note asked** | What our conclusion was on £2.4m paid to an entity whose sole director is the CFO's brother-in-law, with no tender, rates 4–7% above comparators, and £680k paid outside the payment run. |
| **The response** | The CFO's characterisation of the relationship, accepted. |
| **Cleared** | Same day. |
| **Why the clearance fails** | Three reasons, each sufficient. **First**, the conclusion the manager wrote at `C-800` §9 states the supplier was engaged "following competitive tender". There is no tender on the file; there is a single-source justification form. The assistant said so in writing on the workpaper. **Second**, the IAS 24 analysis was never performed — `C-800` §3.7 lists five questions and records that none was addressed. | **Consequence** | Potential undisclosed related party transaction of £2.4m, being **24× the specific materiality we set for related parties**. Potential non-compliance with Companies Act s.412. A fraud risk factor that should have triggered reassessment under ISA 240 and did not. |
| **The aggravating consequence** | I told the Audit Committee Chair in private session on 11 March that we had encountered no matter concerning the Chief Financial Officer. That statement was accurate on my knowledge and inaccurate on the firm's. |
| **Could I have found it?** | Yes. The register shows one note on `C-800`, cleared the same day. That is the pattern that should make a partner open the workpaper. `M-300` §2 also told me directly. |

### D3 — `MRN-014` · CN-4471 estimate · `ISS-01`

| | |

| **My own contribution** | I raised `PRN-06`, `PRN-07` and `PRN-08` on this workpaper and spent 2.5 hours on it, more than on any other. **My notes asked the same questions the manager's note had already asked, received the same answers, and I cleared them the same day.** See `P-300`. |
| **Could I have found it?** | I did find it. I asked about it. Then I accepted the answer that had already failed to satisfy the reviewer below me. |

*Six of the nine grade C notes were self-cleared by the preparer in breach of `AV-AUD-14`. The
correlation between self-clearance and inadequate clearance is not a coincidence: a preparer closing a
note on their own work is, by construction, not a review.*

**Two observations.**

The first is that the quality of the review tracks the difficulty of the answer, not the importance of the
question. Inventory and goodwill produced fifteen good notes because the questions there had findable
answers: back-test the percentages, compare the forecast to the outturn, build the sensitivity table. The
six grade D notes are all questions whose honest answer was "we do not have the evidence and getting it
will cost time we have not got".

The second is that **the two areas with no notes at all are not on this table's problem list, and they
should be at the top of it.** `B-200` and `C-500` between them contain `ISS-04` and `ISS-05`. A grading
exercise that only grades the notes that were raised will always miss the workpapers where nobody raised
any, and my 20 minutes on `B-200` is the entire review that workpaper received from anyone at Level 2 or
Level 3.

## 6. My conclusion on this exercise

Of the 49 notes, six were cleared on a basis that does not support the conclusion, and in one case
(`MRN-019`) the correct treatment changes the misstatement total from below materiality to 2.4× it.

**The manager identified all six of those notes herself, in writing, before the report was signed.** She
did so at `M-100` §6, in a document she was not required to write, and she summarised the three most
serious at `M-300` §2, in a memorandum addressed to me. I opened both documents for a combined eight
minutes on the night before signing and read neither.

The inspection team's draft report attributes these matters to the manager's clearances. I do not accept
that characterisation and I say so at `I-600`. The clearances were inadequate and she says so. But the
purpose of a third level of review is to catch a second level that has gone wrong under pressure, and the
second level here told the third level exactly where to look.

**The review below me failed and documented its own failure. The review that was me did not read the
documentation.**
