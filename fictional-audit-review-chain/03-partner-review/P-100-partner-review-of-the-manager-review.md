---

> **A note on what this document is.** The firm's methodology does not require a partner to review the
> manager's review as such. `AV-AUD-11` requires the engagement partner to review specified *workpapers*
> — those addressing significant risks, the critical accounting judgements, and the completion documents.
> Nothing in the methodology requires him to test whether the manager's clearances of her own review notes
> were sound.
>
> That gap is the subject of this level. What follows is the partner's contemporaneous review record as
> archived, supplemented at §7 with the e-file audit trail extracted by the internal inspection team in
> July 20X5. The two together show a partner who reviewed the file's *conclusions* and did not review the
> *review*, and who therefore inherited every conclusion the manager had already accepted.

## 2. How I approached this review

I opened the file on 6 March. The draft financial statements had arrived on 3 March, five working days
later than planned, and Sasha had four days rather than five for her review. I had two.

I signed three other listed audit reports in the seven days to 12 March: Marchmont Holdings on 7 March,
the Tessendorf Group on 10 March and Caldermoor plc on 11 March. ARG is the largest of the four.

I approached ARG on the basis that it was the fourth year of the engagement, that I knew the business,
that Sasha had run the file for two years and knows it better than I do, and that the significant risks
were the same significant risks as last year with the addition of the acquisition. I read the completion
memorandum, the misstatement schedule, the Audit Committee report and the financial statements first, and
then went to the workpapers behind the judgements those documents disclosed.

That is a reasonable way to review a file when the review below you has been thorough. It is the wrong way
to review a file when it has not been, because it means everything I see has already been filtered.

*(The preceding sentence was added on 20X5-08-29 in the course of preparing the engagement team response
to the draft internal inspection report. The remainder of §2 is contemporaneous.)*

### 6.2 What "all notes closed" told me, and what it did not

The dashboard in the engagement management system shows review notes as a count and a status. On 11 March
it showed 49 raised, 49 closed, none open, and a green indicator.

I took that as evidence that the manager's review was complete. It is evidence that it was **finished**.

Had I opened the register, I would have seen:

| What the register shows | What I would have concluded |
|---|---|
| 19 of 49 notes closed on explanation alone, with no new evidence | That over a third of the review produced no additional audit evidence |
| 11 notes closed by the preparer rather than the reviewer | A breach of `AV-AUD-14`, which prohibits self-clearance |
| 11 notes closed on the day they were raised | That a proportion of the review was not a review |
| Zero notes on `B-200` and zero on `C-500` | That two significant-risk workpapers had not been reviewed by anybody, and that my 20 minutes on `B-200` was the only review it received |
| Zero of three going concern notes closed on new evidence | That the going concern conclusion rested entirely on explanation |
| One note on `C-800`, cleared the same day | That there was something on `C-800` worth a note |

**None of that is hidden. It is in a register in the system, one click from the dashboard I did look at.**

### 6.3 On the manager's hours

`A-100` §4.3, which I approved in September, records that 40 hours were added to the manager's budget
specifically because the senior in charge was new to the role. The purpose of those hours was additional
direction and supervision of a first-year in-charge on a listed group audit.

She worked 118 hours. The budget was 158.

She told me the position verbally on 26 February and again on 6 March. `A-700` §6 records it. `D-200` §9.3
records it. `M-100` §3.1 records it. I did not act on it, and I did not consider what it meant for the
reliability of the review I was relying on.

What it meant is arithmetically obvious. The 40 hours added to supervise a new senior were the 40 hours
that were not worked. The supervision I priced into the engagement in September did not happen, and I was
told so twice before I signed.

## 7. `M-100` and `M-300`

The manager prepared two documents that were not required by the methodology: `M-100`, her review record,
and `M-300`, her conclusion memorandum. Both were franker than the template required. `M-100` §6 lists six
of her own notes that she identifies as inadequately cleared. `M-300` §2 states three things in terms:

the cut-off projection was wrong and that on any projection basis the misstatement number is materially

brother-in-law.

### 7.2 My account

I gave the following account to the internal inspection team on 22 July 20X5 and I stand by it as a
statement of fact, not as a defence:

> *"I opened both documents on my phone at home on the evening of the 11th. I had signed Caldermoor that
> afternoon and I was reading ARG in the expectation of signing it the following morning. I saw that they
> were long. I formed the impression, from the first screen of each, that they were Sasha being
> characteristically thorough about her own performance, and I intended to read them properly after
> signing.*
>
> *I did not read them. I did not see the projection point at `M-300` §2. I did not see Larkspur.*
>
> *I want to be accurate about the sequence, because it matters. The Audit Committee Chair asked me in
> private session on the afternoon of the 11th whether we had encountered anything concerning the Chief
> Financial Officer. I said we had not. That was true on what I knew. Sasha sent me the memorandum telling
> me otherwise at 21:15 that evening, which was after the meeting. She says at `M-300` §2 that she should
> have told me on 7 March and she is right about that.*
>
> *But the reason it mattered that she tell me on 7 March is that I was not going to find it any other
> way. I had not opened `C-800`. I had not opened the review notes register. **The only route by which
> that fact could have reached me was her deciding to tell me, and a system in which a material matter
> reaches the engagement partner only if one person chooses to mention it is not a system.** I am not
> saying that to shift it onto the firm. I am saying it because I think it is the finding."*

## 10. What I would say about this review

*Added 20X5-08-29 for the engagement team response at `I-600`.*

The findings in the draft inspection report are, with two exceptions I address at `I-600`, correct.

The one I would put first is not on the inspection team's list in the form I would put it. It is this: **I
reviewed the conclusions of a review I had not evaluated.** Every document I read on 10 and 11 March —
the completion memorandum, the misstatement schedule, the Audit Committee report — was a summary prepared
by the person whose review I was relying on. They were accurate summaries of what she had concluded. They
could not tell me what she had not looked at, and I never asked.

Eleven and a half hours is the number that will be quoted. I do not dispute it and it is indefensible
against a benchmark of 63 to 94. But I do not think more hours spent the same way would have found
Larkspur or the projection point. **What would have found them is opening the review notes register,
which would have taken twenty minutes.** The register showed a note on `C-800` cleared the same day it was
raised, and zero notes on two significant-risk workpapers. Either of those, followed up, leads straight
to the two things I missed.

The firm's methodology told me to review nine workpapers. It did not tell me to review the review. On this
engagement that was the difference.
