# RR-001 — Failure to Identify Distinct Performance Obligations in Bundled Software and Implementation Arrangements

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Finding reference | RR-001 |
| Entity (fictional) | Vantera Systems Group, Inc. |
| Sector / industry | Enterprise software and implementation services |
| Fiscal year audited | Year ended December 31, 20X4 |
| Framework(s) | ASC 606-10-25-14 through 25-22; ASC 606-10-32-31; COSO 2013 Principles 10 and 12 |
| Assertion(s) affected | Occurrence, cutoff, accuracy, presentation and classification |
| Quantified effect | Revenue overstated by $18.4 million; deferred revenue understated by $18.4 million |
| Risk rating | High |
| Repeat finding | Yes (prior-year reference RR-PY-09, reported as a significant deficiency) |

## Background

Vantera Systems Group, Inc. licenses a suite of enterprise resource planning software to
mid-market manufacturers and distributors, and it sells that software almost exclusively
in bundles that pair a term license with a substantial implementation engagement. For the
year ended December 31, 20X4, the entity reported total revenue of $412.6 million,
composed of $214.9 million of license revenue, $138.2 million of professional services
revenue, and $59.5 million of post contract support revenue. Roughly 71 percent of new
license revenue in the year originated from arrangements in which the customer purchased
the license and the implementation services under a single master agreement with a single
negotiated price, and in which the customer had not previously deployed any Vantera
product. Contracting is centralized in a nine person deal desk that reports to the Chief
Revenue Officer rather than to the Chief Financial Officer, and pricing discretion up to a
25 percent aggregate discount is delegated to regional sales vice presidents. Revenue is
processed in the Helios ERP platform, with revenue schedules generated by a bolt on module
that the entity refers to internally as RevStream and that was implemented during the
prior fiscal year as part of a broader finance transformation. The entity grew rapidly
through the acquisition of two smaller software vendors in 20X2 and 20X3, and each
acquired business retained its own contracting templates for several quarters after
closing. Our audit scope over revenue included understanding the order to cash process,
walkthroughs of contract review and revenue schedule creation, tests of the operating
effectiveness of eleven identified controls, and substantive testing of a nonstatistical
sample of 40 arrangements selected from a population of 612 new arrangements executed
during the year, stratified to include all 23 arrangements with a transaction price
exceeding $2.0 million. We selected this area for detailed testing because of the
prior-year deficiency, the significance of judgment in identifying performance
obligations, the volume of nonstandard contract terms, and the entity's practice of
recognizing license revenue at a point in time.

## Condition

Of the 40 arrangements selected for testing, 14 arrangements, with an aggregate
transaction price of $61.7 million, contained implementation services that significantly
customized or modified the licensed software, yet the entity accounted for the license and
the implementation services as two separate performance obligations and recognized the
allocated license fee in full at the contract date. In each of the 14 arrangements, the
statement of work required Vantera personnel to write custom code within the licensed
application layer, to build interfaces that the customer could not obtain from any other
vendor because the relevant application programming interfaces were not published, and to
deliver a configured environment that the customer accepted only after an integrated
acceptance test. In seven of the 14 arrangements, the master agreement expressly stated
that the license grant would not become effective until the customer issued a written
acceptance certificate for the implementation, and in five of those seven the acceptance
certificate had not been issued as of December 31, 20X4. We inspected the executed master
agreements, all statements of work and amendments, project plans, developer time detail,
customer acceptance correspondence, and the RevStream revenue schedules for each
arrangement, and we reperformed the revenue calculation for each. For the 14 exceptions,
license revenue of $18.4 million was recognized during the year ended December 31, 20X4
that, under the entity's own stated policy for combined arrangements, would have been
recognized over the implementation period using an input measure of progress. Of that
amount, $11.2 million related to arrangements executed in the fourth quarter, including
$6.9 million recorded in the final eleven business days of the year. We also noted that
the entity's contract review checklist, control REV-C-04, was signed as complete for all
14 arrangements even though the checklist question addressing significant customization
was answered "no" in each case, and the deal desk analyst who completed the checklist
confirmed in an interview that the question was interpreted to mean only whether the
customer had requested changes to the base source code repository. The exception rate was
35 percent of sampled arrangements and 41 percent of sampled transaction price.

## Criteria

ASC 606-10-25-14 requires an entity, at contract inception, to assess the goods or
services promised in a contract with a customer and to identify as a performance
obligation each promise to transfer either a distinct good or service or a series of
distinct goods or services that are substantially the same and have the same pattern of
transfer. ASC 606-10-25-19 provides that a promised good or service is distinct only if
both criteria are met: the customer can benefit from the good or service either on its own
or together with other resources readily available to the customer, and the entity's
promise to transfer the good or service is separately identifiable from other promises in
the contract. ASC 606-10-25-21 elaborates on the second criterion and states that factors
indicating that two or more promises are not separately identifiable include that the
entity provides a significant service of integrating the goods or services into a combined
output, that one or more of the goods or services significantly modifies or customizes
another, and that the goods or services are highly interdependent or highly interrelated.
The implementation services in the 14 exception arrangements significantly customized the
licensed software and were highly interrelated with it, and the customer could not obtain
the benefit of the license without them, so the license and the implementation services
were not distinct and should have been accounted for as a single combined performance
obligation. ASC 606-10-25-23 and 25-27 then require revenue for that combined obligation
to be recognized as control transfers, and where the obligation is satisfied over time,
ASC 606-10-25-31 requires a single method of measuring progress applied consistently.
ASC 606-10-32-31 is relevant because an allocation of the transaction price between a
license and services is appropriate only once distinct performance obligations have been
properly identified. The entity's own accounting policy manual, section REV-04, mirrors
these requirements and states that arrangements involving customization within the
application layer must be accounted for as combined obligations. COSO 2013 Principle 10
requires the entity to select and develop control activities that contribute to the
mitigation of risks to acceptable levels, and Principle 12 requires controls to be
deployed through policies that establish what is expected and procedures that put those
policies into action.

## Cause

The proximate cause was that control REV-C-04, the contract review checklist, was designed
with a question that did not operationalize the accounting criterion it was intended to
address. The checklist asked whether the customer had requested modifications to the
software, which the deal desk reasonably read as referring to changes to the core product
codebase maintained by engineering, while the accounting criterion in ASC 606-10-25-21
turns on whether the services significantly customize the software delivered to that
customer, including work performed in the application layer. Because the question could be
answered "no" truthfully under the deal desk's reading, the control operated as designed
and still failed to identify the 14 arrangements, which is a control design deficiency
rather than an operating failure. Three underlying root causes contributed. First,
ownership of the checklist sat within the deal desk, a function reporting through the
Chief Revenue Officer, and no accounting or technical accounting personnel reviewed the
completed checklists for arrangements below a $5.0 million threshold, so the population
most likely to contain judgment errors received no accounting review at all. Second, the
technical accounting group consisted of two people during the year, one of whom was on
leave for four months in the second and third quarters, and the group did not have a
documented protocol for reading statements of work as opposed to master agreements, with
the result that the customization evidence, which appeared only in the statements of work,
was never examined. Third, the RevStream module was configured during the prior-year
finance transformation to default new license lines to point in time recognition, and
overriding that default required a manual change by a systems administrator, which created
a practical bias toward the default treatment and meant that combined obligation
accounting depended on someone affirmatively intervening. Contributing conditions include
an incentive structure in which regional sales vice presidents earned commission on booked
license value in the quarter of signature, the retention of legacy contracting templates
from the 20X2 and 20X3 acquisitions that did not include the customization representations
present in the standard template, and the absence of any monitoring control that compared
recognized license revenue to implementation project status. Management had accepted a
prior-year recommendation to strengthen contract review but had deferred implementation
pending completion of the finance transformation.

## Effect

License revenue for the year ended December 31, 20X4 was overstated by $18.4 million and
deferred revenue was understated by the same amount, before consideration of the
consequential effects. The overstatement equals 4.5 percent of reported total revenue,
8.6 percent of reported license revenue, and 22.1 percent of reported income before taxes
of $83.3 million, and it therefore exceeds our materiality of $8.2 million for the
financial statements as a whole by a factor of more than two. Correcting the error reduces
income before taxes to $64.9 million and reduces reported diluted earnings per share from
$1.42 to $1.11, which moves reported results from above to below the consensus estimate
published for the entity. Because $11.2 million of the affected revenue arose in the
fourth quarter, the error also distorts the interim pattern of revenue and would require
correction of previously issued interim information. Capitalized contract acquisition
costs under ASC 340-40 were amortized in proportion to the erroneously accelerated
revenue, so an additional $1.6 million of commission expense was recognized earlier than
appropriate, partially offsetting the pre-tax effect. The entity's revolving credit
facility contains a covenant requiring a fixed charge coverage ratio of not less than
2.50 to 1.00, and the corrected results produce a ratio of 2.61 to 1.00 compared with the
2.94 to 1.00 originally reported, so the covenant is not breached but the cushion narrows
materially. Annual incentive compensation for eleven executives and 34 sales leaders was
calculated on revenue attainment that included the overstated amounts, resulting in
approximately $2.3 million of incentive compensation that would not have been earned on
corrected results. Beyond the quantified misstatement, we evaluated the severity of the
underlying control deficiency by reference to the potential magnitude of misstatement
rather than only the amount identified. Because the deficient checklist was the entity's
only control over performance obligation identification, and because the untested
remainder of the population contained 589 arrangements with an aggregate transaction price
of $268.4 million subject to the same deficient control, the potential magnitude is
substantially greater than the identified misstatement. We concluded that the deficiency
constitutes a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting, that it
represents a repeat of the prior-year significant deficiency now escalated in severity,
and that it required a modification to our report on internal control.

## Recommendation

We recommend that management take the following actions. First, correct the identified
misstatement by reversing $18.4 million of license revenue, restoring the corresponding
deferred revenue, adjusting the related capitalized contract cost amortization, and
establishing revenue schedules that recognize the combined performance obligation over the
implementation period using a consistent input measure of progress; this correction should
be completed before the financial statements are issued and should be assigned to the
Corporate Controller. Second, redesign control REV-C-04 so that the checklist questions
restate the criteria in ASC 606-10-25-19 and 25-21 in operational terms specific to the
entity's products, replacing the current customization question with separate questions
addressing whether the statement of work requires code written in the application layer,
whether interfaces rely on unpublished application programming interfaces, whether an
integrated acceptance test governs the customer's acceptance, and whether the license
grant is conditioned on acceptance of the implementation. Third, transfer ownership of the
contract review control from the deal desk to the technical accounting group, eliminate
the $5.0 million review threshold so that all arrangements combining a license with
services receive accounting review, and require the reviewer to read the statement of work
and all amendments rather than the master agreement alone. Fourth, reconfigure the
RevStream module so that a license line sold together with implementation services cannot
be assigned point in time recognition without a documented approval from the technical
accounting group, and retain a system generated exception report of all such approvals.
Fifth, staff the technical accounting group with at least one additional qualified
professional and deliver targeted training on the five step model to the deal desk, the
regional sales vice presidents, and the revenue accounting team, with attendance tracked
and a knowledge assessment administered. Sixth, retire the legacy contracting templates
inherited from the 20X2 and 20X3 acquisitions and migrate all open arrangements to the
standard template. Seventh, implement a quarterly monitoring control under which the
Corporate Controller compares recognized license revenue for combined arrangements to
implementation project completion status and investigates variances exceeding a defined
threshold. Each action should have a named owner and a target completion date not later
than June 30, 20X5, and management should test a sample of at least 45 arrangements after
implementation to validate that the redesigned controls operate effectively before
concluding that the material weakness has been remediated.

## Management Response

Management agrees with the finding in part and accepts that a correcting adjustment is
required. We concur that the 14 arrangements identified during the audit involve
implementation services that significantly customize the licensed application for the
customer, and that under ASC 606-10-25-21 those arrangements should have been accounted
for as single combined performance obligations recognized over the implementation period.
We have recorded the $18.4 million adjustment described in the finding, together with the
related $1.6 million adjustment to capitalized contract cost amortization, and the
adjustment is reflected in the financial statements as issued. We respectfully offer
context on two points without disputing the adjustment. First, we believe the
characterization of the incentive structure as a contributing cause overstates its role,
because commission is subject to a twelve month clawback for cancelled implementations,
and we have found no indication that any individual knowingly mischaracterized a statement
of work; we agree, however, that the absence of accounting review below the $5.0 million
threshold created an unacceptable single point of failure. Second, we note that the
prior-year recommendation was deferred with the knowledge of the Audit Committee pending
completion of the finance transformation, and we accept that the deferral proved to be the
wrong judgment. Management concurs with the conclusion that the deficiency constitutes a
material weakness and does not contest that conclusion. Our remediation plan is as
follows. The Chief Accounting Officer will own the redesign of the contract review
checklist and will transfer ownership of the control to technical accounting effective
March 31, 20X5. The Corporate Controller will complete the correcting entries and the
revised revenue schedules by February 14, 20X5. The Vice President of Financial Systems
will complete the RevStream configuration change, including the approval workflow and the
exception report, by May 31, 20X5. The Chief Accounting Officer has received approval to
add two positions to the technical accounting group, with offers extended by April 30,
20X5, and the Vice President of Sales Operations will retire the legacy contracting
templates by April 15, 20X5. Training will be delivered in two sessions during the second
quarter of 20X5. Management will perform validation testing over a sample of at least 45
arrangements during the third and fourth quarters of 20X5 and will report the results to
the Audit Committee at its January 20X6 meeting, at which time we expect to conclude that
the material weakness has been remediated.
