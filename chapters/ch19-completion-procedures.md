# Chapter 19 — Completion Procedures

> By February 2026 the AtlasFlow file holds twelve thousand pages of evidence, and not one of them says whether the
> audit is finished. Completion is where the team stops testing accounts and starts testing its own conclusions:
> whether the misstatements it found matter in the aggregate, whether events after December 31 change what December 31
> should say, and whether a reviewer who was not there agrees.

## Learning Objectives

- **LO 19.1** Sequence the completion phase into a dated calendar running to the February 20, 2026 report date, and
  identify the dependencies that determine what cannot start until something else finishes.
- **LO 19.2** Accumulate misstatements on a summary of audit differences, classifying each as factual, projected, or
  judgmental, and apply a clearly trivial threshold correctly.
- **LO 19.3** Compute the aggregate effect of uncorrected misstatements under both the rollover and iron curtain
  methods, reconcile the difference, and compare each to overall and performance materiality.
- **LO 19.4** Evaluate uncorrected misstatements qualitatively, including the effect on loss per share, covenants, and
  disclosed metrics, and conclude on the effect on the opinion.
- **LO 19.5** Assess indicators of management bias from the direction of accumulated misstatements and from a
  retrospective review of prior-year estimates.
- **LO 19.6** Distinguish recognized from non-recognized subsequent events, document the review through the report
  date, and determine when dual dating is required.
- **LO 19.7** Evaluate whether substantial doubt exists about going-concern ability using a quantified liquidity and
  covenant analysis rather than an assertion.
- **LO 19.8** Draft the audit inquiry letter to counsel and the management representation letter, and determine the
  consequence of a refusal to sign either.
- **LO 19.9** Aggregate control deficiencies to an as-of ICFR conclusion and articulate the material weakness
  determination.
- **LO 19.10** Assemble the AS 1301 audit committee communication, document the engagement quality review and any
  consultation, and conclude on sufficiency of evidence and completeness of documentation.

## 19.1 The completion phase as a sequence

Completion is usually taught as a checklist, which is why it is usually performed badly. It is a dependency network.
The summary of audit differences cannot be finalized until the last substantive test is evaluated; the representation
letter cannot be dated before the report date; and the report date cannot precede the subsequent-events review, the
legal letter responses, the engagement quality reviewer's concurring approval, and the audit committee communication.

**Exhibit 19-1. AtlasFlow FY2025 completion calendar (WP 8100-01).**

| Date (2026) | Event or procedure |
| --- | --- |
| Jan 9–12 | Preliminary trial balance delivered; ICFR roll-forward from the October 31 interim date completed |
| Jan 14–16 | Whistleblower email to the audit committee; chair notified Jan 15; management notified and counsel investigation commissioned Jan 16 |
| Jan 20–26 | Draft financial statements received; audit inquiry letters mailed Jan 23; SAD v1 issued to the CFO Jan 26 |
| Feb 2–4 | Draft Form 10-K with MD&A, metrics, and non-GAAP measures; engagement quality reviewer begins |
| Feb 5–6 | Legal letter responses received; retrospective review and final analytical review completed |
| Feb 9 | Meridian Health Systems disputes the $1,240 professional services invoice |
| Feb 10–11 | Section 404(a) assessment finalized and deficiency schedule agreed; audit committee pre-read issued with the SAD |
| Feb 12–13 | SAD v3 issued and management declines to correct U-1 through U-5; national office consultation concluded |
| Feb 16 | Going-concern, ICFR conclusion, and sufficiency memoranda finalized |

Two dependencies are the ones teams break. The legal letter is effective February 5, so the inquiry must be updated
through February 20. And U-3 is both an uncorrected misstatement and the evidence supporting a revenue cut-off material
weakness, so a change to either document forces a re-read of the other.

## 19.2 Accumulating misstatements: the summary of audit differences

AS 2810 and AU-C 450 require accumulation of every misstatement other than those that are clearly trivial. "Clearly
trivial" is not "immaterial": an item qualifies only if it is inconsequential individually and in the aggregate by
any criterion of size, nature, or circumstance, so a $30 misstatement in executive compensation does not qualify.
AtlasFlow's threshold is $72 (Chapter 3). Nine items fell below it, aggregating $(118); logging them on a
below-threshold schedule is what makes the statement that they change no conclusion defensible rather than circular.

Three types behave differently in the evaluation: **factual (known)** misstatements, about which there is no doubt;
**projected** misstatements, the auditor's best estimate for a population only sampled; and **judgmental**
misstatements, the difference between management's estimate and the auditor's where both lie within a range of
reasonable outcomes. For AtlasFlow's six uncorrected items (Exhibit 19-16), factual items total $(84) — U-1's known
component of $(49) plus U-3 through U-6 — U-1's projected component is $(136), and U-2's $(240) is judgmental.

Two classification calls need their reasoning on the record. U-4 is factual even though it sits inside an estimate,
because the hours-incurred input was wrong as a matter of fact. U-2 is judgmental and contestable: Chapter 12
developed an acceptable allowance range of $1,780 to $2,410 and management recorded $1,900, so one defensible
position is that no misstatement exists. Brightline instead measures from the auditor's point estimate of $2,140,
because an estimate parked at the favorable end of a range is a bias indicator (§19.5) and an item that never reaches
the SAD is never aggregated with the others.

**Exhibit 19-2. Corrected misstatements recorded by management (in thousands).**

| Ref | Description | Income effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C-1 | Revenue recognized from March 15 rather than the April 2 provisioning date, 14 Q1 contracts | (410) |
| C-2 | SLA credits earned but not accrued (Northgate and six others) | (290) |
| C-3 | Capitalized commissions on churned customers not written off | (620) |
| C-4 | Understatement of accrued VAT in the UK entity | (180) |

Corrected items sit outside the uncorrected aggregate but matter three ways: they measure the quality of the close, four
of five run income-increasing, and each is a candidate control deficiency. A $1,260 correction cycle against $1,450 of
materiality is not a clean close.

## 19.3 Quantitative evaluation: rollover and iron curtain

SAB 108 requires quantification under both methods and a conclusion under each. The **rollover method** measures the
effect on the current period's income statement and so includes prior-period turnaround; the **iron curtain method**
measures cumulative misstatement in the balance sheet at the reporting date and so ignores it.

**Exhibit 19-3. Aggregate effect of uncorrected misstatements, both methods (in thousands).**

| Ref | Income effect (rollover) | Assets over/(under) | Liabilities under/(over) | Equity overstatement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| U-1 | (185) | 185 | — | 185 |
| U-2 | (240) | 240 | — | 240 |
| U-3 | (150) | 150 | — | 150 |
| U-4 | 95 | (95) | — | (95) |
| U-5 | (110) | — | 110 | 110 |
| U-6 | 130 | — | — | — |
| **Total** | **(460)** | **480** | **110** | **590** |

The two answers differ by exactly U-6's $130, the FY2024 item that turned around in FY2025. Under the rollover method
the aggregate understates the FY2025 pre-tax loss by **$460**; under the iron curtain method equity is overstated by
**$590**, being assets overstated $480 and liabilities understated $110. Cross-check: $460 + $130 = $590. A team
reporting only $(460) understates its exposure by 28%. Because a full valuation allowance is carried against the US
deferred tax assets, no item bears a tax effect and the pre-tax and after-tax aggregates are equal.

**Exhibit 19-4. Aggregate effect against materiality benchmarks (in thousands).**

| Benchmark | Amount | Rollover $460 | Iron curtain $590 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Overall materiality | 1,450 | 31.7% | 40.7% |
| Performance materiality | 940 | 48.9% | 62.8% |
| Total revenue / pre-tax loss | 148,200 / 20,860 | 0.3% / 2.2% | 0.4% / 2.8% |
| Total stockholders' equity | 7,100 | 6.5% | 8.3% |

The equity row looks alarming at 8.3%, but equity of $7,100 is a residual held down by a $404,100 accumulated deficit,
and no user treats it as a benchmark. The performance materiality row carries the information: at 62.8% the planned
aggregation-risk buffer is nearly two-thirds consumed, so the sufficiency conclusion must address whether undetected
plus known misstatement could exceed $1,450. Here it could not, because revenue and receivables were tested by
full-population procedures (Chapter 18).

## 19.4 Qualitative evaluation and the effect on the opinion

A quantitatively immaterial aggregate can still be material. AS 2810 and SAB 99 both require the evaluation to
consider nature and circumstance.

**Exhibit 19-5. Qualitative evaluation of the $460 / $590 aggregate (WP 8110-06).**

| Factor | Analysis | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loss per share | $(21,400)/50,952 = $(0.4200) rounds to $(0.42); $(21,860)/50,952 = $(0.4290) rounds to $(0.43) | Elevating — a one-cent change in a headline figure |
| A trend the market watches | Revenue overstated $335 (U-1 plus U-3) cuts growth from 24.64% to 24.36%; ARR and NRR are unaffected | Mildly elevating |
| Earnings sign, covenants, compensation | The loss rose year over year on both bases; the only covenant is the $40,000 liquidity test, untouched; PSUs vest on ARR and bookings, so $335 moves no threshold | Not elevating |
| Sensitivity; offsetting; cost of correction | U-3 arises in revenue, a fraud-risk area, in the population the whistleblower identified (Chapter 17); $225 of income-decreasing items offset $685 of income-increasing items; management called correction "not worth reopening the close," which is not a permissible basis for passing an item | Elevating |

Both poles of the one-cent argument are legitimate: a rounding change in a headline per-share figure is the paradigm
SAB 99 case, but no analyst publishes a GAAP loss-per-share estimate for AtlasFlow, and a penny on a forty-two-cent
loss changes no decision. Brightline took the second position and documented the first.

**Conclusion on the opinion.** The aggregate uncorrected misstatement, at $460 (rollover) or $590 (iron curtain), is
not material individually or in the aggregate, quantitatively or qualitatively, so the opinion on the financial
statements is unqualified. That is independent of the adverse ICFR conclusion in §19.13, and Chapter 20 shows the
wording.

**The request to correct.** AU-C 450 and AS 1301 require more than a schedule: request correction of all accumulated
misstatements, not only those you consider material, and communicate the uncorrected items and their effects —
including on future periods — to those charged with governance. Brightline asked for correction of U-1 through U-5 on
February 12; management declined on immateriality grounds, and the refusal and its stated reasons are documented. The
forward-looking point teams omit: U-1, U-2, U-3, and U-5 turn around in FY2026 as U-6 did in FY2025, so the FY2026 team
starts with $685 on its schedule.

## 19.5 Qualitative aspects of accounting practices and indicators of bias

AS 2810 requires an evaluation of whether accounting practices, taken as a whole, indicate bias in management's
judgments. This is a pattern assessment, and its most useful test is directional.

**Exhibit 19-6. Directional analysis of uncorrected misstatements (in thousands).**

| Ref | Origin period | Direction as originated | Amount | Judgment with a range? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| U-1 | FY2025 | Income-increasing | 185 | No — cut-off errors |
| U-2 | FY2025 | Income-increasing | 240 | Yes — CECL allowance |
| U-3 | FY2025 | Income-increasing | 150 | No — date corroboration |
| U-5 | FY2025 | Income-increasing | 110 | Yes — dispute outcome |
| U-6 | FY2024 | Income-increasing when it originated | 130 | Yes — commission accrual |
| U-4 | FY2025 | Income-decreasing | 95 | No — input error |

Five of six uncorrected misstatements originated as income-increasing, and the only one running the other way is the
smallest and is a clerical error rather than a judgment. In amounts, $815 of $910 gross — 89.6% — favors reported
results. Adding the corrected items, where C-1 through C-4 total $1,500 against C-5's $240, income-increasing
misstatements are $2,315 of $2,650 across all eleven items, or 87.4%.

Random misstatement is bidirectional, so a pattern this one-directional has three explanations: coincidence, an
incentive-driven tilt in each judgment call, or deliberate manipulation. The team concluded the second, with the third
unresolved for the population addressed in Chapter 17.

Three further indicators were evaluated. The $1,900 allowance sits 19% of the way up the $1,780–$2,410 range while days
sales outstanding rose from 61 to 68 days and gross retention fell from 93% to 91% — the estimate moved toward optimism
as the indicators moved the other way. The Q4 PSU probability revision produced a $1,340 credit to expense in the
quarter that most benefited from it, $240 of which belonged to Q3 (C-5). And management's Kestrel allocation schedule
carried a $2,000 unexplained difference (§1.6). Together they support tilt rather than pervasive intentional
misstatement.

## 19.6 The retrospective review of prior-year estimates

AS 2401 requires a retrospective review of significant estimates as a fraud procedure regardless of assessed risk,
and AS 2501 uses the same review as evidence about current-year estimates. Perform it as arithmetic.

**Exhibit 19-7. Retrospective review of FY2024 estimates (WP 8110-11, in thousands).**

| FY2024 estimate | Recorded at 12/31/2024 | Subsequent outcome | Variance and direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Allowance for credit losses | 1,350 | $1,040 of the 12/31/2024 gross AR written off in FY2025 plus $180 unresolved over 180 days = $1,220 | 130 over-reserved (9.6%); income-decreasing |
| Accrued commissions | 4,410 | FY2025 payments on FY2024 bookings exceeded the accrual by $130 (U-6) | 130 under-accrued (2.9%); income-increasing |
| Services hours to complete | 12,400 hours on engagements open at 12/31/2024 | 13,150 actual hours, a 6.0% overrun | $185 recognized early; income-increasing |
| ARR-based PSU probability | 100% probable | Revised to 85% in Q4 2025; $1,340 catch-up credit | Income-decreasing |
| Commission benefit period | 4 years | Realized customer life 4.3 years; $1,900 of cost on non-renewed customers | $620 write-off (C-3); income-increasing |

Three of five prior-year estimates resolved income-increasing, and one — the commission accrual — is the origin of U-6.
The allowance row is the counterexample: FY2024's allowance was 9.6% conservative, which is why U-2 was measured as a
judgmental difference rather than treated as unreasonable. A variance under 10% one year out is within the expected
range; above roughly 25% in the same direction two years running, re-audit the current-year estimate from scratch using
the auditor's own loss rates.

## 19.7 Subsequent events

ASC 855 draws the line that matters: a **recognized** subsequent event provides additional evidence about conditions
existing at the balance-sheet date and is reflected in the statements, while a **non-recognized** event arises from
conditions coming into existence afterward and is disclosed if the statements would otherwise mislead. The review period
runs from January 1 through the report date of February 20, 2026 — not the fieldwork exit date.

**Exhibit 19-8. Subsequent-events procedures performed (WP 8120-02).**

| # | Procedure | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Read the January 2026 trial balance and the January revenue and DSO reports | January revenue $12,930; three credit memos totaling $84 against FY2025 invoices, all previously accrued |
| 2 | Inquire of the CFO, CAO, Revenue Manager, Treasury Manager, and General Counsel, with update calls February 19–20 | Meridian dispute (case study); no new litigation or covenant events |
| 3 | Read board and audit committee minutes through February 17 and consents through February 19 | Counsel investigation commissioned January 16; revolver renewed on existing terms; January 31 ARR $173.9M |
| 4 | Compare January and February cash receipts to the 12/31/2025 aged trial balance, per bank statements to February 18 | $24,600 collected against $38,600 gross AR, including Meridian $1,240 on February 18 |
| 5 | Obtain the counsel investigation status report (February 13) and update the litigation inquiry through the report date | No backdating findings beyond the six contracts in U-3; General Counsel email February 20 reports no change from the February 5 letter |
| 6 | Read the final Form 10-K and obtain the representation letter dated the report date | No inconsistency; Item 9A discloses the two material weaknesses |

**Dual dating.** If AtlasFlow revised a footnote after February 20 for an event occurring February 26, Brightline could
re-date the whole report to February 26 — extending responsibility for all subsequent events — or dual-date:
"February 20, 2026, except as to Note 18, as to which the date is February 26, 2026." Dual dating limits the extension
to that matter, and the representation letter must be updated for it. Nothing here required it.

An unexpected result looks like a January credit memo of $310 against a December invoice described as "delivery
shortfall." That is not a January event; obtain the correspondence, determine when the concession was promised, and
evaluate whether December revenue requires adjustment.

## 19.8 Going concern

Under ASC 205-40, management first asks whether conditions or events raise **substantial doubt** — probable inability to
meet obligations within one year after issuance, here through February 20, 2027 — and, if so, whether plans probable of
implementation and of being effective alleviate it. The auditor's job under AS 2415 and AU-C 570 is to evaluate that
conclusion, not to reperform the forecast. Start with the conditions that could raise doubt, because AtlasFlow has some:
a net loss of $21,400, an accumulated deficit of $404,100, and $175,000 of convertible notes.

**Exhibit 19-9. Going-concern liquidity analysis (WP 8130-01, in thousands).**

| Item | FY2025 actual | FY2026 base case | FY2026 downside |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Net cash provided by operating activities | 34,400 | 38,000 | (12,000) |
| Property, equipment, and capitalized software (a) | (16,300) | (17,500) | (11,000) |
| **Free cash flow** | **18,100** | **20,500** | **(23,000)** |
| **Cash and short-term investments at year end** | **150,600** | **171,100** | **127,600** |
| Minimum-liquidity covenant on the $50,000 revolver | 40,000 | 40,000 | 40,000 |
| **Headroom above the covenant** | **110,600** | **131,100** | **87,600** |
| Obligations: notes (due 9/15/2029) / interest / leases | — / 438 / 3,600 | — / 438 / 3,600 | — / 438 / 3,600 |

(a) Liquidity of $150,600 is cash of $96,400 plus short-term investments of $54,200. FY2025 capitalized software of
$10,900 comes from §4.3; the $5,400 equipment component and both FY2026 columns are illustrative extensions applying
free cash flow to the closing balance with no financing inflows. The downside case assumes zero net new ARR and a
12-point fall in gross retention.

The analysis answers itself. AtlasFlow generated $34,400 of operating cash flow while reporting a $21,400 net loss;
the gap is $28,700 of stock-based compensation, $11,900 of depreciation and amortization, $8,600 of deferred-cost
amortization, and $4,120 of favorable working capital movement driven by deferred revenue growth of $15,900. Positive
operating cash flow through a reported loss is the structural signature of a subscription business billing annually
in advance, which is why a SaaS net loss is not by itself a going-concern condition. Liquidity is 3.8 times the
covenant minimum and 6.5 years of the downside case's burn; the notes mature beyond the assessment period, and their
$438 of annual cash interest is 1.3% of operating cash flow. Substantial doubt would require consuming $110,600 of
headroom in twelve months — a $128,700 swing from free cash flow of $18,100 to negative $110,600 — and nothing in the
forecast, the January actuals, or the covenant structure supports that.

**Conclusion.** Conditions and events in the aggregate do not raise substantial doubt for the assessment period. No
ASC 205-40 disclosure arises, no mitigating-plans analysis is required, and the report includes no going-concern
paragraph. State that as the output of a computation with headroom quantified; "the Company has sufficient liquidity"
is worth nothing to a reviewer or an inspector. What would change the answer: an earnings or leverage covenant rather
than a liquidity test; a fundamental-change provision accelerating the notes; or a credit-agreement liquidity
definition excluding the $22,800 of commercial paper and corporate notes, which would cut measured liquidity to
$127,800 and headroom to $87,800 — still ample, but the definition must be read.

## 19.9 Contingencies, litigation, and the legal letter

AS 2505 requires evidence about litigation, claims, and assessments, and the audit inquiry letter to the client's
lawyer is the primary source. Management signs it, because it asks the lawyer to comment on positions management has
taken; a refusal to respond is a scope limitation.

**Exhibit 19-10. Audit inquiry letter to outside counsel (extract, sent January 23, 2026).**

```text

January 23, 2026
Vantage & Rowe LLP

In connection with an audit of our consolidated financial statements as of December 31, 2025 and for
the year then ended, management has furnished to our auditors, Brightline LLP, a description and
evaluation of certain contingencies involving matters to which you have devoted substantive attention
on behalf of the Company in the form of legal consultation or representation.

Pending or threatened litigation, claims, and assessments (management's description, evaluation, and
estimated range of potential loss):

  1. Rincon Systems, Inc. v. AtlasFlow, Inc. (W.D. Tex., filed November 6, 2025), patent infringement
     relating to workflow orchestration. Management believes an unfavorable outcome is reasonably
     possible but not probable and that no reasonable estimate of a range of loss can be made. No
     amount has been accrued.
  2. Former employee wrongful-termination demand arising from the October 2025 reduction in force.
     Demand $220. Management considers a loss probable and has accrued $60, its best estimate within
     a range of $40 to $220.
Please furnish Brightline LLP such explanation as you consider necessary to supplement the foregoing,
including matters as to which your views differ, and indicate whether any pending or threatened
litigation, claims, or assessments have been omitted or confirm that the list is complete.

We understand that whenever, in performing legal services for us with respect to a matter recognized
to involve an unasserted possible claim that may call for financial statement disclosure, you have
formed a professional conclusion that we must disclose or consider disclosure, you will so advise us
and consult with us concerning ASC 450. Please confirm that our understanding is correct.

Our materiality threshold for your response is $250. Please respond as of February 5, 2026 and specify
the effective date of your response.
```

The $250 threshold is an engagement judgment; a defensible range on $1,450 of overall materiality is roughly $150 to
$450, and setting it at overall materiality invites a response that omits an aggregation of related matters.

The February 5 responses confirmed management's descriptions and the unasserted-claims understanding, and the inquiry
was updated on February 20 (Exhibit 19-8, procedure 5). The counsel investigation of the whistleblower allegation is
not a claim and does not belong on the litigation schedule, but it produced accrued professional fees in account 2220
and is a required audit committee communication (Chapter 17). Matter 2 is a straight ASC 450-20 application: a probable
loss with a $40–$220 range and a best estimate of $60 is accrued at $60 with the range disclosed.

## 19.10 The management representation letter

Written representations are audit evidence of the weakest kind: they corroborate, they never substitute. AS 2805 and
AU-C 580 require them; the letter must be dated as of the report date — February 20, 2026 — and cover all periods
reported on, which for the statements of operations means FY2023 through FY2025.

**Exhibit 19-11. Representation letter, selected paragraphs (WP 8150-01, dated February 20, 2026).**

```text
To Brightline LLP:

We are providing this letter in connection with your audits of the consolidated financial statements
of AtlasFlow, Inc. as of December 31, 2025 and 2024 and for each of the three years ended
December 31, 2025, and of the effectiveness of internal control over financial reporting as of
December 31, 2025. We confirm, to the best of our knowledge and belief, as of February 20, 2026:

 4. We acknowledge our responsibility for internal control over financial reporting. We have evaluated

10. We have disclosed to you all information in relation to allegations of fraud or suspected fraud
    communicated by employees, former employees, analysts, regulators, or others.
14. We believe the effects of the uncorrected misstatements summarized in the attached schedule are
    immaterial, individually and in the aggregate, to the consolidated financial statements taken as a
    whole. The schedule presents their effect under both the rollover and iron curtain methods.
17. The methods, significant assumptions, and data used in making accounting estimates are reasonable,

    four-year commission period, the 85% PSU probability, and the $2,500 Kestrel earn-out.
21. All events through the date of this letter requiring adjustment to or disclosure in the financial
    statements have been adjusted or disclosed.
24. We are in compliance with all covenants of the revolving credit facility, including the $40,000
    minimum-liquidity covenant, and our ASC 205-40 evaluation concluded that no conditions or events
    raise substantial doubt about our ability to continue as a going concern for one year after the

```

Paragraph 14 is the operative one for §19.4: management's dated written acknowledgment that the $460 and $590
aggregates are immaterial. Attach the SAD; a representation about "the attached schedule" with nothing attached is a
defect that appears in real files.

**If management will not sign.** A refusal to sign, or to give a required representation, is a scope limitation that
also calls every other representation into question; the auditor ordinarily disclaims or withdraws and cannot qualify
around the missing representation. A willingness to sign with an "except as noted" carve-out is evaluated on its
merits — a factual carve-out for a matter under investigation, as in paragraph 9, is normal. If management signs while
you hold contradicting evidence, resolve the contradiction and reassess integrity and the other representations. Each
situation goes to the audit committee before the report date.

## 19.11 The final analytical review and the disclosure checklist

AS 2810 requires an overall review near the end of the audit. Its purpose is not to find new misstatements from
scratch but to ask whether the audited statements, read as a set, are consistent with your understanding of the
business and whether any relationship remains unexplained.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

**The disclosure checklist.** A checklist is firm methodology, not a professional requirement, and it is worthless

concentration required evaluation because Meridian's $2,410 balance is 6.2% of gross receivables — below the 10%
threshold, but the evaluation belongs in the file.

## 19.12 Non-GAAP measures, operating metrics, and other information

Everything in the Form 10-K outside the audited statements and footnotes is other information. Under AS 2710 you read it
and consider whether it is materially inconsistent with the audited statements or contains a material misstatement of
fact; you do not audit it, and the report says nothing about it. AU-C 720's obligations are broader and its report
includes an "Other Information" section. Neither standard makes the auditor responsible for Regulation G compliance,
but a Regulation G problem you can see and do not raise is a failure of another kind.

## 19.13 The ICFR conclusion

The ICFR opinion is an **as-of** opinion: it addresses whether controls operated effectively at December 31, 2025, not
on average during the year. A deficiency remediated in November and tested successfully in December does not produce
an adverse opinion; one still open at December 31 does, if it is a material weakness. Chapter 14 graded each; completion
aggregates them.

**Exhibit 19-14. ICFR aggregation and as-of conclusion (WP 8170-01).**

| Cluster | Deficiencies and individual grades (Chapter 14) | Aggregate severity as of 12/31/2025, all open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Logical access | W-1, W-3, W-6 significant deficiencies; W-2, W-5 deficiencies | **Material weakness** |
| B — Change management | W-4 significant deficiency; W-7, W-14 deficiencies | **Material weakness** |
| C — Journal entries and management override | W-12, W-13 both significant deficiencies | **Material weakness** |
| D — Monitoring and end-user computing | W-8, W-10 significant deficiencies; W-9, W-11 deficiencies | Significant deficiency |
| E — Revenue cut-off | Non-existent order-validation control FIN-REV-07 / OTC-05, with U-3 as its demonstrated consequence | **Material weakness** |
| F — Treasury | Unescalated 5.7% single-issuer policy exception; positive pay not enabled on account 1015 (Chapter 8) | Significant deficiency |

Clusters A, B, and C each reach material weakness severity on their own and share one root cause: IT general controls
over the systems that produce revenue and financial reports were neither designed nor operating at the level a large
accelerated filer requires. Reporting them as three weaknesses would imply three unrelated remediation programs, so they
are aggregated and reported as **one material weakness in information technology general controls over the revenue and
financial reporting systems**. Cluster E is separate because it is not an IT matter: FIN-REV-07/OTC-05, the
order-validation control management described in its documentation, did not exist (Chapter 13), and its absence has a
demonstrated consequence in U-3's $150. Clusters D and F remain significant deficiencies, communicated in writing.

Two material weaknesses existed as of December 31, 2025, so ICFR was not effective and the ICFR opinion is adverse.
That does not require a modified opinion on the financial statements: the substantive procedures performed in response
— full-population revenue testing and the expanded December contract procedures — provided sufficient appropriate
evidence that the statements are fairly stated.

**Management's assessment.** AS 2201 requires you to evaluate management's assessment process, not merely its
conclusion. The February 10 assessment identifies the same two material weaknesses, so there is no disagreement to
report; its exclusion of Kestrel Labs is within the SEC staff's accommodation for a business acquired during the year,
is disclosed, and was tested for scope — Kestrel contributed $340 of revenue, and its balances were subjected to
substantive procedures even though its controls were not.

## 19.16 Documentation completion, archiving, and the sufficiency conclusion

AS 1215 requires a complete and final set of audit documentation to be assembled no later than **45 days after the
report release date** — April 6, 2026 — and retained for seven years, a period Rule 2-06 of Regulation S-X imposes in
parallel. After the documentation completion date information may be added but nothing deleted, and each addition must
identify who added it, when, and why.

The final judgment is the sufficiency conclusion required by AS 2810. Perform it as a mapping, not a sentence: for each
significant account and disclosure, record the assessed risk, the planned response, whether the response was executed
as planned, and whether the results support the assertion. Three areas required an explicit statement that planned
reliance was reduced and replaced: revenue and deferred revenue, where the ITGC material weakness forced
full-population recomputation from contract data; journal entries, where cluster C meant testing could not rely on the
$250,000 approval threshold; and the consolidation, where the W-8 workbook required independent recomputation of FX
translation and the 27 top-side entries. The conclusion is then a sentence with a file behind it: sufficient appropriate
evidence supports an unqualified opinion on the FY2025 financial statements and an adverse opinion on ICFR as of
December 31, 2025.

**Completing and evaluating the summary of audit differences.** The inputs are the misstatement logs from each area
workpaper; the outputs are Exhibit 19-16, the evaluation memorandum, the audit committee schedule, and paragraph 14 of
the representation letter. Amounts in thousands.

**Step 1.** Fix the thresholds first: overall materiality $1,450, performance materiality $940, clearly trivial $72.
Accumulate every identified misstatement above $72 and keep the below-threshold items on a separate schedule (nine
items, $(118) aggregate) so the omission is documented rather than assumed.

**Step 2.** Pull the corrected items and confirm each was recorded in the ledger, not merely agreed. Trace C-1 through
C-5 to journal entries JE-8841 through JE-8845 posted February 6, 2026, and agree the $(1,260) net to the movement
between the January 20 draft and the February 19 final statements.

**Step 3.** Pull the uncorrected items. For each, record the account, the assertion, the source workpaper, the

## Extended Case Study: The February 9, 2026 Meridian Health Systems Dispute

### Background

Meridian Health Systems is AtlasFlow's second-largest customer, with a gross receivable of $2,410 at December 31,
2025 — 6.2% of the $38,600 gross balance. Chapter 7 examined SOW-2: a $2,110 fixed-fee implementation signed
October 3, 2025, recognized on an input method, and accepted by Meridian's program office on December 3, 2025. The
related $1,240 invoice was issued December 18, 2025 and unpaid at year end.

### The Facts

On February 9, 2026 — after fieldwork was substantially complete, after the February 5 legal letter responses, and
eleven days before the planned report date — Meridian's procurement director emailed AtlasFlow's CRO disputing the
$1,240 invoice and stating that Meridian "does not consider the amount payable as invoiced." The email did not say why.
The amount is 85.5% of overall materiality and 1.3 times performance materiality, so it cannot be disposed of by size.
Two readings were available: the work delivered under SOW-2 during the fourth quarter did not meet specification,
notwithstanding the December 3 acceptance; or the dispute is a 2026 negotiating position connected to Meridian's pending
renewal.

### What the Engagement Team Did

Omar Haddad obtained the February 9 email and the SOW-2 correspondence file; interviewed the CRO, delivery lead, and
program manager separately, asking each what Meridian had complained about and when; agreed the December 3 acceptance
certificate to the SOW-2 §4 criteria; ran his own ticketing-system query; read the renewal negotiation file; and
inquired of the General Counsel whether a claim had been asserted.

### Analysis

The classification question under ASC 855 is not when the auditor learned of the matter but when the underlying
condition came into existence.

Under the first reading the condition existed at December 31, 2025: if the delivered work did not conform, it did not
conform on the balance-sheet date, and the February 9 email is new evidence about an old condition — a **recognized**
subsequent event. Three consequences follow. The December 3 acceptance, and therefore satisfaction of the performance
obligation, must be re-evaluated. If a price concession was in substance expected at December 31, the transaction price
was overstated: a concession is variable consideration under ASC 606, not a bad debt, so it reduces revenue rather than
creating an allowance charge. And if the amount is collectible but at risk, it belongs in the CECL analysis behind U-2.

Under the second reading the condition arose in 2026. A February decision to use an unpaid invoice as leverage in a
renewal negotiation is a new event, and the December 31 receivable was properly stated and reserved on the facts then
existing. That makes it a **non-recognized** subsequent event, disclosed only if non-disclosure would make the
statements misleading — which at $1,240 against a $317,900 balance sheet, with no claim asserted, it would not.

The evidence favored the second reading, but not on management's assertion. No Meridian severity-1 or severity-2 ticket
was opened between October 3, 2025 and February 9, 2026; the January status report listed no open defects; the
December 3 certificate was unqualified; and the renewal file showed a February 4 counterproposal seeking a 12%
subscription discount, four days before the dispute. Had the evidence gone the other way — a severity-1 ticket opened
December 12 and still open, or a January email listing unmet acceptance criteria — the team would have reperformed the
SOW-2 revenue conclusion, quantified the concession probable at December 31, and added it to the SAD. A $1,240
recognized adjustment on top of the existing $(460) produces $1,700 against $1,450 of overall materiality: management
corrects, or the opinion is qualified.

### Resolution and Conclusion

The parties settled on February 17, 2026 and Meridian paid in full on February 18 — $1,240, no concession, no credit
memo — confirmed to the bank statement at Exhibit 19-8, procedure 4. Payment in full is the strongest available evidence
on both readings at once: it establishes that no price concession existed at December 31 and that the receivable was
collectible, so no adjustment and no disclosure arises. The matter was classified as a non-recognized subsequent event,
and no dual dating was necessary because nothing in the statements changed. The dispute was reported to the audit
committee on February 17 with the outcome pending, and the February 18 payment was confirmed to the chair before release.

### Lessons

An event arriving eleven days before the report date does not get a lighter standard of evidence because the file is
nearly closed; the classification here turned on a ticketing-system query and a negotiation file, not on management's
characterization. And "recognized or non-recognized" is never a property of the notification date, only of when the
underlying condition arose.

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 19.1 — Treating the clearly trivial threshold as a small materiality

**What it looks like.** Items below $72 are discarded without being logged.
**Why it happens.** The threshold is a number, so teams apply it as a numeric filter.
**What goes wrong.** Aggregate inconsequence cannot be demonstrated, and a qualitatively sensitive small item is
discarded with the rounding differences.
**How to avoid it.** Log every misstatement, apply the threshold as a documented judgment, and report the
below-threshold aggregate ($(118) here).

### Mistake 19.2 — Quantifying under one method only

**What it looks like.** The SAD shows a single income-statement column and the conclusion cites one number.
**Why it happens.** Prior-period turnaround is invisible unless someone builds the column for it.
**What goes wrong.** AtlasFlow's exposure is $590, not $460 — a 28% understatement — and SAB 108 expects both.
**How to avoid it.** Build balance-sheet columns on the same schedule and show $460 + $130 = $590 on its face.

### Mistake 19.3 — Netting misstatements without showing the gross

**What it looks like.** The evaluation reports $(460) net and nothing else.
**Why it happens.** The net is what the statements would change by, so it feels like the relevant figure.
**What goes wrong.** The $685 of income-increasing items and the $225 running the other way carry the bias
information, and netting destroys it.
**How to avoid it.** Report net and gross and subtotal by direction; direction is the input to the bias evaluation.

### Mistake 19.4 — Passing a judgmental difference because management is inside the range

**What it looks like.** The allowance is $1,900 within a range of $1,780 to $2,410, so nothing goes on the SAD.
**Why it happens.** It is a defensible technical position, and it avoids an argument.
**What goes wrong.** An estimate at the 19th percentile is a bias indicator, and an item off the SAD is never
aggregated or communicated to the audit committee.
**How to avoid it.** Measure from the auditor's point estimate, record the difference, and state separately that
management's amount is within the acceptable range.

### Mistake 19.5 — Asserting the going-concern conclusion instead of computing it

**What it looks like.** "Management concluded substantial doubt does not exist, and we concur."
**Why it happens.** The answer is obvious to the team, so the analysis feels ceremonial.
**What goes wrong.** No headroom, no downside case, and no assessment period, so nobody can test the conclusion.
**How to avoid it.** Quantify headroom ($110,600), state the period (through February 20, 2027), model a downside case,
and say what would change the answer.

### Mistake 19.6 — Relying on the legal letter as of its effective date

**What it looks like.** The response is effective February 5 and the report is dated February 20.
**Why it happens.** The response arrives, gets filed, and the litigation box gets ticked.
**What goes wrong.** A fifteen-day gap in litigation coverage sits inside the auditor's subsequent-events period.
**How to avoid it.** Update the inquiry of counsel through the report date and document it, as Exhibit 19-8 does.

### Mistake 19.7 — Dating the representation letter to the fieldwork exit date

**What it looks like.** The letter is dated February 13 and the report February 20.
**Why it happens.** The letter is chased when fieldwork ends and the draft date never changes.
**What goes wrong.** Management has not represented on the window containing the Meridian settlement and payment.
**How to avoid it.** Date the letter as of the report date, and obtain an updating representation for any matter that
causes re-dating or dual dating.

### Mistake 19.8 — Concluding on ICFR on an average-for-the-year basis

**What it looks like.** "Controls operated effectively for most of the year, and access issues were being remediated,"
followed by an unqualified ICFR opinion.
**Why it happens.** Substantive evidence is period-based and the habit carries over.
**What goes wrong.** The opinion is wrong: weaknesses open at December 31 require an adverse opinion whenever
remediation began.
**How to avoid it.** Assess status as of the balance-sheet date, and treat a remediated control as closed only after
testing it operating for a sufficient period before December 31.

### Mistake 19.9 — Reporting related material weaknesses separately or bundling unrelated ones

**What it looks like.** Three weaknesses for access, change management, and journal entries; or one covering everything,
including revenue cut-off.
**Why it happens.** The schedule is organized by cluster and the reporting decision is treated as clerical.
**What goes wrong.** Three weaknesses with one root cause imply three remediation programs; one bundled weakness hides
that revenue cut-off has a different cause and cure.
**How to avoid it.** Aggregate by root cause: one ITGC material weakness over the revenue and financial reporting
systems, and a separate revenue cut-off material weakness.

### Mistake 19.10 — Documenting the consultation conclusion without the question

**What it looks like.** "Consulted national office; they concurred."
**Why it happens.** The conclusion is what the team needed, so the record stops there.
**What goes wrong.** No reader can tell what facts were provided, what alternatives were weighed, or whether the
consultation covered the position actually taken.
**How to avoid it.** Record the question, facts, alternatives, conclusion, authority, and who reached it — and the same
for differences of professional opinion, including the dissenting view.

### Solution 19-1

## Answers to Review Questions

**RQ 19-1.** An item is clearly trivial only if it is inconsequential individually and in the aggregate by any criterion
of size, nature, or circumstance. Immaterial, by contrast, is a conclusion reached after evaluating accumulated
misstatements against materiality, so it is a far larger amount.

**RQ 19-2.** A factual (known) misstatement is one about which there is no doubt. A projected misstatement is the
auditor's best estimate for a population from which only a sample was examined. A judgmental misstatement is the
difference between management's estimate and the auditor's where both lie within a range of reasonable outcomes.

**RQ 19-3.** The rollover method measures the effect on the current period's income statement, including prior-period
turnaround; the iron curtain method measures cumulative misstatement remaining in the balance sheet. Both are required
because each can be immaterial while the other is material — $460 and $590 for AtlasFlow, differing by U-6's $130.

**RQ 19-4.** All of them — every accumulated misstatement, not only those the auditor considers material. AU-C 450
states this explicitly, and AS 2810 with AS 1301 produce the same result for issuers.

**RQ 19-5.** Any four of: the effect on earnings per share or the earnings trend; debt covenant compliance; management
compensation; a metric users follow; a sensitive or fraud-risk area; an unlawful act; intentionality.

**RQ 19-6.** Random misstatement is bidirectional, so a pattern in which most items and most dollars favor reported
results is evidence of bias in management's judgments, which AS 2810 requires the auditor to evaluate. At AtlasFlow,
89.6% of the gross uncorrected amount favors reported results.

**RQ 19-7.** It compares significant prior-year estimates with outcomes or revised current-year estimates to detect
bias and obtain evidence about current estimates. AS 2401 requires it regardless of assessed risk; AS 2501 uses the
same evidence.

**RQ 19-8.** A recognized subsequent event provides additional evidence about conditions existing at the balance-sheet
date and is reflected in the statements. A non-recognized event arises from conditions coming into existence afterward
and is disclosed only if non-disclosure would mislead.

**RQ 19-9.** From the balance-sheet date through the report date — January 1 through February 20, 2026 — not the
fieldwork exit date. Dual dating keeps the original date for the audit as a whole while assigning a later date to one
specified matter; re-dating the whole report extends responsibility for all subsequent events.

**RQ 19-10.** One year after the date the statements are issued or are available to be issued — February 20, 2027 for
AtlasFlow. Mitigating plans are considered only if conditions and events, before considering plans, raise substantial
doubt, and must be probable of implementation and of being effective.

**RQ 19-11.** Because the letter conveys management's own description and evaluation of contingencies, on which the
lawyer is asked to comment; the auditor is the addressee of the response. A refusal to respond, or a response so
limited that it provides no assurance, is a scope limitation that ordinarily precludes an unqualified opinion.

**RQ 19-12.** As of the report date — February 20, 2026 — covering all periods reported on. A withheld representation is
a scope limitation that also calls the remaining representations into question; the auditor ordinarily disclaims or
withdraws and cannot qualify instead.

**RQ 19-13.** It asks whether the audited statements, read as a whole, are consistent with the auditor's understanding
of the entity and whether any relationship remains unexplained. A substantive analytical procedure instead tests a
specific assertion against a stated expectation and earns assurance.

**RQ 19-14.** Under AS 2710 the auditor reads other information and considers whether it is materially inconsistent
with the audited statements or contains a material misstatement of fact, but reports nothing about it. AU-C 720 requires
specified procedures and an "Other Information" report section.

**RQ 19-15.** Because ICFR effectiveness is reported at a point in time — December 31, 2025 — so a material weakness
existing on that date produces an adverse opinion even if remediation was underway. AS 1220 requires the reviewer's
concurring approval of issuance before release, dated February 19 here.

## Key Definitions

**Adverse opinion on internal control over financial reporting.** An opinion that ICFR was not effective as of the
assessment date because one or more material weaknesses existed (AS 2201), irrespective of remediation progress.

**Aggregation of deficiencies.** The evaluation of whether individually less severe deficiencies, considered in
combination, reach material weakness severity, ordinarily grouped by root cause (AS 2201).

**Clearly trivial threshold.** The amount below which identified misstatements need not be accumulated because they are
inconsequential individually and in the aggregate by any criterion of size, nature, or circumstance (AS 2810; AU-C 450).
AtlasFlow's is $72.

**Concurring approval of issuance.** The engagement quality reviewer's documented approval, required by AS 1220 for
issuer audits, without which the firm may not release the report.

**Documentation completion date.** The date, no later than 45 days after report release, by which a complete and final
set of audit documentation must be assembled (AS 1215). Afterward documentation may be added but not deleted, and each
addition must identify who, when, and why.

**Dual dating.** Reporting practice in which the report retains its original date except as to a specified matter, for
which a later date is given, limiting responsibility for later subsequent events to that matter.

**Engagement quality review.** An objective evaluation of the significant judgments and conclusions of the engagement
team, performed by a qualified reviewer not otherwise involved in the engagement (AS 1220; SQMS No. 2).

**Factual misstatement.** A misstatement about which there is no doubt, such as a recorded amount that does not agree to
the underlying document. Also called a known misstatement.

**Iron curtain method.** Quantification of the cumulative effect of uncorrected misstatements on the balance sheet at
the reporting date, ignoring prior-period turnaround (SAB 108). For AtlasFlow it produces a $590 overstatement of
stockholders' equity.

**Judgmental misstatement.** The difference between management's estimate and the auditor's point estimate where both
fall within a range of reasonable outcomes. It is recorded on the SAD even though management's amount is acceptable.

**Material weakness.** A deficiency, or combination of deficiencies, in ICFR such that there is a reasonable possibility
that a material misstatement of the annual or interim financial statements will not be prevented or detected on a timely
basis (AS 2201).

**Non-recognized subsequent event.** An event arising from conditions that came into existence after the balance-sheet
date; not recorded, but disclosed if non-disclosure would make the statements misleading (ASC 855).

**Other information.** Information in a document containing audited financial statements, other than those statements
and the auditor's report. Read for material inconsistency and misstatement of fact under AS 2710; AU-C 720 imposes
broader procedures and a reporting section.

**Projected misstatement.** The auditor's best estimate of misstatement in a population from which only a sample was
examined, obtained by extrapolation. U-1's $(136) component is one.

**Recognized subsequent event.** An event providing additional evidence about conditions that existed at the
balance-sheet date, reflected in the statements for the period then ended (ASC 855).

**Retrospective review of estimates.** A comparison of significant prior-period estimates with subsequent outcomes or
revisions, required by AS 2401 regardless of assessed risk and used under AS 2501 as evidence about current estimates.

**Rollover method.** Quantification of the effect of uncorrected misstatements on the current period's income statement,
including prior-period turnaround. For AtlasFlow it produces a $460 understatement of the FY2025 pre-tax loss.

**Significant deficiency.** A deficiency, or combination of deficiencies, less severe than a material weakness yet
important enough to merit attention by those charged with governance (AS 2201). It does not affect the ICFR opinion.

**Substantial doubt.** The condition, under ASC 205-40, in which it is probable that an entity will be unable to meet
its obligations as they become due within one year after the statements are issued, before considering mitigating plans.

**Summary of audit differences.** The schedule accumulating all misstatements above the clearly trivial threshold,
showing description, account, type, income-statement and balance-sheet effects, and whether the item was corrected. It
supports the AS 2810 evaluation and the AS 1301 communication.

**Unasserted claim.** A possible claim or assessment not yet asserted against the entity. It requires disclosure only if
assertion is probable and an unfavorable outcome reasonably possible; the ABA Statement of Policy governs when the
client's lawyer will advise on one.

**Written representation.** A written statement by management confirming matters or supporting other audit evidence,
required by AS 2805 and AU-C 580, dated as of the report date and covering all periods reported on.

## Chapter Summary

1. Completion is a dependency network, not a checklist: the report date cannot precede the subsequent-events review,
   the legal letter update, the audit committee communication, or the reviewer's concurring approval.
2. Every misstatement above the $72 clearly trivial threshold must be accumulated, and the below-threshold items must
   still be logged so their insignificance is demonstrated rather than assumed.
3. AtlasFlow's uncorrected misstatements aggregate $(460) under the rollover method and $590 under the iron curtain
   method, the $130 difference being the FY2024 commission accrual turning around; reporting one figure understates the
   exposure by 28%.
4. Neither aggregate is quantitatively material against $1,450, but the iron curtain figure consumes 62.8% of
   performance materiality, which forces the sufficiency conclusion to address aggregation risk explicitly.
5. The qualitative evaluation turns on a genuine argument — a one-cent change in reported loss per share against a
   company valued on ARR and non-GAAP operating margin — and both positions must be on the record.
6. Five of six uncorrected items and 89.6% of the gross dollars favor reported results, supporting incentive-driven
   tilt; the retrospective review, in which the FY2024 allowance proved 9.6% conservative, is why U-2 was treated as a
   judgmental difference rather than an unreasonable estimate.
7. Subsequent-event classification depends on when the condition arose, so the Meridian dispute is non-recognized as a
   2026 negotiating position and would have been recognized had it concerned 2025 delivery quality — in which case a
   price concession under ASC 606, not a bad debt, would have been the issue.
8. Going concern is a computation: liquidity of $150,600 against a $40,000 covenant leaves $110,600 of headroom and
   survives the downside case with $87,600, so substantial doubt does not exist and no plans analysis is required.
9. A refusal to give a required representation is a scope limitation that contaminates the remaining representations;
   the auditor disclaims or withdraws and cannot qualify around it.
10. The ICFR conclusion is as-of December 31, 2025 and aggregates by root cause — one ITGC material weakness and one
    revenue cut-off material weakness — while coexisting with an unqualified opinion on the statements; misstatements,
    deficiencies, and the going-concern conclusion are communicated to the audit committee before issuance, and
    documentation is assembled within 45 days of release and retained seven years.
